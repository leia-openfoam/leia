geometric Heaviside · analytic volume fractions · interface-normal velocity extension

Authors: T. Marić · J. Reitzel · M. Fricke · D. Bothe — MMA, TU Darmstadt

leia is a level-set library for OpenFOAM: an abstract, runtime-selectable model hierarchy (libleiaLevelSet) plus two thin solvers. The talk focuses on the code architecture: how the pieces plug together and the numerics behind the two "hard" parts — the phase indicator and the velocity extension.

The level set

Signed-distance field \(\psi(\mathbf{x},t)\):

\[ \Sigma = \{\mathbf{x} : \psi = 0\}, \quad \Omega^{-}=\{\psi<0\} \;(\text{phase A}) \]

Signed-distance property

\[ |\nabla\psi| = 1 \]

Distance to \(\Sigma\); sign encodes the phase.

Everything downstream assumes psi is a signed distance. When advection erodes that property, the phase indicator and surface tension degrade — which motivates redistancing and (crucially) velocity extension later in the talk.

Advection

Conservative transport of the level set:

\[ \frac{\partial \psi}{\partial t} + \nabla\!\cdot(\mathbf{u}\,\psi) = S \]

discretised with the finite-volume flux \(\phi = (\mathbf{u}_f\cdot\mathbf{S}_f)\):

fvm::ddt(psi) + fvm::div(velExt->phi(), psi) == source->fvmsdplsSource(psi, U)
The solver is deliberately thin: it wires up autoPtr-held models and runs a fixed pipeline each step. Note the equation advects with velExt->phi(), not the raw phi — that is the non-invasive coupling point for velocity extension.

Phase indicator / Heaviside

Volume fraction of phase A in cell \(c\):

\[ \alpha_c = \frac{1}{|\Omega_c|}\int_{\Omega_c} H(-\psi)\,\mathrm{d}V, \qquad H(s)=\begin{cases}1 & s>0\\ 0 & s\le 0\end{cases} \]

Problem: an algebraic Heaviside of the transported \(\psi\)

  • is only first order at the interface, and
  • relies on \(\psi\) staying a signed distance (\(|\nabla\psi|=1\)).

Both assumptions are violated by advection.

The naive indicator (heavisidePhaseIndicator / sharpJump) just thresholds psi. Cheap, but its accuracy is capped and it drifts as |grad psi| leaves 1. We want an indicator whose order follows psi and does not depend on the SDF property.

Idea: recover \(\alpha\) geometrically

Per cell, build a linear reconstruction of \(\psi\):

\[ \psi^{\ell}_c(\mathbf{x}) = \mathbf{n}_c\cdot\mathbf{x} + d_c \]

then compute \(\alpha_c\) as the fraction of the cell below that plane.

  • \(\alpha\) inherits the order of \(\psi\)
  • no \(|\nabla\psi|=1\) requirement
This is the shared design principle behind both geometric indicators: the reconstruction (plane) is computed the same way; only the "clip the cell" step differs (polygon intersection vs analytic tet fill).

LLS reconstruction

Shared code: levelSetPlaneReconstruction

Fit the plane over cell \(c\) and its neighbours \(N_c\) by least squares:

\[ \min_{\mathbf{n}_c,\,d_c}\; \sum_{k\in\{c\}\cup N_c}\big(\psi_k-(\mathbf{n}_c\cdot\mathbf{x}_k+d_c)\big)^2 \]

Normal equations — a \(4\times4\) system for \((n_x,n_y,n_z,d_c)\):

\[ \left[\begin{array}{cccc} \sum x_k^2 & \sum x_k y_k & \sum x_k z_k & \sum x_k \\ \sum x_k y_k & \sum y_k^2 & \sum y_k z_k & \sum y_k \\ \sum x_k z_k & \sum y_k z_k & \sum z_k^2 & \sum z_k \\ \sum x_k & \sum y_k & \sum z_k & N \end{array}\right] \!\begin{bmatrix} n_x\\ n_y\\ n_z\\ d_c \end{bmatrix} =\begin{bmatrix} \sum \psi_k x_k\\ \sum \psi_k y_k\\ \sum \psi_k z_k\\ \sum \psi_k \end{bmatrix} \]

2D fix: in an empty direction, row/col is decoupled and \(n_{\text{cmpt}}\!=\!0\) is pinned → keeps the \(4\times4\) system non-singular (mesh.geometricD()).

Solved with simpleMatrix (small direct solve). Coupled-boundary neighbours are added explicitly for parallel correctness. In 1-cell-thick 2D/1D meshes there is no variation along the empty direction, so that normal component is undetermined and the matrix is singular; pinning it to 0 and decoupling the row/column fixes it.

Geometric Heaviside

geometricPhaseIndicator — clip the cell against the plane

\[ \alpha_c = \frac{\big|\{\mathbf{x}\in\Omega_c : \psi^{\ell}_c(\mathbf{x})<0\}\big|}{|\Omega_c|} \]

The below-plane sub-volume is found by polygon/polyhedron intersection (divergence-theorem volume of the clipped cell).

intersectCell + hesseNormalPlane do the clipping; the negative volume is clamped and divided by the cell volume. Accurate, but the polygon intersection carries geometric tolerances and edge cases (plane grazing a face).

Analytic volume fraction — Detrixhe & Aslam (2016)

Decompose the cell into tets (cell centroid \(\mathbf{x}_c\), face centroid \(\mathbf{x}_f\), face-edge vertices):

Sort vertex distances \(\psi_1\!\le\!\psi_2\!\le\!\psi_3\!\le\!\psi_4\). Fraction of the tet in \(\Omega^{-}\):

\[ f = \begin{cases} 1 & \psi_4 \le 0\\[2pt] 1 - \dfrac{\psi_4^{3}}{(\psi_4-\psi_1)(\psi_4-\psi_2)(\psi_4-\psi_3)} & \psi_3\le 0<\psi_4\\[10pt] \dfrac{\psi_3\psi_4(\psi_1^2+\psi_1\psi_2+\psi_2^2) - \psi_1\psi_2(\psi_1+\psi_2)(\psi_3+\psi_4) + \psi_1^2\psi_2^2} {(\psi_3-\psi_1)(\psi_4-\psi_1)(\psi_3-\psi_2)(\psi_4-\psi_2)} & \psi_2\le 0<\psi_3\\[10pt] -\dfrac{\psi_1^{3}}{(\psi_2-\psi_1)(\psi_3-\psi_1)(\psi_4-\psi_1)} & \psi_1\le 0<\psi_2\\[6pt] 0 & \psi_1>0 \end{cases} \]
Sort the four vertex distances and apply the closed form; f is the fraction of the tet in Omega^-. The cell value is assembled on the next slide.

Analytic volume fraction — properties

Assemble the cell value as the tet-volume-weighted mean:

\[ \alpha_c = \frac{\sum_t f_t\,V_t}{\sum_t V_t} \]

Tolerance-free: the denominators are cross-interface distance gaps, bounded away from zero, so there is no clipping tolerance — it matches the polygon clipping to machine precision on the same plane, and stays robust in 2D.

The middle (two-in / two-out) case uses the corrected \(\Omega^{-}\) form — the paper's printed numerator gives the complement.

Denominators are products of cross-interface distance gaps, so they are bounded away from zero — no clipping tolerance. stabilise() only guards a vertex exactly on the interface. The middle-case numerator in the code is the corrected Omega^- form (verified vs divided-difference formula and direct integration).

geometric vs detrixheAslam — they coincide

Bulk shear vortex, velocity extension none. Both integrate the same LLS plane, so they agree to round-off: the gradient error is identical (ψ-advection is indicator-independent), shape/volume differ by \(\lesssim\!10^{-10}\). detrixheAslam is the tolerance-free, 2D-robust choice.

Snakemake study config/phaseIndicatorConvergence.yaml sweeps PHASE_INDICATOR {geometric, detrixheAslam} x N_CELLS x T with velocity extension = none. Max relative indicator difference: gradient 0, shape 5e-11, volume 3e-11. Figures live in the study dir (export_slides:false) — this one copied in for the deck.

Velocity extension — why

Only motion along the interface normal moves \(\Sigma\). The cell-centred \(\mathbf{U}\) varies along \(\mathbf{n}\), so advecting \(\psi\) with it changes the spacing of the level sets and breaks \(|\nabla\psi|=1\). The extension velocity \(\mathbf{U}_{\text{ext}}\) removes that variation, \((\mathbf{n}\cdot\nabla)\,\mathbf{U}_{\text{ext}}=0\), so each normal ray is advected rigidly.

Only the normal velocity component moves the interface; the tangential part just shears the level-set field and ruins the SDF property. Raw U varies along n, so the iso-psi contours change spacing (|grad psi| != 1); U_ext is constant along each normal, so the contours shift rigidly and |grad psi| = 1 is preserved.

Interface velocity

\(\mathbf{x}_\Sigma\) is the closest point on \(\Sigma\) to the cell centre — the orthogonal projection:

\[ \mathbf{x}_\Sigma=\operatorname*{arg\,min}_{\mathbf{y}\in\Sigma}\|\mathbf{x}_c-\mathbf{y}\| \]

Approximate it by steepest descent on \(\psi\) (\(\mathbf{n}=\nabla\psi/|\nabla\psi|\)):

\[ \mathbf{x} \leftarrow \mathbf{x} - \psi(\mathbf{x})\,\mathbf{n} \]

Exact in one step only for a true signed distance; otherwise the iterate \(\tilde{\mathbf{x}}\) lands near \(\mathbf{x}_\Sigma\) with an approximation error.

Then \(\mathbf{U}_\Sigma=\operatorname{interp}(\mathbf{U},\tilde{\mathbf{x}})\) — IDW cell-point interpolation.

interfaceExtension: seed cells = the single layer with \(0<\alpha<1\).

x_Sigma is the orthogonal projection (closest point) onto Sigma. Steepest descent on psi (n = grad psi / |grad psi|) approximates it: one step is exact only for a true SDF (|grad psi| = 1); otherwise the landing point x-tilde is displaced along Sigma -- an approximation error. interpolationCellPoint (inverse-distance from cell points) then samples U at x-tilde. Seed layer = interface-cut cells (fractional alpha).

Extension PDE — two models

Goal: \((\mathbf{n}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{U}_{\text{ext}}=0\) off \(\Sigma\), with \(\mathbf{U}_{\text{ext}}=\mathbf{U}_\Sigma\) at the interface layer (Dirichlet).

Anisotropic harmonic

\[ \nabla\!\cdot\!\big(\mathbf{D}\,\nabla\mathbf{U}_{\text{ext}}\big)=0 \] \[ \mathbf{D}=\mathbf{n}\otimes\mathbf{n}+\varepsilon\,\mathbf{I} \]

whole domain, zeroGradient outer BC; PCG/DIC.

Pseudo-time (Adalsteinsson–Sethian)

\[ \frac{\partial \mathbf{U}_{\text{ext}}}{\partial\tau} + \operatorname{sign}(\psi)\,(\mathbf{n}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{U}_{\text{ext}}=0 \]

implicit backward Euler, upwind; \(n_{\text{iter}}\) marches.

Each model gets its own slides next: definition → discretization → verbatim solver code. The interface layer is imposed as Dirichlet directly in the assembled matrix via fvMatrix::setValues — no submesh.

Both are whole-domain solves; the seed cells are constrained by setValues so no submesh/region machinery is needed. Anisotropic diffuses only along n (plus eps I for well-posedness); pseudo-time propagates outward with w = sign(psi) n.

Model: none — definition (baseline)

No extension is built. The advecting velocity is the prescribed field itself, so the level set is advected with the exact, (near-)divergence-free flux \(\phi\):

\[ \mathbf{U}_{\text{ext}} = \mathbf{U}, \qquad \phi_{\text{ext}} = \phi \]

The reference against which the two genuine extension models are measured — and, as the results show, the most accurate choice on a prescribed divergence-free field.

none is the abstract base itself (addToRunTimeSelectionTable as "none").

none — discretization (UFVM)

No linear system is assembled — correct() re-syncs the registry fields each step (after the oscillation rescaling of \(\mathbf{U}\), \(\phi\)):

\[ \texttt{Uext == U;}\qquad \texttt{phiExt == phi;} \]

The \(\psi\)-equation advects with fvm::div(velExt->phi(), psi) = the exact prescribed div(phi, psi), which is discretely divergence-free — the property that keeps it stable and conservative.

Source — base class registered as the none type: src/leiaLevelSet/velocityExtension/velocityExtension.C → velocityExtension::correct()

none — solver code (\(\psi\) advection)

// non-invasive: U/phi are never mutated; advect psi with velExt->phi().
// For "none", correct() simply re-syncs:  Uext == U;  phiExt == phi;
velExt->correct();

// Defect-correction loop: each pass re-assembles with the LATEST psi so the
// deferred (linearUpwind - upwind) correction converges (~2-3 passes -> 2nd
// order). Safe: fvm::ddt reuses psi.oldTime(), fixed within the step.
const label nDefCorr =
    runTime.controlDict().getOrDefault<label>("nDefCorr", 2);

for (label corr = 0; corr < nDefCorr; ++corr)
{
    fvScalarMatrix psiEqn
    (
        fvm::ddt(psi)
      + fvm::div(velExt->phi(), psi)     // Gauss linearUpwind grad(psi)
     ==
        source->fvmsdplsSource(psi, U)
    );

    psiEqn.solve();
}

applications/solvers/leiaLevelSetFoam/leiaLevelSetFoam.C. grad(psi) = cellLimited leastSquares 1; div(phi,psi) = Gauss linearUpwind grad(psi).

Model: anisotropicDiffusion — definition

Extend the interface velocity \(\mathbf{U}_\Sigma\) off \(\Sigma\) by diffusion that acts only along the normal (normal-aligned harmonic extension):

\[ \nabla\!\cdot\!\big(\mathbf{D}\,\nabla\mathbf{U}_{\text{ext}}\big)=0, \qquad \mathbf{D}=\mathbf{n}\otimes\mathbf{n}+\varepsilon\,\mathbf{I} \]

\(\mathbf{n}\otimes\mathbf{n}\) diffuses along \(\mathbf{n}\) (making \(\mathbf{U}_{\text{ext}}\) normal-constant); \(\varepsilon\mathbf{I}\) regularizes the tangential/empty directions. Dirichlet \(\mathbf{U}_{\text{ext}}=\mathbf{U}_\Sigma\) at the interface seed layer.

D = n(x)n + eps I. Diffusion along n makes Uext constant on each normal ray (the extension property); eps I regularizes the tangential direction for well-posedness. Seed layer pinned to U_Sigma as Dirichlet.

anisotropicDiffusion — discretization (UFVM)

  • tensor \(\mathbf{D}=\) sqr(nHat) + eps*I (volSymmTensorField), with \(\mathbf{n}=\) fvc::grad(psi)/|grad psi|
  • implicit anisotropic Laplacian: fvm::laplacian(D, Uext)
  • interface Dirichlet in the assembled matrix: fvMatrix::setValues(seedCells, UΣ) — no submesh
  • symmetric matrix → PCG/DIC, one solve; zeroGradient outer BC
\[ \sum_{f}\, \mathbf{D}_f\,\big(\nabla\mathbf{U}_{\text{ext}}\big)_f\!\cdot\!\mathbf{S}_f = 0, \qquad \mathbf{U}_{\text{ext}}|_{\text{seed}}=\mathbf{U}_\Sigma \]

Source: src/leiaLevelSet/velocityExtension/anisotropicDiffusion.C → anisotropicDiffusion::extend(); shared seed/band/flux backbone in interfaceExtension.C.

anisotropicDiffusion::extend() — code

void Foam::anisotropicDiffusion::extend()
{
    // D = n (x) n + eps I  (diffuse along the normal; eps regularizes)
    volSymmTensorField D
    (
        IOobject("velExtD", /* ... */),
        sqr(nHat_) + dimensionedSymmTensor("eps", dimless, epsilon_*symmTensor::I)
    );

    fvVectorMatrix UextEqn(fvm::laplacian(D, Uext_));

    // Dirichlet only at the single interface layer, directly in the matrix:
    forAll(seedCells_, i)
    {
        fixedCells.append(seedCells_[i]);
        fixedVals.append(Uext_[seedCells_[i]]);   // = U_Sigma
    }
    UextEqn.setValues(fixedCells, fixedVals);

    // symmetric matrix -> PCG/DIC, one solve; zeroGradient outer BC.
    UextEqn.solve(solverControls);   // solver=PCG, preconditioner=DIC
}

Whole-domain harmonic solve; no submesh — the seed layer is the only constraint.

Model: pseudoTime — definition (Adalsteinsson–Sethian)

March \(\mathbf{U}_{\text{ext}}\) to the steady state of a pseudo-time transport that carries \(\mathbf{U}_\Sigma\) outward along the normals on both sides of \(\Sigma\):

\[ \frac{\partial \mathbf{U}_{\text{ext}}}{\partial\tau} + \operatorname{sign}(\psi)\,(\mathbf{n}\cdot\nabla)\,\mathbf{U}_{\text{ext}}=0 \]

Dirichlet \(\mathbf{U}_{\text{ext}}=\mathbf{U}_\Sigma\) at the interface seed layer; \(n_{\text{iter}}\) pseudo-time steps set the reach. The direction \(\mathbf{w}=\operatorname{sign}(\psi)\,\mathbf{n}\) flips across \(\Sigma\) and is not divergence-free — which forces the non-conservative discretization.

Adalsteinsson-Sethian: transport U_Sigma outward along w = sign(psi) n so it becomes constant along each normal. n_iter pseudo-time steps set the reach (tau1, tau2). w flips sign across Sigma and is not solenoidal -> non-conservative discretization required.

pseudoTime — discretization (UFVM, implicit backward Euler)

  • propagation direction \(\mathbf{w}=\operatorname{sign}(\psi)\,\mathbf{n}\); flux phiW = fvc::interpolate(w) & Sf
  • non-conservative advection assembled as \((\mathbf{w}\!\cdot\!\nabla)\mathbf{U}_{\text{ext}} = \nabla\!\cdot\!(\phi_W\mathbf{U}_{\text{ext}}) - \mathbf{U}_{\text{ext}}\,(\nabla\!\cdot\!\phi_W)\) — needed because \(\mathbf{w}\) is not divergence-free
  • \(1/\Delta\tau_{\text{cell}}=\) rDtau = 1/(deltaTau*V^(1/3)); upwind convection → stable, positive diagonal
  • Dirichlet setValues(seedCells, UΣ); PBiCGStab/DILU, one solve per iteration
\[ \tfrac{1}{\Delta\tau}\mathbf{U}_{\text{ext}}^{k+1} + \big(\mathbf{w}\!\cdot\!\nabla\big)^{\text{up}}\mathbf{U}_{\text{ext}}^{k+1} = \tfrac{1}{\Delta\tau}\mathbf{U}_{\text{ext}}^{k} \]

Source: src/leiaLevelSet/velocityExtension/pseudoTime.C → pseudoTime::extend(); shared seed/band/flux backbone in interfaceExtension.C.

pseudoTime::extend() — code (non-conservative)

void Foam::pseudoTime::extend()
{
    // w = sign(psi) n  (outward, NOT divergence-free)
    forAll(w, c) w[c] = ((psi_[c] < 0) ? -1.0 : 1.0)*nHat_[c];
    const surfaceScalarField phiW("velExtPhiW", fvc::interpolate(w) & mesh_.Sf());
    forAll(rDtau, c) rDtau[c] = 1.0/(deltaTau_*Foam::pow(mesh_.V()[c], 1.0/3.0));

    // NON-CONSERVATIVE: (w.grad)Uext = div(phiW,Uext) - Uext*(div phiW)
    const volScalarField divW("velExtDivW", fvc::div(phiW));
    for (label it = 0; it < nIterations_; ++it)
    {
        fvVectorMatrix UextEqn
        (
            fvm::Sp(rDtau, Uext_)
          + fv::gaussConvectionScheme<vector>
            (mesh_, phiW, new upwind<vector>(mesh_, phiW)).fvmDiv(phiW, Uext_)
          - fvm::Sp(divW, Uext_)
         == rDtau*Uext_
        );
        UextEqn.setValues(fixedCells, fixedVals);  // seed Dirichlet
        UextEqn.solve(solverControls);             // PBiCGStab/DILU
    }
}

The - fvm::Sp(divW, Uext_) term converts the conservative flux divergence into the advective \((\mathbf{w}\!\cdot\!\nabla)\) derivative an extension needs.

Non-invasive coupling

  • Reads base \(\mathbf{U}\) and flux \(\phi\) (never mutated).
  • Writes a separate field \(\mathbf{U}_{\text{ext}}\) (AUTO_WRITE) and advection flux \(\phi_{\text{ext}}\).
  • Level-set equation advects with \(\phi_{\text{ext}}\).
  • Default model none = identity (\(\mathbf{U}_{\text{ext}}\!=\!\mathbf{U}\)).
Clean separation of concerns: a future Navier-Stokes solve can own U/phi; velocity extension is an opt-in correction layer, so nothing breaks when it is "none". Only band faces of phi_ext are overwritten with the Uext-derived flux.

Verification metrics

Signed-distance error \(e = \big|\,|\nabla\psi| - 1\,\big|\)  (gradPsiError)

Volume-weighted norms  (gradPsiErrorCSV):

\[ L_1 = \frac{\sum_c e_c V_c}{\sum_c V_c}, \qquad L_2 = \sqrt{\frac{\sum_c e_c^2 V_c}{\sum_c V_c}} \]

reported globally and over the narrow band, per time step to CSV.

Plus, from the solver: geometric shape error and volume-conservation error.

gradPsiError stores the field e; gradPsiErrorCSV reduces it to L1/L2 (global + narrow-band via the NarrowBand mask) and appends a row each step. Shape/volume errors come from advectionErrors.H / errorCalculation.H in the solver.

Conclusions — why the extensions first failed

none advects ψ with the prescribed flux \(\phi\), which is discretely ~divergence-free ⇒ stable, conservative, accurate. Both extensions rebuild \(\phi_{\text{ext}}=\operatorname{interp}(\mathbf{U}_{\text{ext}})\!\cdot\!\mathbf{S}_f\), which breaks that. Written out, ψ obeys

\[ \frac{\partial\psi}{\partial t} = -\,\phi_{\text{ext}}\!\cdot\!\nabla\psi \;-\; \psi\,\underbrace{(\nabla\!\cdot\!\phi_{\text{ext}})}_{\ne\,0\ \Rightarrow\ \text{source}} \]
  • A. Non-solenoidal flux — \(\nabla\!\cdot\!\phi_{\text{ext}}\neq0\) acts as a compression source ⇒ \(|\nabla\psi|\) and volume grow (interfaceExtension::updateFlux).
  • B. Wrong time step — Δt from CFL of the base \(\phi\), while ψ is advected with \(\phi_{\text{ext}}\) (leiaLevelSetFoam.C).
  • C. Feedback — no redistancing ⇒ ψ (hence \(\mathbf{n}=\nabla\psi/|\nabla\psi|\) and the foot point) degrades ⇒ worse \(\mathbf{U}_{\text{ext}}\) ⇒ worse ψ.

Two discretization issues compounded it: central Gauss linear advection of \(\psi\) (dispersive wiggles), and pseudoTime discretizing the non-solenoidal \(\mathbf{w}=\operatorname{sign}(\psi)\mathbf{n}\) in conservative form (spurious \(\mathbf{U}_{\text{ext}}\) compression) → the \(\sim\!10^{108}\) blow-up. aniso stayed bounded (max-principle harmonic solve) but degraded with \(T\).

Conclusions — the fixes

  • 1. Non-conservative pseudoTime (the big one): assemble \((\mathbf{w}\!\cdot\!\nabla)\mathbf{U}_{\text{ext}} =\nabla\!\cdot\!(\phi_W\mathbf{U}_{\text{ext}})-\mathbf{U}_{\text{ext}}(\nabla\!\cdot\!\phi_W)\) — - fvm::Sp(fvc::div(phiW), Uext_) in pseudoTime::extend(). Kills the \(10^{108}\) blow-up.
  • 2. Bounded 2nd-order advection: Gauss linearUpwind grad(psi) with grad(psi) = cellLimited leastSquares 1, inside a defect-correction loop (nDefCorr) in leiaLevelSetFoam.C — deferred correction → ~2nd order, no central wiggles.
  • 3. Divergence-free advecting flux (removes A). Helmholtz-project: \(\nabla^2 p=\nabla\!\cdot\!\phi_{\text{ext}}\Rightarrow \nabla\!\cdot\!\phi_{\text{ext}}\approx0\). projectFlux true → correctFlux() (+ setReference for the pure-Neumann Poisson — was NaN/SIGFPE).
  • 4. CFL from the actual flux (removes B): maxDeltaT(velExt->phi(), …); plus a band clip \(|\mathbf{U}_{\text{ext}}|\le\)maxScale\(\cdot\max|\mathbf{U}|\) (clipExtended()).
  • Future (mitigates C): PDE redistancing (parallel temp-mesh) to refresh \(\mathbf{n}\)/foot-points under strong stretch.

OpenFOAM's declareRunTimeSelectionTable / addToRunTimeSelectionTable macros. Models look up their fields via mesh.lookupObject, with the field names taken from the sub-dict (getOrDefault), so nothing is hard-wired.

Same pattern, everywhere

RTS + object registry = pluggable, KISS.

Adding a new model = one class + addToRunTimeSelectionTable + a dict entry. No solver edits. The same idiom recurs for surfaceTensionForce and schemes.

Architecture map

One library of runtime-selectable models feeds two thin solvers; a Snakemake suite verifies them.

Take-away: the design is a small library of interchangeable numerical models (RTS + registry) that two solvers compose, with a reproducible verification harness on top. New physics/schemes plug in without touching the solvers.

Add your own model — without touching existing code

The velocity-extension hierarchy is runtime-selected (open/closed): a new model is a new class, no edits to existing ones.

  1. Derive from interfaceExtension (reuse the seed / band / flux backbone) — or from velocityExtension for full control.
  2. TypeName("myModel"); + a (const fvMesh&) constructor.
  3. addToRunTimeSelectionTable(velocityExtension, myModel, Mesh);
  4. Implement the one pure-virtual extend() (fill Uext_ in the band from the pinned seed layer).
  5. Add the .C to src/leiaLevelSet/Make/files; wmake.
  6. Select at runtime: levelSet{ velocityExtension{ type myModel; } }.

Templates: anisotropicDiffusion.C (one linear solve) and pseudoTime.C (iterative march).
