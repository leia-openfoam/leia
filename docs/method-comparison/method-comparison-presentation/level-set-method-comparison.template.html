Part 1 of 8

The contenders

  • SL:quadraticWeightedLeastSquares (+ uncached variant) — matrix-free characteristic transport, weighted quadratic foot reconstruction (semi-lagrangian-level-set deck).
  • euler — implicit FV transport, advective form, defect-corrected linearUpwind (nDefCorr).
  • euler+SDPLS:R / beta — + source-term gradient control, zero interface displacement by construction (sdpls deck).
  • euler+VE:closestPoint — + interface-normal-constant velocity extension (velocity-extension deck).
  • Not competing: interface-rewriting redistancing — retired with measurements (grl negative-results deck): the zero level set and the band values are the same degrees of freedom; every rewrite displaces the interface.

Same case, same error norms, same reporting cadence (write times only) in every line — the wall clock is comparable by construction.

Part 2 of 8

Convergence, T = 2 (resolved regime) — overview

Five panels: shape / volume / band-gradient at t = T; volume + band-gradient at t = T/2 (maximal stretching — the volume fraction of that shape is unknown, so no shape reference there). The next five slides take each panel one at a time.

T = 2 · shape error \(E_{geom}(\alpha)\) at t = T

The headline accuracy: SL and plain Euler converge at ~2nd order and coincide; the SDPLS sources and velocity extension trail by a constant factor. Everything is well-resolved here, so all lines converge cleanly.

T = 2 · volume conservation \(E_{vol}\) at t = T

Reversed-state volume error. SL and Euler are best; the source terms add a small mass-exchange penalty at the end state (the price for the T/2 volume advantage two slides on).

T = 2 · signed-distance quality: band \(L_2(||\nabla\psi|-1|)\) at t = T

The one panel where the methods SEPARATE by design: SL keeps \(|\nabla\psi|\approx1\) (the reconstruction is signed-distance-aware); the SDPLS sources force a large band gradient deviation (they trade signed distance for the conservation shown next); plain Euler sits between.

T = 2 · volume conservation \(E_{vol}\) at t = T/2 (maximal stretch)

The SDPLS home turf: at the most-deformed instant the R/beta sources give the best volume conservation of any method — the zero-interface-displacement property paying off exactly where it should. (At 512² SL closes this gap.)

T = 2 · signed-distance quality at t = T/2

Same separation as the t = T gradient panel but under maximal strain: SL stays closest to a signed distance; the sources deviate most (by construction, they control the gradient's evolution, not its band value).

Convergence, T = 8 (filament regime) — overview

At N ≤ 128 the wound filament is below the mesh resolution for every method; 256²/512² restore the T = 2 ranking (SL first). Per-panel T=8 figures are in data/figures/benchVortex_T8_*.png.

Part 6 of 8

Faults & improvements — per method

every fault code-documented or benchmark-measured; every improvement concrete. Several are already implemented (this deck's SL rows).

Semi-Lagrangian quadratic (winner)

Faults

  • No maximum principle: quadratic fits overshoot; Barth–Jespersen collapses order 3.0→0.1, so it ships with only a ±10× value cap.
  • Cached model memory-bound (~8 GB @128³ → single precision; pseudo-inverse mat-vec 28% of solver).
  • Uncached squares the condition number → degenerate cell falls back to a constant.
  • Value fit: no signed-distance property; volume small but not conserved.

Improvements

  • Venkatakrishnan limiter (implemented): smooth, order- preserving bound — where BJ collapsed the order.
  • Householder-QR fit (implemented): no condition-number squaring, fewer degenerate fallbacks.
  • Flux-form SL (implemented, RTS scheme fluxForm): conserves ∫ψdV to machine precision.
  • CFC stencil on polyhedra (cheaper, better conditioned).

The three implemented items are measured on the next "SL improvements" slide.

Eulerian defect-corrected PDE (2nd)

Faults

  • linearUpwind numerical diffusion erodes \(|\nabla\psi|\) (band grad 1.5e-4 @T2 → 7.7e-2 @T8).
  • 2nd order only via the nDefCorr loop = 2–3 assembles/step (2× SL cost).
  • 20× behind SL shape error @T8/512²; non-conservative advective form.
  • Cell-limited LSQ gradient can mislimit on coarse meshes.

Improvements

  • Higher-order bounded div (WENO / limited-cubic) to cut diffusion without the defect-correction cost.
  • SDPLS source to hold \(|\nabla\psi|\) (with the resolution caveat).
  • Refinement is the cure for the T=8 filament death (method-independent).

SDPLS source (R / β)

Faults

  • Trades signed distance for gradient control (band \(L_2\) → 0.5–0.96 vs SL 8.4e-5).
  • R over-flattens near the resolution limit (vol 0.488 vs noSource 0.238 @T8/N128).
  • R controls steepening, not flattening (band min→0 unchanged).
  • β+explicit diverges every CFL; strictNegative can lose the M-matrix.

Improvements

  • Resolution-aware (\(\kappa h\)) mollification/gating of R (open work).
  • Strain-limited source strength; β always implicit.
  • Position at its niche: under-resolved production + two-phase zero-displacement coupling — not a pure-advection default.

Velocity extension (closestPoint + family) (dominated)

Faults

  • Worst accuracy at 12–27× the cost (16,100 s @T8/512²) — the per-step extension solve is the overhead.
  • closestPoint geometric failures: colliding rays diverge; needs \(\psi\) near-SDF (circular redistancing dependency); cross-processor feet.
  • Family: anisotropic ε-floor + one-sided ~2.7; pseudoTime truncated reach/blow-up; steadyUpwindLinear diverges; meshWave O(h).

Improvements

  • Use extension ONLY where genuinely needed (moving contact lines, prescribed non-solenoidal U), not pure advection.
  • Cheaper geometric extension (fast marching) if kept.
  • Narrower band / only-when-needed extension to cut cost.

Retired geometric redistancing & the shared phase indicator

Redistancing (retired)

  • Zero set and band values are the same DOF → any band rewrite displaces the interface (O(h²κ)/event, one-signed, compounds).
  • planeFootWave scalloping; anchoredEikonal 1st-order fill (2nd-order cure diverges); PDE reinit central-Hamiltonian oscillation.
  • Frozen-band bulk-only pseudo-time Eikonal IMPLEMENTED + gated (transported band + 2 guard layers as Dirichlet anchors → static gate Egeom = Evol = 0 EXACTLY per event; Godunov fill repairs bulk 0.56 → 0.009 where central diverges 0.56 → 59.7).
  • ...yet MEASURED INJURIOUS in transport (Eulerian line, T=8): Evol 0.017→1.77 @256, 0.24→2.53 @128 — the fill rebuilds distance to linearUpwind's spurious BULK sign-changes, which the indicator reads as spurious phase. Redistancing can't fix what transport corrupted; the static gate misses it. SDPLS (never touches sign structure) stays the only viable mechanism.

Phase indicator (LLS plane)

  • The plane is a chord → O(h²κ) one-signed bias, caps α at 2nd order (both indicators identical).
  • Thin/coplanar clouds → fall back to sign(ψ); curvature staircase → parasitic currents.
  • Fix: curvature-corrected plane distance (chord-bias subtraction, already in the redistancer anchors); PLIC/ELVIRA; plicRDF/height-function curvature.

SL improvements — measured

  • Venkatakrishnan limiter (K=5) — costs more than it protects here. Shape-error order 3.0 → ~0.9 (BETTER than Barth–Jespersen's 0.1 stall, but NOT order-preserving in the advection setting); \(L_\infty(e_\psi)\) worse at every N except 32; and α was never out of bounds anyway (Ebound = 0 — the geometric indicator bounds α by construction). Wins: N=32 volume error 40× better (2.97e-2 → 7.4e-4), band-gradient control 0.98 → 0.74. Verdict: keep OFF on hex; use as the softer alternative to the hard clamp on polyhedra.
  • Householder-QR fit — free robustness. Bit-parity with the Cholesky normal equations on hex AND on an α=0.1-perturbed mesh (errors identical to the last digit; zero degenerate fallbacks in either). The ill-conditioning it guards against was not reachable in these 2D studies — it remains the safeguard for polyhedral/CFC stencils.
  • Flux-form (conservative) SL — competitive at resolved conditions. A distinct conservative scheme (own RTS): at T=2 it matches point-value's convergence order with errors within ~2× AND conserves \(\int\psi\,dV\) to 2.2e-14 (at 256² its shape error edges point-value). Its one cost is extra numerical diffusion from the half-step swept flux, which only bites on the T=8 sub-cell filament (volume error 3–13× worse there). Verdict: an attractive conservative alternative when the interface is resolved; point-value still wins the extreme long-horizon.

Pitfall found on the way: leiaPerturbMesh was a NO-OP on 2D meshes (every point lies on the empty patches, and the boundary reset reverted the whole perturbation) — fixed (pin physical patches only, zero the empty-direction component, deterministic seed). The perturbed study is the first to actually run on a perturbed mesh. Reproduce: benchVortexSLimproved / ...Perturbed / ...flux.

T = 8 (the hardest case for flux-form). Left: the Venkatakrishnan limiter softens the shape-error slope (order ~0.9, above BJ's 0.1 stall) but trails the unlimited baseline; flux-form trails on shape here. Where the flux-form volume loss comes from

  • Mechanism = upwind finite-volume dissipation, not the invariant mismatch. The flux evaluates the UPWIND cell's reconstruction at the face — a dissipative (irreversible) operation. When the reversed vortex compresses the interface toward sub-cell scale (band \(|\nabla\psi|\) climbs to ~5), that dissipation smears the steep ψ.
  • Left: flux volume drops MONOTONICALLY (−17% by t=6.5) because dissipation is irreversible; point-value only OSCILLATES (±1%) and RECOVERS on flow reversal (its errors are dispersive = reversible).
  • Right: flux ends with band \(|\nabla\psi|\) = 0.72 < 1 (flattened interface) + fewer interface cells; point-value stays steeper (0.96).
  • \(\int\psi\,dV\) is machine-conserved throughout — but α = H(−ψ) is nonlinear, so a smeared ψ shifts the zero contour inward. At T = 2 the interface never compresses far, dissipation is negligible, flux = point-value.

Frozen-band redistancing — measured injurious

Eulerian line, T = 8. The redistancer engineered for zero interface displacement (static gate: Egeom = Evol = 0) still inflates the volume error 10–100× in transport — it rebuilds distance to linearUpwind's spurious BULK sign-changes, which the indicator reads as phase. Redistancing can't fix what transport corrupted. The GRL line is closed; SDPLS stays the only viable ψ-maintenance.

Surface tension now has one enforceable face-flux contract

model  →  \(G_{\sigma,f}=\int_{S_f}\mathbf f_\sigma\!\cdot\mathbf n_f\,dS\) [N/m]  →  pressure flux  →  fvc::reconstruct

What changed

  • Every model integrates over \(|S_f|\) before returning.
  • The base API rejects wrong dimensions or unoriented fluxes.
  • The direct cell-vector source has been removed.

What PIMPLE sees

\(\phi_g=r_{AU,f}\,[G_{\sigma,f}-g h_f\,\mathrm{snGrad}(\rho)|S_f|]\)

\(\mathbf U=\mathbf H/A+r_{AU}\,\mathrm{reconstruct}[(\phi_g-\phi_p)/r_{AU,f}]\)

The same evaluated \(G_{\sigma,f}\) is reused in the momentum predictor and pressure corrector. A production model can no longer enter through a discretely inequivalent cell source.

Face-centered curvature: the offset is the defect, the stabilized foot point removes it

  • What the CSF force actually applies — \(\kappa_f\) on the \(|\mathrm{snGrad}\,\alpha|>0\) faces — converges at only \(h^{1.1}\) for EVERY contour-referenced delivery (cell quadratic, FVM \(\psi\)-curvature, Kang); the Newton-foot and closest-point extensions do not help, because a quadratic fit's constant Hessian pins its curvature to the stencil centre.
  • Re-referencing the same \(\kappa_f\) to the interface through the stabilized foot point (parallel-curve inverse \(\kappa=\kappa_d/(1-d_f\kappa_d)\), \(d_f\) from footPointDistance) restores clean 2nd order: \(L_2 = 0.10\) vs \(11.4\;\mathrm{m}^{-1}\) at \(N{=}512\) — 100×. Order matters: the per-side variant (correct each cell, then combine) reaches only \(h^{1.4}\) — the inverse must act on the face-combined value.
  • Foot-point height function reaches \(h^{1.75}\); the CSF-support mean converges at \(h^{2}\) (bias is 2nd order — variation is the defect, consistent with the replay); connected-interface stalls at \(h^{0.5}\) with its default \(w{=}3,\lambda{=}1\) chain fit; \(\alpha\)-based FVM curvature diverges.

The old representation fitted many interfaces, not one interface

Independent-cell construction

  • signChange is two cells thick; it is not the set of cut cells.
  • Each cell reconstructed its own LSQ zero plane and its own foot.
  • CPC clouds and parabola fits differed from one cell to the next.
  • Normal delivery copied inconsistent curvature values into the same CSF support.

Observed consequence

The clean circle could have a good local fit in every cell while still having no single ordering, no shared nodes and no tangential smoothness constraint.

Transport-profile noise changed the local feet independently; the resulting \(\kappa_f\) variation was not a discrete pressure gradient.

Design change: define the zero surface globally first; only then fit and deliver curvature.

Curvature is now one degree of freedom per interface element

For element midpoint \(x_i\), gather the ordered chain stencil \(i-w,\ldots,i,\ldots,i+w\). Align and length-average the LSQ normals to obtain \(\bar n_i\), then set \(t_i=e_z\times\bar n_i\).

Tangential coordinates

\(\tau_j=t_i\cdot(x_j-x_i)/h_i,\quad \zeta_j=\bar n_i\cdot(x_j-x_i)/h_i\)

Fit \(\zeta(\tau)=\tfrac12 A_i\tau^2+B_i\tau+C_i\) by least squares.

Element curvature

\[\kappa_i^{raw}=-\frac{A_i/h_i}{(1+B_i^2)^{3/2}}\]

Sign convention: \(\nabla\psi\) points outward, so a circular drop has \(+1/R\).

Unlike the foot-point model, every stencil follows the same ordered chain. Half-widths \(w=3,4,5\) were replayed.

Tangential regularization removes grid modes without normal mixing

Chain Laplacian: \( (L_s\kappa)_i=2\kappa_i-\kappa_{i-1}-\kappa_{i+1} \)

Helmholtz candidate

\[(I+\lambda L_s)\kappa=\kappa^{raw}\]

Jacobi iteration; tested \((w,\lambda)=(3,1),(4,4),(5,16)\).

Biharmonic candidate

\[\kappa\leftarrow\kappa-\theta L_s^2\kappa,\qquad\theta\le1/16\]

Higher selectivity for grid modes; tested 20 and 50 passes with \(w=3,\theta=0.05\).

Closed-curve constraint: after filtering, add one constant so \(\sum_i\kappa_i\,\ell_i=\pm2\pi\). This fixes the global curvature null mode without deleting spatially varying physical modes.

The replay now rejects falsely quiet candidates

Failure caught during development

The first implementation reported \(|U-U_{trans}|\approx2\times10^{-17}\) m/s—apparently better than the exact-curvature control.

It was false: all reconstructed elements had been rejected, kappaInterfaceFace was zero, and no capillary force was applied.

Mandatory validity audit

  • non-empty reconstruction;
  • exactly one component for this one-drop case;
  • zero open endpoints;
  • successful fits = interface elements;
  • zero fallbacks and solver status 0.

Decision metric remains only maximum cell-centred \(|U-U_{trans}|\). Topology is a validity condition, not a substitute force-error norm.

The quietest circle candidate may erase the oscillation we need

On a periodic chain with \(N_\Gamma\) elements, Helmholtz filtering transfers Fourier mode \(m\) by \[ H_m=\frac{1}{1+\lambda\mu_m},\qquad \mu_m=4\sin^2\!\left(\frac{\pi m}{N_\Gamma}\right). \]

Replay benefit

For \(N_\Gamma=52,w=5,\lambda=16\), the transported circle has element curvature only about \([978,1034]\) m−1 at \(t\approx0.05\).

Physical cost

For the oscillating-drop mode \(m=2\), the filter alone gives \(H_2\approx0.52\). The wide local fit attenuates it further.

Therefore: replay quietness is necessary but not sufficient. The selected candidate is not yet an oscillating-droplet model.

Integral traction models inherit the geometry, not automatic balance

What they can reuse

  • shared zero-crossing nodes;

What remains unsolved

  • integralConormal is curvature-free; forcing it through \(\kappa_f\nabla\alpha\) changes the model.
  • A globally conservative cell traction is not necessarily integrable as a scalar pressure jump on OpenFOAM faces.

Part 8 of 8

Verdict (final, 32²–512²)

  • Given sufficient resolution, choose semi-Lagrangian quadratic transport. At 512²/T=8 it is 20× more accurate than the best Eulerian at HALF the wall clock (and 27× cheaper than velocity extension); its T = 8 accuracy equals its T = 2 accuracy once the filament is resolved.
  • Second: plain defect-corrected Eulerian transport — simple, robust, matches SL's shape error at T = 2/512² but falls 20× behind on the long horizon and costs 2× more.
  • SDPLS sources: measurable volume-conservation help under maximal stretching on COARSE meshes only (their regime: under-resolved two-phase production runs + zero-interface-displacement coupling) — a niche, not a default; they never beat plain Euler at t = T in this benchmark.
  • Velocity extension (closestPoint): dominated at every resolution and end time — up to 27× the cost for the worst accuracy.
  • The under-resolution death of the T = 8 filament at N ≤ 64 is method-independent; 512² resolves it for every line — refinement, not maintenance, is the cure.
