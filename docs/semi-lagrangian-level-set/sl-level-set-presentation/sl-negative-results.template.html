What did NOT fix the parasitic-current instability, and why

semi-Lagrangian reinit-free level set · 2D stationary water/air droplet (R=1 mm, ρ ratio 839, La = 7.3×107)

Every entry: a reasonable idea, implemented and verified correct, that failed for a mechanistic reason worth remembering.
Reference problem: the universal \(h\)-driven blow-up, \(t_{\mathrm{blow}} = 0.466/0.444/0.105/0.032\) at \(N=32/64/128/256\) (Euler, \(\Delta t_\sigma/4\), cell-local symbolic \(\kappa\), CSF).

Root cause (verified): Defect 1 — no discrete equilibrium (cell \(\kappa\) is the parallel-contour value, \(\kappa_d=\kappa/(1+d\kappa)\) varies across the force support); Defect 2 — grid-scale \(\psi\)-profile modes alias into the tangential Hessian (sign-flipping \(\kappa\) errors, diagonal-dominant). Companion deck: sl.html.

Higher-order momentum time integration (backward, CN)

Idea: replace 1st-order Euler in the momentum equation by 2nd-order backward (or Crank–Nicolson) — better temporal accuracy should not hurt a spatially-seeded problem.

Measured: backward DESTABILIZES the previously stable \(N{=}64\) case (frozen rhoPhi: peak 48 at \(t{=}0.048\); active: \(10^{102}\) at 0.051). Stable only at \(\Delta t \lesssim \Delta t_\sigma/8\) (2× the steps). CN 0.9 diverges even faster (\(t{=}0.017\)).

Why it failed: Euler is L-stable and damps the grid-scale parasitic capillary mode every step; backward is only A-stable, CN is neutral — the L-stable damping was load-bearing for the explicit capillary coupling, not an accuracy defect.

Lesson: in an explicitly-coupled capillary loop, the time scheme is part of the STABILIZATION, not just the accuracy budget. Monotone: less numerical dissipation ⇒ smaller required capillary \(\Delta t\).

Smaller / adaptive time step

Idea: if the coupling is unstable, an adaptive \(\Delta t\) that respects a stricter capillary constraint should stabilize every resolution.

Measured: reducing \(\Delta t\) only DELAYS the onset; the blow-up scaling is \(h\)-driven (coarse meshes with BIGGER \(\Delta t\) survive LONGER: \(t_{\mathrm{blow}}\) 0.466 at \(N{=}32\) vs 0.032 at \(N{=}256\)). We already run 2.8× inside Brackbill's limit. Later replicated for CST: \(\Delta t/2\) moved \(t_{\mathrm{blow}}\) only 0.047→0.050.

Why it failed: the instability is a SPATIAL feedback (curvature-error force → interface distortion → worse curvature), with a per-unit-time gain set by \(h\), not a capillary-CFL violation. (The capillary limit itself is SUM-density \(\rho_1{+}\rho_2\) — the "light-phase \(\Delta t/29\)" hypothesis was wrong.)

Lesson: if refining the MESH at fixed physics destabilizes, no time-step policy can cure it — measure \(t_{\mathrm{blow}}(N)\) before touching \(\Delta t\).

Foot-point (Newton) normal extension of κ

Idea: make \(\kappa\) constant along interface normals (the height-function property) by Newton-projecting each band cell to its interface foot point and copying the foot value.

Measured: the divergence onset moves EARLIER (\(t\approx0.06\) vs 0.11 without); at blow-up the written \(\kappa = 1.36\times10^{5}\) vs a cell-centre refit of only \(3.3\times10^{3}\) at the same cells — the projection amplifies 40–85×.

Why it failed: the Newton step \(\delta \leftarrow \delta - \psi/|\nabla\psi|^2\,\nabla\psi\) assumes a clean signed distance; on the drifting field it lands on a displaced evaluation point and the amplification grows exactly when accuracy is needed most. The GOAL (normal constancy) was right; the mechanism was the amplifier.

Lesson: any post-processing that divides by \(|\nabla\psi|\)-dependent quantities inherits the profile drift as gain.

Harmonic (Laplace) curvature extension

Idea: pin \(\kappa\) at interface cells (fvMatrix::setValues), solve \(\nabla^2\kappa=0\) — a smooth, monotone extension with no Newton step.

Measured: \(N{=}64\) blows at \(t\approx0.051\) (tight solve) or 0.013 (loose) vs 0.44 for the plain cell-local \(\kappa\). Strictly worse.

Why it failed: smoothing/coupling CORRELATES the curvature error across the band: the arithmetic face average of decorrelated ± speckle partially cancels at faces; once both sides of a face agree on one signed error, the parasitic force acts at full strength, coherently.

Lesson: "smooth the noisy input" backfires when the noise was self-cancelling at the delivery points. Cell-LOCAL \(\kappa\) is the most stable of the three extension options — it stayed the default.

Curvature clamping + temporal under-relaxation

Idea (E5): clamp \(\kappa\in[0,2000]\) — if the sign-flipping grid-scale \(\kappa\) excursions drive the blow-up, capping them should cure it.

Measured: \(t_{\mathrm{blow}} = 0.111\) vs 0.105 unclamped — unchanged.

Why it failed: the SMOOTH error channels (Defect-1 normal variation + \(\theta\)-dependent isosurface error), all inside the clamp, suffice to drive the feedback. The spectacular excursions are a symptom, not the engine.

Idea (foot-point HF rescue): temporal under-relaxation \(\kappa^n = 0.2\,\kappa_{\mathrm{new}} + 0.8\,\kappa^{n-1}\) and ray-distance parent assignment to filter discrete foot/parent switching.

Measured: \(t_{\mathrm{blow}}\) 0.006 → ~0.009 — delay only. Why: the estimator's RESPONSE to interface deformation is mis-phased; filtering its jitter does not fix its phase.

FVM curvature \(\nabla\cdot(\nabla\psi/|\nabla\psi|)\)

Idea: normalize BEFORE differentiating and use conservative Gauss operators — a different discrete route to \(\kappa\) with built-in low-pass character.

Measured: \(N{=}128\) still diverges (\(t_{\mathrm{blow}}=0.116\) vs 0.105); \(N{=}64\) stable but with a standing current of \(8\times10^{-4}\) — 60× the reconstruction's \(1.3\times10^{-5}\) (interFoam-class).

Why it failed: it still READS THE \(\psi\) PROFILE. All profile-reading curvatures diverged in the same \(0.105{-}0.116\) window — the operator choice changes the gain slightly, not the loop.

Lesson: the family is the problem, not the member.

\(\nabla\cdot(\nabla\alpha/|\nabla\alpha|)\) on the sharp geometric α

Idea: a geometry-only curvature from the volume fraction (interFoam-style), decoupled from the \(\psi\) profile entirely.

Measured: instant/fast blow-up in every variant (raw \(t\sim8\times10^{-5}\); smoothed, consistent pairs \(10^{-3}\)–\(6\times10^{-3}\)). Offline: interface-layer \(\kappa\) is fine (1016±141) but the force support contains ±3750 SUPPORT-EDGE CLIFFS, and the geometric indicator's tangential staircase survives smoothing into the tails.

Why it failed: differential \(\kappa(\alpha)\) estimators require an algebraically-diffused (MULES-like, tangentially smooth) \(\alpha\). On a SHARP geometric \(\alpha\), geometry-only needs INTEGRAL estimators — height functions integrate the staircase out.

Lesson: estimator and interface REPRESENTATION are a pair; porting one without the other transplants the failure.

Foot-point height-function κ from ψ-planes

Idea: geometry-only + integral + normal-constant \(\kappa\) — feet from the linear LSQ planes, tangential parabola fit, shared column value (the HF property built from \(\psi\), not \(\alpha\)-columns).

Measured: STATIC: excellent — band rel-\(L_2\) vs the constant \(1/R\): 8.3/1.6/0.48/0.22% at \(N{=}32/64/128/256\) (5–16× better than symbolic). COUPLED: a NEW, faster instability — \(t_{\mathrm{blow}}\) 0.006 (\(N{=}64\)), 0.003 (\(N{=}128\)); ms-scale, worse at finer \(N\).

Why it failed: the fitted-feet \(\kappa\)'s response to interface DEFORMATION is mis-phased/destabilizing in the loop. Popinet (2018) anticipated it: point/weight selection in fit-based estimators is "complicated and somewhat fragile".

Lesson (the campaign's central one): static accuracy is NOT dynamic sufficiency — gate estimators on their deformation transfer function \(\delta\kappa(\cos m\theta)\), not on static convergence tables.

Closest-point NEWTON foot for κ (the root-finder upgrade)

Idea (user-proposed): the retired foot-point extension used a gradient-projection flow (\(\delta \leftarrow \delta - P/|\nabla P|^2\,\nabla P\)) — replace it with a TRUE closest-point Newton on the cell's own quadratic (KKT system with the fit Hessian), trust-region-guarded (\(|\delta| \le r_{\mathrm{stencil}}\), indefinite/degenerate rejection, per-cell fallback to the centre value), symbolic \(\kappa\) at the converged foot.

Measured (straight-to-coupled, user's call): \(N{=}64\): decays to \(3.0\times10^{-6}\) at \(t=0.14\) — the best CSF-delivery floor of the campaign (4× below the no-extension plateau) — then the \(\psi\)-drift-led feedback returns (min\(|\nabla\psi|\) 0.975→0.76 in lockstep) and it blows at \(t=0.279\) vs no-extension 0.44. \(N{=}128\): blows at \(t=0.075\) — later than the old projection flow (0.06; the guards help) but earlier than no-extension (0.105).

Why it failed: the root-finder was never the load-bearing defect — Newton converges beautifully to the foot of a Defect-2-corrupted quadratic. Displaced evaluation of corrupted coefficients + normal-constant coherence raise the loop gain exactly as before.

Lesson (6th replication): the better the static balance, the earlier the coupled failure — now demonstrated across projection-flow vs Newton root-finders too. Estimator INPUT quality, not foot-finding precision, is the frontier.

Higher-order fits, SVD, steeper weights (the pole-bias thread)

Idea(s): the \(\cos 4\theta\) grid-anisotropy curvature bias at the poles — attack with (a) SVD instead of normal equations, (b) steeper IDW weights, (c) a quartic (deg-4) fit.

Measured: (a) SVD identical TO ALL DIGITS (the bias is a property of the least-squares PROBLEM, not the solver; conditioning \(5\times10^9\) yet the solve is machine-accurate). (b) steeper weight halves the bias — but the optimum scales with \(R/h\): case-local tuning, rejected on principle. (c) quartic: 30× better on the CLEAN field, \(O(h^4)\) — but WORSE than quadratic on the noisy run field (bias–variance crossover at noise \(\sim10^{-4}h\); the run's drift is far above it).

Why they failed: curvature is a second derivative — maximally noise-amplifying; more fit freedom chases noise. Also: a BIGGER stencil at the SAME order is worse (more quartic contamination).

Lesson: clean the field first; only then is higher order safe. Order without cleanliness is dangerous.

Signed-distance-preserving SL reconstruction (foot reprojection)

Idea: make the SL advection itself SDF-preserving — reproject the departure foot onto the reconstructed interface and copy the signed distance instead of the interpolated value.

Measured: mathematically correct (unit test \(1.3\times10^{-3}\)), but UNDERPERFORMS the plain value-copy control on the 2D reversed vortex.

Why it failed: the reprojection injects reconstruction noise into every step's transport; the plain value copy is the exact identity at rest and adds nothing that isn't already there.

Lesson: a per-step geometric correction must beat the noise it injects; "preserving a property approximately every step" can be worse than not touching it.

Kang/GFM interface-weighted \(\kappa_f\) (± sharp Heaviside)

Idea: the published exact-balance cure (Kang 2000; Abadie 2015): \(\kappa_f=(\kappa_P|\psi_N|+\kappa_N|\psi_P|)/(|\psi_P|+|\psi_N|)\) — interpolate the parallel-contour values TO the crossing, killing the Defect-1 normal variation at the faces.

Lesson: the better the static balance, the higher the dynamic feedback gain (replication 1 and 2 of the tension).

CST integral model — verbatim transcription (the sign trap)

Idea: Abu-Al-Saud/Popinet/Tchelepi (2018) — surface tension as \(-\nabla\cdot(p\mathbf{I}+\sigma)\), momentum-conserving, machine-zero equilibrium reinit-free. Transcribe Algorithms 1–2 + Appendix B exactly.

Measured: \(\Delta t\)-INDEPENDENT exponential instability at the grid-capillary frequency (same blow-up TIME at \(\Delta t = 2\times10^{-5}\) and \(2\times10^{-6}\)); survives \(10^{-14}\) pressure solves; erf-smoothed \(\rho\) slows it 6× only. Decisive clue: measured Laplace jump \(-72.7\) Pa — exact magnitude, WRONG SIGN.

Why: the PAPER's printed relations carry an inverted overall sign (its Eq. 8 has \(+\nabla\cdot\sigma\) against its own \(-\nabla\cdot(p\mathbf{I}+\sigma)\) identity; the App-B equilibrium relations hand-evaluate to \(p_{\mathrm{air}} = p_{\mathrm{water}} + \gamma\kappa\) on an SDF circle). The algorithms are \(\psi\)-orientation-INVARIANT, so no convention rescues them; their sign-blind error metric \(|[p]_{\mathrm{comp}}-[p]_{\mathrm{exact}}|\) hides it.

Lesson: an inverted capillary force = ANTI-RESTORING: growth at \(\omega_{\mathrm{capillary}}\), immune to \(\Delta t\), tolerances, smoothing. Lock the SIGN with a static Laplace-jump check (signed!) before any coupled run.

CST integral model — sign-corrected, at fine resolution

Idea: with the physical sign, CST should reach the machine-zero equilibrium of the paper.

Measured: \(N{=}64\): the campaign's FIRST reachable equilibrium — decay \(3.3\times10^{-3}\to5\times10^{-7}\) (t=0.2), \(\Delta p\) 0.02% — then slow regrowth from \(t\approx0.25\). \(N{=}128\): blows at \(t\approx0.047\), EARLIER than CSF's 0.105; \(\Delta t/2\) does not help.

Why it failed at fine \(h\): the CST inputs still read the \(\psi\) profile — \(\tilde\kappa\) at crossings, and tangents that are unit only while \(|\nabla\psi|=1\): tangential balance error \(\sim\gamma(|\nabla\psi|-1)/h^2\). The paper caps this with THRESHOLD reinitialization at the \(h^2\) deviation level ("particularly important in the present context") — hygiene a reinit-free loop lacks by design.

Lesson: replication 3 and 4 of the tension — CST has the deepest statics of all, and (at \(N{=}128\)) the fastest coupled failure. Published equilibrium results can silently depend on the reference code's reinit policy.

Mass–momentum-consistent flux (steps 2a, 2b)

Idea: the literature cure for density-ratio-839 blow-ups (Rudman; Vaudor; Ghods–Herrmann): consistent \(\rho_f\phi\) — first arithmetic \(\mathrm{interp}(\rho)\phi\) (2a), then the geometric UPWIND face fraction \(\alpha_f\) from the level-set planes (2b, rhoLENT-style).

Measured: onset essentially UNCHANGED (0.1055 → 0.1086); but the terminal blow-up is \(\sim\)97 ORDERS gentler (peak \(1.6\times10^{7}\) vs \(7.9\times10^{104}\)).

Why (partially) a negative: consistency governs the CASCADE (once currents advect the interface, the light phase amplifies), not the TRIGGER (the curvature-error seed). Right fix, wrong lever for the onset.

Lesson: separate trigger from cascade in the diagnosis before selecting the remedy — a 97-orders improvement can still be "onset unchanged".

Threshold-triggered plane-anchored redistancing (PRELIMINARY)

Idea: cap the \(\gamma(|\nabla\psi|-1)/h^2\) channel with the paper's criterion (fire when band-\(L_1\) of \(||\nabla\psi|-1|\) exceeds \((h/L)^2\)), anchoring to the SAME LSQ planes the indicator/mass-flux read (Eikonal + foot-wave fills).

Measured (static gate, \(N{=}64\)): threshold \((h/L)^2 = 2.4\times10^{-4}\); band \(L_1\) of the ANALYTIC SDF = \(6.9\times10^{-3}\) — the Gauss-gradient METRIC FLOOR is 28× the threshold ⇒ fires EVERY step. Both fills currently RAISE \(L_1\) (Eikonal → \(8.6\times10^{-2}\); foot-wave → \(2.1\times10^{-2}\), scalloping of the discrete foot-POINT cloud). Every-step firing kicks \(\max|\mathbf u|\) 10–20× in 5 steps — the regime both papers warn destroys equilibrium.

Open, not closed: the paper's threshold presumes high-order (WENO) derivatives with a far lower metric floor. Candidate repairs: threshold referenced to the measured \(t{=}0\) floor; continuous plane-fill instead of point-feet. Policy pending. Lesson so far: a trigger criterion is only as good as its measurement floor in YOUR discretization.

Band-limited ψ-filter (grid-scale damping alone)

Idea: the stability inequality says the reinit-free SL loop has ZERO damping for \(\delta\psi\) — add the minimal explicit term: \(\psi \leftarrow \psi - \theta\,L(L(\psi))\) in the band + 1 ring, \(L = I - \mathrm{avg}(\mathrm{interp}(\cdot))\) (annihilates quadratics ⇒ no smooth-interface displacement; damps the 2h carrier at near-maximal rate). Everything else unchanged — the clean falsifier of the damping theory.

Measured (N=128, quadratic pipeline, baseline \(t_{\mathrm{blow}}=0.105\)): static sanity at \(\theta=0.2\): smooth-field perturbation at the \(10^{-4}\)-relative level ✓. Coupled: \(\theta=0.05 \Rightarrow\) survives \(t=0.3\) (tail growing at ~33 s\(^{-1}\), 6× slower than the unfiltered 190 s\(^{-1}\)); \(\theta=0.2 \Rightarrow t_{\mathrm{blow}}=0.442\) — 4.2× the baseline, delay monotone in \(\theta\). The damping mechanism is CONFIRMED quantitatively.

Why it did not close the gap: min\(|\nabla\psi|\) erodes steadily (0.94→0.33) with NO 2h content — the feedback migrates to LONGER wavelengths the scale-selective filter deliberately ignores. The instability cascades to the shortest UNDAMPED scale.

Lesson: grid-scale dissipation buys constant factors, not a sign flip of the eigenvalue — the damping must be BROADBAND in the band (i.e. SDF maintenance), not mode-selective.

In stability language: the linear transport's interpolation diffusion is BROADBAND \(\delta\psi\) damping.

Methodology traps that produced false positives

  • The premature-cure trap (fell in TWICE): "stable at \(t=0.075\)" claims retracted after longer runs — the no-extension switch and the foot-point HF both looked cured until run past the baseline \(t_{\mathrm{blow}}\). Rule: run 2–3× past the baseline blow-up time before any verdict.
  • The accidental-blend trap: a "temporal relaxation" captured \(\kappa_{\mathrm{old}}\) AFTER the symbolic refill — silently testing a 0.2 foot + 0.8 symbolic SPATIAL blend that "passed" only because the symbolic part is stable at \(N{=}64\). Rule: verify what a filter actually blends before believing its pass.
  • The contention trap: concurrent solver runs on one memory-bound machine produce FALSE divergences. Rule: verdict-grade runs are serial.
  • The written-field trap: curvature recomputed from a WRITTEN \(\psi\) (6 significant digits) shows \(\sim\)90% error — a post-processing artifact, not solver state. Rule: diagnose 2nd derivatives in-memory.
  • The sign-blind-metric trap: a published scheme with an inverted force passes its own \(|[p]-[p]_{\mathrm{exact}}|\) test. Rule: check SIGNED jumps.
