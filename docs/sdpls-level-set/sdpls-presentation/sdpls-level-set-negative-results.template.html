Why beta fails — start from the gradient law

Differentiate \(\partial_t\psi + \mathbf u\cdot\nabla\psi = f_{nl}\,\psi\) and evaluate at \(\psi = 0\). With \(g = |\nabla\psi|\) and the interfacial normal strain \(a = \hat n\cdot\nabla\mathbf u\cdot\hat n\):

\[\frac{\mathrm D g}{\mathrm D t} = g\,(f_{nl} - a)\]

R: \(f_{nl} = a\)

\(\mathrm D g/\mathrm D t = 0\) — identically. The source cancels interfacial stretching exactly. There is no target to chase and no fixed point to be unstable about; whatever error remains is numerical, which is why it converges under refinement.

beta: \(f_{nl} = \beta - g\)

\(\mathrm D g/\mathrm D t = g\,(\beta - g - a)\) — a logistic equation in \(g\) with two fixed points:

  • \(g^\ast = \beta - a\) — stable, but set by the flow
  • \(g = 0\) — unstable whenever \(\beta - a > 0\)

Both of them are trouble, and for different reasons.

The same law is why the two sources are not variants of one idea: one sets the right-hand side to zero, the other sets up a competition against the flow.

Failure 1 — the target is set by the flow, so refinement cannot fix it

measured — h-independent Band mean \(|\nabla\psi|\) at maximal deformation, N = 32…512: 1.516, 1.498, 1.478, 1.485, 1.481 — flat to ~2% over a sixteenfold refinement. R over the same ladder: 1.071 → 1.002.

\(g^\ast = \beta - a\) contains no mesh spacing. A discretization residual vanishes as \(h\to0\); a wrong target does not. This is the cleanest discriminator in the record, and it says the offset is structural rather than numerical — so no amount of resolution, and no better limiter, will remove it.

Note what it is not: volume and shape errors sit at \(10^{-5}\)–\(10^{-4}\) for every arm. beta is not displacing the interface. It is holding \(|\nabla\psi|\) at the wrong level.

Failure 2 — over most of the interface the target is unreachable

measured — target negative Band strain reaches \(\max a = 2.77\) against \(\beta = 1\). The target \(\beta - a\) is negative somewhere in the band for 93% of the time steps.

Wherever \(a > \beta\), the stable fixed point \(\beta - a\) is negative — and \(g = |\nabla\psi|\) is a magnitude. It cannot reach it. The logistic dynamics then drive \(g\) toward the only reachable fixed point, \(g = 0\): the level set flattens hardest exactly where the flow stretches hardest.

consequence Band minimum \(|\nabla\psi|\) at N = 512: 0.423 for beta against 0.883 for R — the two seeing the identical flow.

Choosing a bigger \(\beta\) does not escape this: \(a\) is a property of the flow, and on any stretching interface it will exceed whatever constant you pick somewhere.

Failure 3 — \(g=0\) is unstable, so the flattening rebounds

Near \(g = 0\) the logistic equation linearises to \(\mathrm D g/\mathrm D t \approx (\beta - a)\,g\). While \(a > \beta\) that rate is negative and \(g\) collapses. When the strain reverses sign, the rate turns positive and the collapsed cells grow exponentially.

measured — onset at the reversal Band max \(|\nabla\psi|\): 154.9 at t = 1.025 for T = 2, and 98.0 at t = 4.063 for T = 8 — i.e. at t/T = 0.5125 and 0.508. The same fraction of the period, four times the absolute time: the trigger is the reversal, not a clock.

So flattening and blow-up are not two problems. They are one mechanism seen on either side of the reversal: collapse toward an unstable fixed point, then exponential escape from it.

retracted — falsified at its own configuration The earlier claim was that beta+explicit blew up at N=128, T=2 for CFL ∈ {0.1, 0.5, 1.0} through an anti-damped oscillation, dt-independently. Re-run after the 2026-08 source fixes: all three linearizations complete at every CFL and agree to five significant digits.

Kinematic verification is structurally blind to the coupled failure

\[\psi \;\longrightarrow\; \kappa \;\longrightarrow\; \mathbf{f}_\sigma \;\longrightarrow\; \mathbf{v} \;\longrightarrow\; a \;\longrightarrow\; \psi\]

structural — not a matter of more rungs or more arms Under a prescribed velocity the return path \(\psi \rightarrow \mathbf{v}\) does not exist, so no ladder of the verification section — however many rungs, however many arms — could have detected what the coupled section measures.

In a two-phase flow the path exists: the level set produces the curvature, the curvature the surface-tension force, the force the velocity, and the velocity the interfacial normal strain \(a = \hat n\cdot\nabla\mathbf{v}\cdot\hat n\) that the source reads back. A source term that reads \(a\) closes a feedback loop that no kinematic ladder can exercise.

The rule. A source term that reads the computed velocity must be verified on both tracks. A method paper that reports only the kinematic one is incomplete by construction rather than by omission.

Article Section 8, retained item 5; Section 4 (verification); Section 5 (the coupled droplet). The coupled measurements are in the main deck, Parts 9–11.

An M-matrix requires two things: a dominant diagonal, and non-positive off-diagonals. The sign split addresses the diagonal, and the diagonal was never the problem — no row of the Rdiv system loses dominance outright, which is why every run completes. The mechanism: a difference of two upwind operators loses the maximum principle

fvMatrix::operator== subtracts termwise, so Rdiv assembles \[\mathrm{ddt}(\psi) + 2\,\mathrm{div}(\phi,\psi) - \mathrm{div}(\phi_{\mathbf{w}},\psi) + (\text{diagonal terms}) = 0\] — a difference of two upwind convection operators rather than one. A single upwind fvm::div has non-positive off-diagonals whatever the sign of the face flux; subtracting one flips them. On a face where \(\mathrm{sign}(\phi_f) \neq \mathrm{sign}(\phi_{\mathbf{w},f})\) the composite off-diagonal is exactly \(+|\phi_{\mathbf{w},f}|\).

Two controls close it — and why Rdiv cannot be repaired

control 1 — the 1D gate: zero mismatches, and Rdiv is exact On the 1D uniaxial-stretch gate \(\mathbf{w} = \mathbf{v}\), hence \(\phi_{\mathbf{w}} = \phi\): the probe reports zero sign-mismatched faces and zero positive off-diagonals, and Rdiv is exact.
control 2 — the blend sweep is unimodal and peaks at λ = 1 \(\phi_\lambda = \lambda\phi + (1-\lambda)\phi_{\mathbf{w}}\); every member is continuum-equivalent because the tangential velocity does no work on a level set, \(\mathbf{v}\cdot\nabla\psi = \mathbf{w}\cdot\nabla\psi = (\hat n\cdot\mathbf{v})\,|\nabla\psi|\). The sweep peaks at \(\lambda = 1\) — at \(\phi\), the flux the algebraic source R convects on — on gradient, shape and volume simultaneously.

A temporal correction cannot repair a spatial order

measured — three deliveries of one source, within 0.03 in gradient order The exponential linearization delivers the source's one-step factor exactly and removes the \(O(z^2)\) rectification of implicit Euler identically — \(\frac{1}{1-z}\cdot\frac{1}{1+z} = 1 + z^2 + O(z^4) > 1\). It changes the band gradient order by 0.003: +1.109 against +1.112 on the reversed 2D vortex (tab:expsource, sdplsExpSource2DvortexRev, seven rungs, four MPI ranks), and by 0.028 on the serial three-rung ladder that carries all three deliveries: +0.936 against +0.908 (tab:expimplicit).

The order loss of the source-error section is \((\nabla e)_h = O(h^{p-1})\), which no time discretization touches. This is the most direct test of that statement in the paper: a rational approximation of the one-step factor, the exact factor delivered explicitly, and the exact factor delivered implicitly, all landing within 0.03 of each other in gradient order.

A single scalar \(\varepsilon\) displaces the zero contour by \(\varepsilon/|\nabla\psi|\) everywhere at once, so it can only remove a volume error that is itself a nearly uniform interface displacement; where no such displacement remains it is fitted to residual noise and moves the interface by that noise instead. \(|\nabla\psi|\) is untouched in all four pairs of arms and at both dimensionalities.

Two coupled-patch defects suspend every parallel kinematic SDPLS result

defect 1 — a face-centre value in a cell-centre slot: O(a), not O(h) velocityModel::setVelocity wrote a face-centre velocity into coupled patches, where the neighbouring cell-centre value is expected, giving an \(O(a)\) — not \(O(h)\) — error in fvc::grad(U) in every processor-adjacent cell, and hence in the strain \(a\) the source reads.
defect 2 — the raw flux written over processor faces interfaceExtension::updateFlux wrote the raw flux over processor patches, which are interior faces in serial, so the field advecting \(\psi\) differed between serial and parallel by construction.

Both are fixed; both were parallel-only; neither could be caught by a serial gate. Fitted orders from multi-rank ladders are provisional until re-measured; the findings they support stand only where a serial measurement reproduces them — which, for the two that matter here, it does: the explicit exponential's shape and volume cost (tab:expimplicit) and the divergence of Rdiv at the same magnitude (tab:rdiv-serial). Suspended as fitted orders: tab:expsource and tab:volcorr, four MPI ranks in their own captions. Unaffected: everything measured serially — tab:rdiv-coefficient, tab:rdiv-serial, tab:rdiv-blend, tab:expimplicit, tab:expimplicit1d and the 1D gate — and the coupled droplet studies, which obtain \(\mathbf{U}\) from the momentum equation.

Article Section 8, retained item 12; Section 7 (the two defects and the results they suspend). Post-mortem: docs/gradU-coupled-patch-contamination.md. Main deck Part 13.
