\title{The SDPLS level-set method for two-phase flows: source-term control
of the signed-distance property without interface displacement}

\author[tuda]{Tomislav Mari\'{c}}
\author[tuda]{Mathis Fricke}
\author[tuda]{Dieter Bothe}
\address[tuda]{Mathematical Modeling and Analysis, TU Darmstadt, Germany}

\begin{abstract}
Reinitialization displaces the interface: the zero level set and the
narrow-band values are the same degrees of freedom, so any rewrite of the
band trades transport accuracy for reconstruction accuracy and pays a
spurious volume change per redistancing step. The SDPLS method avoids the
trade entirely: a source term $S = f_{nl}(\psilev^n)\,\psilev$ that
vanishes identically on the zero level set controls the gradient of
$\psilev$ through the transport equation itself --- zero interface
displacement by construction. We analyze and measure the stability of
three source-term linearizations, control the source gradients through
dedicated discretization keywords, and benchmark the method against
semi-Lagrangian transport and interface-normal velocity extension on the
reversed vortex at resolutions up to $512^2$, including wall-clock cost
and the maximally-stretched state at $t = T/2$. TODO: benchmark numbers.
\end{abstract}

\section{Method}
\subsection{The source term}
\begin{equation}
  \partial_t \psilev + \nabla\cdot(\mathbf{v}\psilev)
  - \psilev\,\nabla\cdot\mathbf{v} = f_{nl}(\psilev^n)\,\psilev .
  \label{eq:sdpls}
\end{equation}
Because $S \propto \psilev$, the source vanishes identically on the zero
level set: the interface evolves by transport alone. Along characteristics
$d\ln|\nabla\psilev|/dt = -(\nhat\cdot\nabla\mathbf{v}\cdot\nhat)$, so
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{R}: $f_{nl} = (\nhat\cdot\nabla\mathbf{v}\cdot\nhat)$
    cancels the normal-strain drift of $|\nabla\psilev|$ (the SDPLS model);
  \item \textbf{beta}: $f_{nl} = \beta - |\nabla\psilev|$ relaxes
    $|\nabla\psilev| \to \beta$ (the convected level-set idea
    \cite{ville2011}).
\end{itemize}
The source gradients are discretized under dedicated scheme keywords
(\texttt{gradPsiSdpls}, \texttt{gradUSdpls}) --- never silently coupled to
the advection scheme's limited $\nabla\psilev$.

\subsection{Linearization and stability}
$S = f_{nl}(\psilev^n)\,\psilev^{n+1}$ splits into an explicit part
$S_c$ and an implicit diagonal part $S_p$:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{l l l}
\hline
discretization & split & stability (measured, $N{=}128$, CFL 0.1--1.0) \\
\hline
explicit & $S_c = f_{nl}\psilev^n$, $S_p = 0$ &
  R: stable; \textbf{beta: diverges at every CFL} \\
simpleLinearImplicit & $S_p = f_{nl}$ (any sign) &
  both stable; beta: best accuracy (4.5e-5) \\
strictNegativeSp & $S_p = \min(f_{nl},0)$, $S_c = \max(f_{nl},0)\psilev^n$ &
  both stable; diagonally dominant by construction \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The beta+explicit divergence is dt-independent: the relaxation source turns
its stable fixed point into an anti-damped oscillation when lagged
explicitly --- the implicit diagonal must carry it. R is mild
($|f_{nl}| \le \max\|\nabla\mathbf{v}\|$), stable in all variants.

\subsection{Defect correction and the two-phase coupling}
Second-order deferred div schemes (linearUpwind) require re-assembly of
(\ref{eq:sdpls}): \texttt{nDefCorr} passes in pure advection; in the
two-phase solver the equation is re-assembled \emph{every PIMPLE outer
corrector} (verified: steps $\times$ correctors $=$ $\psilev$ solves), so
\texttt{nOuterCorrectors} is the defect-correction control. Interface
updates (band, redistancing criterion, volume fraction) run once per time
step on the final corrector.
