\title{The SDPLS level-set method for two-phase flows: source-term control
of the signed-distance property without interface displacement}

\author[tuda]{Tomislav Mari\'{c}}
\author[tuda]{Mathis Fricke}
\author[tuda]{Dieter Bothe}
\address[tuda]{Mathematical Modeling and Analysis, TU Darmstadt, Germany}

\begin{abstract}
Reinitialization displaces the interface: the zero level set and the
narrow-band values are the same degrees of freedom, so any rewrite of the
band trades transport accuracy for reconstruction accuracy and pays a
spurious volume change per redistancing step. The SDPLS method avoids the
trade entirely: a source term $S = f_{nl}(\psilev^n)\,\psilev$ that
vanishes identically on the zero level set controls the gradient of
$\psilev$ through the transport equation itself --- zero interface
displacement by construction. We report the first valid measurement of what
that source does to the level-set gradient error in this framework. Two
implementation defects had to be removed first: the source was applied with a
\emph{reversed sign} on its explicit branch, so it doubled the gradient drift
instead of cancelling it, and the error metric measured $\gradpsi$ with a
\emph{cell-limited} gradient operator that returns exactly zero at a local
extremum --- a different operator from the one the source controls. With both
corrected, and with every arm run on one discretization, the strain-cancelling
source reduces the band gradient error by $31\times$ on the finest mesh of a
seven-rung 2D vortex ladder and \emph{converges under refinement where the
sourceless baseline diverges} ($+0.74$ against $-0.26$). The separation survives
into 3D shear ($+0.668$ against $-0.094$) and weakens to boundedness on the
3D LeVeque deformation, where the source holds the error flat ($-0.045$) while
the baseline diverges ($-0.914$). A third contribution is negative and methodological:
the reversed (oscillating) benchmark on which such source terms are
conventionally assessed cannot answer this question at all. Because the
interfacial stretching integrates to zero over the period, the final-time error
of a passively advected level set cancels by construction, and the same case
run reversed reports the sourceless baseline as $477\times$ \emph{more}
accurate. Every measurement here is therefore made on a non-reversing flow.
Coupled to momentum on a stationary droplet, the same source fails, and the
failure is now attributed: against a surface-tension force built from the
\emph{exact} curvature of a circle, whose spurious current is machine zero by
construction, the source produces $0.000$ relative volume error and $0.000$ shape
error at every resolution and on both level-set time discretizations, twelve
cases out of twelve, while against the production curvature it destroys the
droplet at $N=32$ and diverges at $N=64$ and $N=128$. The capillary feedback loop
therefore has exactly one seed --- curvature error --- and the repair belongs in
the surface-tension force rather than in the source; three time discretizations
of the level set, spanning first-order L-stable, second-order A-stable and
second-order L-stable, change when the coupled run fails but not whether it does.
A conservative divergence-form rewrite of the source is retracted as evidence
about that loop and then withdrawn outright: it diverges under a \emph{prescribed}
divergence-free velocity with no capillary force at all, at band gradient order
$-3.800$ on the 2D vortex and $-3.383$ on 3D shear, with every run completing, and
a sign-split variant that demonstrably removes the M-matrix violation of its
implicit coefficient --- the one mechanism its failure analysis had named ---
leaves the divergence essentially where it was ($-3.554$). The mechanism is
elsewhere, and is now measured in the assembly: because
\texttt{fvMatrix::operator==} subtracts the source termwise, the divergence form
assembles a \emph{difference} of two upwind convection operators, whose composite
off-diagonal is $+|\phi_{\mathbf{w},f}|$ on every face where the two fluxes
disagree in sign ($36.3\%$ of internal faces, $21.0\%$ positive off-diagonal
coefficients, at a mesh-independent strength of about $0.38\,\mathrm{CFL}$), so the
discrete maximum principle is lost while diagonal dominance --- and hence
solvability --- is retained, which is why every run completes. Repairing it would
recover stability and not accuracy: a sweep over the continuum-equivalent
convecting fluxes $\lambda\phi + (1-\lambda)\phi_{\mathbf{w}}$ peaks at
$\lambda = 1$, the flux the algebraic source already convects on, on gradient,
shape and volume simultaneously, while the conservation the divergence form exists
to provide forces $\lambda \neq 1$. An earlier closeout attributing that failure to
a violated discrete product rule is retracted here: measured at the first assembly
rather than at $t=T$, and over a fixed physical band rather than through a global
$L_\infty$ whose argmax sits on the circle-centre kink, the product-rule residual
and the coefficient error are both second order. On the source's delivery,
requiring the exact one-step factor to be applied \emph{implicitly},
$S_p = (1-e^{-z})/\Delta t$ with $S_c = 0$, recovers in full the shape and volume
order that the explicit exponential delivery gives up, while keeping unconditional
sign preservation and removing the $|f_{nl}|\Delta t < 1$ diagonal restriction by
construction. Two coupled-patch defects --- a face-centre velocity written into
coupled patches, and the raw flux written over processor faces --- have been fixed;
they are parallel-only, and every parallel kinematic result carrying an SDPLS
source predates them and is pending re-measurement.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Level-set transport degrades $\gradpsi$: the gradient magnitude obeys
$\mathrm d\ln\gradpsi/\mathrm dt = -(\nhat\cdot\nabla\mathbf{v}\cdot\nhat)$
along characteristics, so an initially signed-distance field stops being one as
soon as the interface strains. Classical reinitialization
\cite{sussman1994,russo2000} restores $\gradpsi\approx1$ by rewriting the band,
and pays for it with interface displacement --- the cost measured in the
companion GRL line. The SDPLS idea is to leave the interface alone and act
only where the level set is free: a source proportional to $\psilev$ itself.

% ============================================================================
\section{Method}
\subsection{The source term}
\begin{equation}
  \partial_t \psilev + \nabla\cdot(\mathbf{v}\psilev)
  - \psilev\,\nabla\cdot\mathbf{v} = f_{nl}(\psilev^n)\,\psilev .
  \label{eq:sdpls}
\end{equation}
Because $S \propto \psilev$, the source vanishes identically on the zero
level set: the interface evolves by transport alone. Two choices are
implemented:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{R}: $f_{nl} = (\nhat\cdot\nabla\mathbf{v}\cdot\nhat)$
    cancels the normal-strain drift of $\gradpsi$ (the SDPLS model);
  \item \textbf{beta}: $f_{nl} = \beta - \gradpsi$ relaxes
    $\gradpsi \to \beta$ (the convected level-set idea \cite{ville2011}).
\end{itemize}
The two differ structurally, and the difference is visible in the results.
\textbf{R} cancels the drift and leaves no preferred value, so its interfacial
equilibrium is set only by the cancellation residual. \textbf{beta} relaxes
towards $g_\ast = \beta - a$ with $a = \nhat\cdot\nabla\mathbf{v}\cdot\nhat$:
its target is \emph{velocity dependent}, and therefore wrong wherever the
interface strains.

The source gradients are discretized under dedicated scheme keywords
(\texttt{gradPsiSdpls}, \texttt{gradUSdpls}) --- never silently coupled to
the advection scheme's limited $\nabla\psilev$, which is a different operator
serving a different purpose (Section~\ref{sec:metric}).

\subsection{Linearization and stability}
$S = f_{nl}(\psilev^n)\,\psilev^{n+1}$ splits into an explicit part
$S_c$ and an implicit diagonal part $S_p$: \texttt{explicit}
($S_c = f_{nl}\psilev^n$, $S_p = 0$), \texttt{simpleLinearImplicit}
($S_p = f_{nl}$) and \texttt{strictNegativeSpLinearImplicit}
($S_p = \min(f_{nl},0)$, $S_c = \max(f_{nl},0)\psilev^n$, diagonally dominant
by construction).

All three discretize the \emph{same} continuum term, so at $\psilev=\psilev^n$
they must apply the same physical source. That identity is now asserted by a
standalone unit test on an affine exact solution
(\texttt{leiaTestSdplsSource}); it is what exposed the two defects of
Section~\ref{sec:defects}, and it is the reason the measured orders of
Section~\ref{sec:verification} agree to three digits between linearizations.

\subsection{Defect correction}
Second-order deferred div schemes (\texttt{linearUpwind}) carry their
second-order part as an explicit right-hand-side source, so one pass is
first-order in practice. The same loop also Picard-iterates the SDPLS
nonlinearity, because $f_{nl}$ is relinearized from the current iterate at
every pass. Both argue for at least three passes; all results below use
\texttt{nDefCorr} $=3$.

% ============================================================================
\section{Two defects that invalidated the previous record}
\label{sec:defects}

\subsection{A reversed sign on the explicit branch}
\texttt{fvMatrix::operator==} subtracts the right-hand matrix termwise, so a
contribution written into \texttt{source\_} enters the transport equation with
a \emph{minus} --- OpenFOAM's own \texttt{fvm::Su} encodes this by doing
\texttt{source\_ -= V*su}. The assembled equation was therefore
$\mathrm D\psilev/\mathrm Dt = S_p\psilev^{n+1} - S_c$. Consequently
\texttt{explicit} \emph{doubled} the gradient drift instead of cancelling it,
and \texttt{strictNegativeSpLinearImplicit} did so on its $f_{nl}>0$ branch
only. \texttt{simpleLinearImplicit} ($S_c=0$) was never affected --- which is
precisely why the defect survived, because every published mesh ladder used
\texttt{strictNegativeSp}.

The signature is unambiguous in the stored output: with no source
$\ln g = -\!\int\! a$, with correct SDPLS $0$, and with a reversed sign
$-2\!\int\! a$. Measured at $N=512$, the ratio
$\ln g(\text{SDPLS:R})/\ln g(\text{noSource})$ was
$2.003,\ 2.002,\ 2.001,\ 1.997$ at $t = 0.05,\ 0.10,\ 0.20,\ 0.30$.

\subsection{A use-after-free in the same branch}
\texttt{explicitDiscretization::Sc} returned a \texttt{tmp} bound to a
temporary destroyed at the end of the return statement, so the values later
multiplied by the cell volumes were whatever remained in the freed block. It
was caught by the affine unit test: \texttt{explicit} disagreed with
\texttt{strictNegativeSp} in the regime where the two have identical $S_c$ and
$S_p$ by construction, which no legitimate discretization difference can
produce.

\subsection{The metric measured the wrong operator}
\label{sec:metric}
The gradient-error function objects called the \emph{unnamed}
\texttt{fvc::grad(psi)}, which resolves to the advection scheme's
\texttt{grad(psi)} --- \texttt{cellLimited leastSquares 1}. A cell-limited
(minmod) gradient returns \emph{exactly zero} at a local extremum, so the
metric reported $\gradpsi = 0$ and $e = 1$ there regardless of the true
gradient. On an \emph{exact analytic signed distance} the old metric reported
$E^{\max}_{\nabla\psi} = 1.000$ and $\min\gradpsi = 0.000$ at every resolution
from $N=32$ to $512$. Both objects now use a dedicated unlimited keyword; on
the same exact field the maximum error becomes $0.667$ (the circle-centre
kink, finite and mesh-independent) and the band $L_2$ error retains its
$O(h^2)$ behaviour ($6.4\times10^{-3} \to 2.5\times10^{-5}$ over
$N=32\to512$; fitted orders $1.97,\ 2.04,\ 2.01$).

\subsection{One discretization, or no comparison}
Arms run with different level-set advection schemes are different methods. An
earlier attempt at the 3D studies pinned \texttt{div(phi,psi) Gauss linear}
while the 2D studies used \texttt{Gauss linearUpwind grad(psi)}, and nothing in
the curated output recorded the difference; the 3D result appeared to show the
source achieving nothing. Every run reported here records the discretization it
actually used, parsed from its rendered \texttt{fvSchemes}, and a gate
(\texttt{make check-discretization}) asserts that a study group used one:
114 finite-volume cases, one discretization, for the results below.

\section{Verification}
\label{sec:verification}

Non-reversing flows: the 2D single vortex, the 3D shear and the 3D LeVeque
deformation, each on a uniform refinement ladder at $\mathrm{CFL} = 0.5$, with
\texttt{noSource}, \textbf{R} and \textbf{beta} $\times$ the two admissible
linearizations. The reversal is switched off deliberately, and the reason is
the subject of the next paragraph.

\paragraph{Which instant measures what.}
\label{par:reversal}
On a reversed flow the velocity carries a $\cos(\pi t/\tau)$ factor, so
$\int_0^T a\,\mathrm dt = 0$ along every interfacial trajectory. That
cancellation corrupts one column, and it is worth being precise about which,
because the reversal is also the only thing that makes a shape error definable.

At $t=T/2$ the factor has been positive throughout, so $\int_0^{T/2} a\,\mathrm dt$
is at its \emph{maximum}: the interface is maximally deformed and nothing has
cancelled. Gradient and volume error are both honest measurements there. At
$t=T$ the interface has returned to its starting position, which is what makes
the shape error --- measured against the initial condition --- meaningful at
all; volume conservation remains a valid check. But the $t=T$ \emph{gradient}
error compares a source's residual against an exactly-cancelling control: a
passively advected level set is carried back to its initial signed-distance
state, while \emph{any} source does work on $\psilev$ along the trajectory that
the return leg does not undo. That column rewards whichever scheme accumulated
the most \emph{recoverable} error, and it is not reported in this work.

The distortion is not small. On the 2D vortex at $T=2$, band gradient error at
$t=T$:

while \textbf{R}'s is unchanged at $+0.74$.

the corruption is confined to the $t=T$ gradient column rather than

in the generated tables rather than left to the reader's judgement.

Without a source the level set loses the signed-distance property monotonically
under refinement: the band mean of $\gradpsi$ runs $2.15 \to 3.52$ as $N$ goes
$32 \to 256$. The vortex winds the interface into a filament, the stretching
term $a$ steepens $\gradpsi$ without bound, and nothing in the sourceless
equation restores it. Only \textbf{R} converges, and it is $31\times$ more
accurate than the baseline on the finest mesh.

Volume conservation is not paid for to buy this. From $N=128$ upward
\textbf{R} conserves volume better than the baseline as well as converging
faster in it ($+3.37$ against $+1.39$). The volume orders are fitted over
$N \ge 64$: at $N=32$ the sourceless baseline itself loses $24.5\%$ of the
phase volume, and a run that has lost a quarter of its volume is not a
convergence data point whatever its gradient error reads.

Geometric (shape) error is \emph{not} reportable on this non-reversing control:
it is defined against the initial condition, which is the correct answer at
$t=T$ only if the flow returns the interface there. That is precisely why the
reversed configuration is retained for the shape measurement rather than
discarded --- the two configurations answer different questions, and the
protocol above assigns each metric to the instant at which it is defined.
Shape error at $t=T$ does benefit from the return leg, in the same way the
gradient error does; it is reported as the conventional benchmark it is, and
the signed-distance claim of this work rests on the $t=T/2$ column and on the
non-reversing control, neither of which does.

\paragraph{3D shear.}
The reversed 3D shear ladder ($N = 64, 81, 102, 128, 161, 203$, each level
doubling the cell count) reproduces the 2D result and prices it. Band gradient
error at $t=T/2$: the sourceless baseline stalls at order $-0.094$ while
\textbf{R} converges at $+0.668$, within striking distance of the 2D $+0.74$,
and is $21.4\times$ more accurate on the finest mesh ($0.119$ against $2.535$).
\texttt{beta} converges weakly, $+0.292$.

The cost is real and runs the other way from 2D. At $t=T$, where shape error is
defined, \textbf{R} is $3.7\times$ worse in shape than the baseline
($5.4\times10^{-4}$ against $1.5\times10^{-4}$, orders $+1.34$ and $+2.21$) and
$18\times$ worse in volume conservation ($2.5\times10^{-2}$ against
$1.4\times10^{-3}$, orders $+0.96$ and $+2.57$). On the non-reversing 2D vortex
\textbf{R} conserved volume \emph{better} than the baseline from $N=128$ up; on
3D shear it does not. \texttt{beta} is the best of the three in both volume
($+4.12$) and shape ($+2.00$) while being the worst source term for the
gradient --- which is the whole point of reporting all three metrics rather
than the headline one. The two linearizations agree to four digits on every
entry.

\paragraph{3D LeVeque deformation, where \textbf{R} stops converging.}
The deformation ladder is the hardest of the three and it does not reproduce
the convergence. Band gradient error at $t=T/2$: the sourceless baseline
diverges hard, order $-0.914$, running $2.65 \to 7.73$ across the ladder.
\textbf{R} is \emph{flat} --- order $-0.045$, sitting at $0.39\ldots0.41$ at
every resolution --- and \texttt{beta} diverges at $-0.442$. So on this case
\textbf{R} does not converge; it holds the signed-distance error bounded while
the baseline's grows, ending $18.6\times$ more accurate at $N=203$. That is a
weaker claim than 2D and 3D shear support, and it is the honest one.

Volume conservation inverts relative to shear. Here \textbf{R} is the best arm
by a wide margin, $+3.56$ against the baseline's $+1.26$ and $13\times$ better
in absolute terms on the finest mesh ($8.6\times10^{-3}$ against $0.114$);
\texttt{beta} diverges, $-0.621$. Shape error separates the arms hardly at all
($+1.07$, $+1.14$, $+1.08$). The volume story is therefore case-dependent, not
systematic: \textbf{R} is $18\times$ worse than doing nothing on shear and
$13\times$ better on deformation.

One caveat governs every number in this paragraph. The sourceless baseline
still loses $11\%$ of its phase volume on the finest $203^3$ mesh, and $51\%$ on
the coarsest. The LeVeque deformation at $T=3$ draws the interface into sheets
thinner than the cell size across this whole ladder, so no arm here is
resolution-converged and the fitted orders describe an under-resolved regime.

\paragraph{The result.}
The sourceless band gradient error \emph{grows} under refinement while the
strain-cancelling source's shrinks (2D vortex $+0.74$, 3D shear $+0.668$) or at
worst stays flat (3D deformation $-0.045$, against the baseline's $-0.914$).
That separation is the substance of the method: it is the difference between a
level set that degrades as the mesh improves and one that does not. It is also
invisible on a reversed flow read at $t=T$, which is why it went unmeasured for
so long. What the source buys is the signed-distance property. What it costs is
not yet understood: on 3D shear it pays in volume conservation and interface
position, on 3D deformation it is the best volume-conserving arm of the three,
and nothing in the present data explains the difference.

\paragraph{Where the missing order is not.}
\label{par:ablation}
\textbf{R} converges, but at $+0.74$ --- well short of the formal order of the
discretization, and the shortfall needs an explanation rather than a caveat.
Two candidates can be eliminated by measurement. The first is the
\emph{reconstruction gradient} of the \texttt{linearUpwind} advection: it is
cell-limited by default, and a limiter that clips at local extrema is an
obvious order-killer for a quantity whose extrema are the thing being measured.
A 56-case ablation replacing it with cell-limited Gauss, unlimited least
squares and face-limited least squares, holding everything else fixed over the
full $N = 32\ldots256$ ladder, gives fitted orders of $0.740$, $0.740$, $0.743$
and $0.658$. Removing the limiter entirely moves the order by $0.003$. It does
improve the error by about $5\%$ at every rung, so the limiter costs a little
accuracy and no order at all. The sourceless arm is equally indifferent
($-0.255$ against $-0.240$). Face limiting is the only variant that changes
anything, and it is worse in both order and error. The second candidate is the
time integration, and it is excluded by inspection: these runs use
\texttt{CrankNicolson}, which is second order, not first-order Euler, so there
is no first-order temporal cap to explain a sub-unit slope.

The default arm of this ablation reproduces the $+0.74$ of the main ladder to
three digits from an independently configured study, which is the cross-check
that the two are measuring the same thing.

What remains is not yet resolved, and two things are worth separating. On the
measurement side, the narrow band is the set of sign-change cells, so it is
attached to the interface and its physical width shrinks like $O(h)$: the norm
is taken over a different region at every rung, which is the right region to
care about but not a fixed domain on which a classical order is defined. On the
method side, the cancellation is built from $\mathbf{n} = \nabla\psilev/\gradpsi$,
so the accuracy of the strain that the source cancels is bounded by the accuracy
of the gradient it is computed from --- the source cannot cancel a stretching
term more precisely than it can estimate it. Distinguishing these requires a
fixed-width band and a normal-error measurement, and neither is claimed here.

\subsection{Where the order is lost: an error estimate for the source}
\label{sec:sourceerror}

The measurements of the previous paragraph exclude every implementation-side
cause, so the remaining explanation has to be structural. It is, and it follows
from the error-estimation framework of Jasak \cite{jasak1996} and Jureti\'{c}
\cite{juretic2004,juretic2010}: expand every discrete operator about the cell
centroid, and separate what acts on the \emph{exact} field from what acts on the
\emph{solution error}.

Write $\psilev$ for the exact solution and $\psilev_h$ for the discrete one, and
let $e = \psilev_h - \psilev$ be the solution error. On the uniform orthogonal
hexahedral meshes used here the gradient reconstruction is second order
\emph{for a smooth field},
\begin{equation}
  (\nabla\phi)_h = \nabla\phi + C_2\,h^2\,\nabla^3\phi + O(h^3),
  \label{eq:gradtrunc}
\end{equation}
with the non-orthogonality and skewness contributions of
\cite{juretic2004,juretic2010} absent by construction. Applying it to
$\psilev_h = \psilev + e$ and using linearity,
\begin{equation}
  \underbrace{(\nabla\psilev_h)_h - \nabla\psilev}_{\displaystyle \delta\mathbf{g}}
  \;=\; \underbrace{C_2 h^2 \nabla^3\psilev}_{\text{smooth part, } O(h^2)}
  \;+\; \underbrace{(\nabla e)_h}_{\text{error field}} .
  \label{eq:gradsplit}
\end{equation}
The two terms are not the same order. The first is the classical truncation
term. The second differentiates a field that is $O(h^p)$ in magnitude but is
\emph{not smooth on the scale $h$}: $e$ is defined only at cell centres and
generically varies from one cell to the next at its own magnitude, so
$|(\nabla e)_h| \sim |e|/h = O(h^{p-1})$. For the second-order transport used
here, $p=2$ and the error-field term is $O(h)$ --- it dominates.

The consequence propagates directly to the source. Only the \emph{tangential}
part of a gradient perturbation moves the normal,
$\delta\mathbf{n} = (\mathbf{I}-\nhat\otimes\nhat)\cdot\delta\mathbf{g}/\gradpsi$,
so
\begin{equation}
  \delta a \;=\; 2\,\nhat\cdot\nabla\mathbf{v}\cdot\delta\mathbf{n}
              \;+\; \nhat\cdot\delta(\nabla\mathbf{v})\cdot\nhat
        \;=\; O(h^{p-1}) + O(h^2),
  \label{eq:straerr}
\end{equation}
the second term being second order because $\mathbf{v}$ is prescribed and
smooth. With the numerical source $f_{nl}=a_h$, the gradient law of
Section~\ref{par:beta} becomes $D(\ln g)/Dt = a_h - a = \delta a$, so
\begin{equation}
  \bigl\|\,\gradpsi-1\,\bigr\| \;=\; \Bigl\|\int_0^T \delta a\,\mathrm dt\Bigr\|
  \;=\; O(h^{p-1}) \;=\; O(h) .
  \label{eq:sdplsorder}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{The estimate is falsifiable, and the data test it.}
Equation~\eqref{eq:gradsplit} predicts something sharp: at $t=0$ the discrete
field \emph{is} the sampled exact signed distance, so $e=0$ identically, the
error-field term vanishes, and only the $O(h^2)$ truncation term survives. The
order must therefore be $2$ at $t=0$ and fall to $1$ as soon as transport
creates a nonzero $e$ --- not gradually as the interface deforms, but
immediately. Fitting the band error at successive instants:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lrrrrrr}
\toprule
$t/T$ & $0$ & $0.02$ & $0.05$ & $0.10$ & $0.25$ & $0.50$ \\
\midrule
2D reversed vortex & $+2.010$ & $+1.071$ & $+1.108$ & $+1.195$ & $+1.323$ & $+1.227$ \\
3D shear           & $+2.006$ & $+1.122$ & $+1.143$ & $+1.301$ & $+0.896$ & $+0.668$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Both cases sit at exactly $2$ before the first step and drop to $\approx1.1$
within $2\%$ of the period, which is the predicted behaviour and not what a
slowly growing $\nabla^3\psilev$ would produce. The later decay on 3D shear is
the separate under-resolution effect of Section~\ref{par:ablation}: where the
interface approaches the cell size the expansion \eqref{eq:gradtrunc} has no
validity at all, and the $L_\infty$ norm degrades faster than $L_1$
($+0.27$ against $+0.72$ at $t=T/2$) exactly as a few unresolved cells would
predict.

\paragraph{The estimate bounds the GRADIENT error only.}
Equation~\eqref{eq:sdplsorder} is a statement about $\gradpsi-1$, because that
is what $\delta a$ drives through $D(\ln g)/Dt$. It says nothing about the shape
and volume errors, which are properties of the \emph{zero level set} rather than
of the gradient, and which are limited by a different quantity: an interface
displacement of $e/\gradpsi = O(h^p)$ rather than a strain error of
$O(h^{p-1})$. The two therefore need not --- and do not --- share an order:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{llrrrr}
\toprule
case & source & gradient $(T/2)$ & volume $(T/2)$ & volume $(T)$ & shape $(T)$ \\
\midrule
2D vortex & none      & $-0.094$ & $+1.724$ & $+2.725$ & $+1.531$ \\
2D vortex & \textbf{R}& $+1.228$ & $+2.913$ & $+1.984$ & $+1.630$ \\
3D shear  & none      & $-0.094$ & $+2.224$ & $+2.574$ & $+2.213$ \\
3D shear  & \textbf{R}& $+0.668$ & $+1.773$ & $+0.956$ & $+1.347$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Two readings follow, and both belong in any account of the method. The shape and
volume orders sit at $1.3$--$2.9$, comfortably above the gradient orders, which
is what the $O(h^p)$ against $O(h^{p-1})$ split predicts: the gradient error is
one order behind by construction, and no choice of scheme, time step or
iteration count closes that gap. And the three metrics do not move together
under the source. On the 2D vortex the source is free --- it converts a
divergent gradient error into $+1.23$ while leaving shape and volume unharmed.
On 3D shear it is not: the gradient error becomes convergent, but volume falls
from $+2.57$ to $+0.96$ and shape from $+2.21$ to $+1.35$. Reporting the
gradient order alone would overstate the method on one case and understate its
cost on the other.

\paragraph{A direct measurement of $\delta a$, and where it disagrees.}
The estimate above is inferred from the band gradient error. Measuring
$\delta a = a_h - a$ directly --- comparing the solver's registered strain field
at $t=0$ against $\nhat\cdot\nabla\mathbf{v}\cdot\nhat$ evaluated with the exact
circle normal and the analytic Rider--Kothe velocity --- gives a partly
different picture. Over the narrow band of the 2D ladder ($N=32\ldots512$) the
fields agree closely, correlation $0.99994$ and r.m.s.\ matching to $0.05\%$,
and the error converges at $L_1$ order $+1.25$. But two cells out of $868$ at
the finest resolution carry errors of order $20\%$ that do not converge at all,
so $L_2$ and $L_\infty$ fall to $+0.63$ and $+0.12$.

Two things follow, and the second is an open question rather than a result.
The bulk order $+1.25$ is consistent with \eqref{eq:sdplsorder}, but it is
\emph{not} the $O(h^2)$ that \eqref{eq:gradsplit} predicts at $t=0$, where
$e=0$ by construction. The resolution is that the two projections of
$\delta\mathbf{g}$ need not share an order: the band metric
$\gradpsi-1$ senses the \emph{normal} component $\nhat\cdot\delta\mathbf{g}$,
which is second order at $t=0$ as measured, while $\delta\nhat$ --- and hence
$\delta a$ --- senses only the \emph{tangential} component. The estimate as
written does not separate them, and the direct measurement says it should. That
refinement, and the identity of the few non-converging cells, are left open
here; what is established is that the source error and the band gradient error
share the same $O(h)$ bulk behaviour, which is what
\eqref{eq:sdplsorder} needs.

\paragraph{What the estimate implies.}
Three things, and they are all consequences of $p-1$ rather than of any choice
we made. \emph{First}, the gradient scheme cannot help: it changes $C_2$ in
\eqref{eq:gradtrunc}, not the exponent in $(\nabla e)_h$. Measured --- compact
least squares, extended point-cell least squares and Green--Gauss give
$+1.256$, $+1.254$ and $+1.253$. \emph{Second}, second order in the
\emph{gradient} error requires $p=3$: a third-order transport scheme, not a
rearranged source. \emph{Third}, the estimate bounds $\delta a$ through
$(\nabla e)_h$, so a normal that does not differentiate $\psilev_h$ at all ---
one reconstructed from an interface fit --- is not covered by
\eqref{eq:gradsplit}. That avenue has now been measured and it closes. The
Detrixhe--Aslam phase indicator already fits a least-squares plane to
$\psilev$ in every band cell to construct the volume fraction; registering that
fitted normal and using it as the source's $\nhat$ (with the phase indicator,
and hence $\alpha$, unchanged --- a single-variable ablation) gives band
gradient orders of $+1.19$ against $+1.25$ for the differentiated normal, with
the absolute error $40\%$ larger at every resolution and the shape order
falling from $+2.21$ to $+1.61$. The fitted normal is \emph{less} smooth
tangentially than the gradient it replaces --- it is a per-cell fit over a
compact stencil --- and it is the tangential perturbation that drives
$\delta a$. With this, every implementation-side candidate is measured: the
advection limiter ($0.003$), the time scheme (Crank--Nicolson), the time step
($\le1.6\%$), the Picard iteration ($\le0.4\%$), three gradient schemes for the
normal ($0.003$), and the interface-fitted normal (worse). Within second-order
transport, the band gradient order of $\approx1.2$ is the ceiling, and second
order in the gradient requires third-order transport.

\paragraph{Why \texttt{beta} fails.}
\label{par:beta}
\texttt{beta} is not unstable on this flow and it is not under-resolved; it
converges accurately to the wrong value. Differentiating~\eqref{eq:sdpls} along
the interface with $g = \gradpsi$ gives $Dg/Dt = g\,(f_{nl} - a)$. The
strain-cancelling choice $f_{nl} = a$ makes this identically zero, so $g$ is
left wherever it started, namely at $1$. The choice $f_{nl} = \beta - g$ makes
it logistic,
\begin{equation}
\frac{Dg}{Dt} = g\,(\beta - g - a),
\label{eq:betalogistic}
\end{equation}
with a stable fixed point at $g^{*} = \beta - a$ and an unstable one at $g=0$.
The stable target is set by the \emph{flow}, not by the signed-distance
condition, and equals the value actually wanted only where $\beta = 1 + a$.
Since $a$ varies over the interface and over time, no constant $\beta$ is
correct except by coincidence.

The prediction is quantitative and it holds. On the non-reversing vortex with
$\beta = 1$ and $a$ measured per time step from the registered strain field,
the band mean of $\gradpsi$ at $t=T$ against the predicted $\beta - a$:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lccccccc}
\toprule
$N$ & 32 & 45 & 64 & 90 & 128 & 181 & 256 \\
\midrule
measured $g$          & 1.600 & 1.851 & 2.023 & 2.080 & 2.001 & 1.922 & 1.927 \\
predicted $\beta - a$ & 1.820 & 1.790 & 1.804 & 1.836 & 1.841 & 1.872 & 1.866 \\
\addlinespace
\textbf{R} control    & 0.953 & 0.995 & 1.041 & 1.075 & 1.109 & 1.073 & 1.021 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The measured equilibrium tracks $\beta - a$ to between $2.7\%$ and $13.3\%$ and
shows no tendency toward $1$ under refinement, while the \textbf{R} control on
the same flow, subject to the same strain, holds $g$ at $1.02$. This is why
\texttt{beta}'s band gradient error saturates near $0.98$ and its order is
$-0.04$: refinement cannot remove an error that the equation is asking for. A
28-case sweep over $\beta \in \{0.5, 1.0, 1.5, 2.0\}$ confirms the target
independently --- the band mean shifts one-for-one with $\beta$, measured slope
$0.938$ against a predicted $1$.

\paragraph{The equilibrium is only measurable on a steady flow.}
That evidence is deliberately drawn from the \emph{non-reversing} vortex, and
the reversed 3D shear shows why it has to be. Equation~\eqref{eq:betalogistic}
is pointwise and instantaneous, so $g^{*} = \beta - a$ is a target that moves
whenever $a$ does. Over the 3D shear trajectory at $N=203$ the interfacial
strain has time-mean $-0.005$ --- zero, as $\int_0^T a\,\mathrm dt = 0$
requires --- but mean magnitude $0.78$, ranging over $[-1.22, +1.21]$ and
changing sign at the turnaround. \texttt{beta} therefore chases a target that
reverses under it: its band mean runs $1.000 \to 1.542 \to 0.723$ from $t=0$
through $T/2$ to $T$, settling at neither $1$ nor $\beta - a$ for any $a$ the
run ever had. A steady flow holds $a$ still long enough for the equilibrium to
be reached, and is the only configuration in which the target can be measured
at all.

The same trajectory makes the cancellation argument of
Section~\ref{par:reversal} concrete rather than algebraic. The sourceless band
mean runs $1.000 \to 3.162 \to 1.003$: the level set loses the signed-distance
property by a factor of three and is then carried all the way back, so a
measurement taken only at $t=T$ would score it as very nearly perfect.
\textbf{R} over the same trajectory runs $1.000 \to 1.050 \to 1.008$ with a
maximum of $1.070$ --- it does not merely arrive in a good state, it never
leaves one.

Equation~\eqref{eq:betalogistic} also accounts for the failure mode seen on the
reversed runs. Where $\beta - a < 0$ the target is negative, so the source
drives $g \to 0$; but $g = 0$ is the \emph{unstable} fixed point, so those cells
grow without bound once the strain changes sign, with band maxima reaching
$154.9$. Flattening and blow-up are one mechanism, not two.

\paragraph{Linearization agreement.}
The two admissible linearizations agree to three digits on every fitted order
($0.698/0.699$, $0.477/0.478$, $-0.169/-0.169$), as they must: they discretize
the same continuum term. Disagreement between them was the symptom that led to
the defects of Section~\ref{sec:defects}.

\subsection{Where the error lives}
\label{sec:atlas}
A fitted order says that a method improved; it does not say \emph{where}.
Figure~\ref{fig:graderr-atlas} maps the signed-distance error
$\bigl|\gradpsi-1\bigr|$ over the whole domain for the strain-cancelling source
and for \texttt{beta}, across the refinement ladder, at the initial instant, at
maximal deformation, and after the flow has reversed.

The two behave differently in a way the aggregate norms compress away. For
\texttt{R} the error collapses over a growing neighbourhood of the recovered
interface as the mesh refines --- at $N=512$, $t=T$ the field around the zero
level set is four to five decades below unity --- and the recovered
$\psilev=0$ contour is a clean circle. For \texttt{beta} the bulk error stays
at $O(1)$ at every resolution, and the recovered contour carries additional
internal structure rather than closing on the initial circle. That is the
expected signature of a flattening level set: where $\gradpsi$ collapses, the
interface position error scales like $e_\psilev/\gradpsi$, so small value noise
produces spurious zero crossings. It is the same statement as the band minimum,
$0.423$ for \texttt{beta} against $0.883$ for \texttt{R} at $N=512$, seen in
space instead of as a single number.

\paragraph{What two instants cannot show.}
The tables report $t=T/2$ and $t=T$. That suffices to fit an order and is not
enough to see a mechanism. Figure~\ref{fig:trajectory} plots the band statistics
at \emph{every} time step, together with the strain the source is reacting to,
and it exposes a failure the aggregates conceal. The band \emph{maximum} of
$\gradpsi$ peaks at $3.81$ for no source and at $1.23$ for \texttt{R}; for
\texttt{beta} it reaches $154.9$, and it is still $123.5$ at $t=T$. The band
mean at $t=T$ is $1.256$, so the blow-up is confined to a few cells and the mean
never reveals it.

It begins at $t \approx 1.03$ --- immediately after the flow reverses at $T/2$
--- and the gradient law says why. Where $a > \beta$ the source drives $g$ toward
zero, but $g=0$ is an \emph{unstable} fixed point of
$\mathrm{D}g/\mathrm{D}t = g(\beta - g - a)$ as soon as the strain changes sign
and $\beta - a > 0$: the flattened cells then grow exponentially. Flattening and
blow-up are the same mechanism seen on either side of the reversal, and the
right-hand panel shows the target $\beta - a$ is negative over part of the band
for $93\%$ of the time steps.

\section{The source under two-way coupling: the stationary droplet}
\label{sec:coupled}

Every measurement reported so far was made under a \emph{prescribed} velocity.
That is the correct configuration in which to measure what the source does to
$\gradpsi$: the velocity is exact, so every error in the band is the level set's
own, and the fitted orders of Section~\ref{sec:verification} mean what they say.
It is also structurally blind to one thing. Under a prescribed velocity the path
$\psilev \rightarrow \mathbf{v}$ does not exist. In a two-phase flow it does: the
level set produces the curvature, the curvature produces the surface-tension
force, the force produces the velocity, and the velocity produces the interfacial
normal strain $a = \nhat\cdot\nabla\mathbf{v}\cdot\nhat$ that the source reads
back,
\begin{equation}
  \psilev \;\longrightarrow\; \kappa \;\longrightarrow\; \mathbf{f}_\sigma
  \;\longrightarrow\; \mathbf{v} \;\longrightarrow\; a \;\longrightarrow\; \psilev .
  \label{eq:capillaryloop}
\end{equation}
A source term that reads $a$ therefore closes a feedback loop that no kinematic
ladder can exercise. This section measures the source on that loop, and the
result is negative, specific, and --- this is the part that was not established
before --- attributable to a single seed.

\subsection{Configuration, and what counts as an error}
\label{sec:coupled-setup}

The case is the two-dimensional stationary droplet
(\texttt{cases/stationaryDroplet2D}): a water droplet of radius
$R = 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m}$ centred in a square box of side
$L = 10^{-2}\,\mathrm{m}$ filled with air, on a uniform Cartesian mesh of
$N \times N$ cells, $N \in \{32, 64, 128\}$. Water and air are
$\rho = 998.2$ and $1.19\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$, $\nu = 10^{-6}$ and
$1.53\times10^{-5}\,\mathrm{m^2/s}$, the surface-tension coefficient is
$\sigma = 0.07274\,\mathrm{N/m}$, gravity is zero, the four side walls are
\texttt{noSlip} and the front and back patches are \texttt{empty}. The solver is
\texttt{leiaLevelSetTwoPhaseFoam} (PIMPLE, level set advected in the same outer
loop as momentum and pressure), every run is on $8$ MPI ranks, the end time is
$0.1\,\mathrm{s}$, and the time step is fixed at
$\Delta t = 0.010861\,\mathrm{s} \cdot N^{-3/2}$ --- the capillary limit
$\Delta t \sim h^{3/2}$, calibrated to $6\times10^{-5}\,\mathrm{s}$ at $N=32$ and
therefore about $2\times10^{-5}\,\mathrm{s}$ at $N=64$. No time-step adjustment
is used, so the step is the same in every arm at a given $N$ and cannot silently
differ between them.

The exact solution is known in closed form and is trivial: $\mathbf{v} = \mathbf{0}$
everywhere, with a pressure jump $\sigma\kappa = \sigma/R$ across a circular
interface that never moves. \emph{Every velocity the solver produces on this case
is error.} That is what makes the configuration diagnostic rather than merely
demanding: there is no physical motion for a numerical artefact to hide behind.

The reported metric is the relative \emph{signed} phase-volume error
\begin{equation}
  E^{\mathrm{vol}}_{\mathrm{rel}}(t) \;=\;
  \frac{\sum_c \alpha_c(t)\,|\Omega_c| \;-\; \sum_c \alpha_c(0)\,|\Omega_c|}
       {\sum_c \alpha_c(0)\,|\Omega_c|},
  \label{eq:evolsigned}
\end{equation}
written as \texttt{E\_VOL\_ALPHA\_SIGNED\_REL} in the curated output, with
$\alpha$ the phase indicator the level set carries. The sign is retained
deliberately: a magnitude cannot distinguish systematic volume \emph{loss} from
an error that oscillates in sign, and on this case those are different failure
modes. $E^{\mathrm{vol}}_{\mathrm{rel}} = -1$ means the droplet is gone --- the
indicator is zero in every cell --- and a large positive value means the run has
diverged and $\alpha$ has left $[0,1]$. The companion shape error is
$E^{\mathrm{geom}} = \sum_c |\alpha_c(t) - \alpha_c(0)|\,|\Omega_c|$, reported
relative to the same denominator.

\paragraph{Which metrics exist here, and which do not.} The protocol of this
paper is to report gradient, shape and volume error together, so it must be said
plainly where a column is absent. The coupled studies report the volume error
\eqref{eq:evolsigned} at every write time and the shape error alongside it; the
band gradient error $\|\gradpsi - 1\|$ was \emph{not} measured in any of the
coupled droplet studies, and no gradient order is claimed anywhere in this
section. For the production-curvature arms below the shape column is likewise not
restated, because it was not extracted for this record. Where an arm reports
$E^{\mathrm{vol}}_{\mathrm{rel}} = -1.000$ the interface has ceased to exist, and
no shape number is needed to establish that the run failed.

Three readings, in order of how much they constrain the explanation.

\emph{The solver and the force are sound.} The sourceless control converges
cleanly and rapidly: on Euler, $2.810\times10^{-3} \to 1.843\times10^{-4} \to
1.204\times10^{-5}$ is a factor of $15.2$ and then $15.3$ per mesh doubling. The
other two time discretizations of $\psilev$ reproduce that control to about one
per cent at $N=32$ and $N=64$; only at $N=128$ do they separate, where
Crank--Nicolson reaches $7.647\times10^{-6}$ against Euler's
$1.204\times10^{-5}$ and backward's $1.210\times10^{-5}$, which is a difference
between three good answers and not a difference in kind. A discretization that
conserves the phase volume to one part in $10^5$ at $N=128$ while carrying a
parasitic current is not the thing that destroys the droplet when a source term
is switched on.

\emph{The failure is not a blow-up at coarse resolution and a mild error at fine
resolution; it is worse under refinement.} At $N=32$ the source drains the
droplet to nothing over the run, $-1.000$, on a mesh whose cell size
$h = L/N = 3.125\times10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m}$ puts $6.4$ cells across the droplet
diameter; at $N=128$ that becomes $25.6$ cells, four times better resolved. At
$N=64$ and $N=128$ the run does not survive to the end time at all on either
Euler or Crank--Nicolson: the volume error passes
through zero from below and reaches $+27.7$ and $+19.3$ before the solver fails,
and it fails \emph{earlier} on the finer mesh ($t=0.0398$ against $t=0.0584$).
Refinement makes this worse, monotonically, which excludes every explanation
resting on under-resolution of the interface.

\emph{The failure is not the time step.} The step is set by the capillary limit
and shrinks like $N^{-3/2}$, so the $N=128$ arm takes about eight times as many
steps per unit time as the $N=32$ arm and still fails soonest. A violated
temporal stability limit would be relieved by that, not aggravated. (A fourfold
refinement of $\Delta t$ at fixed mesh, reported in the companion deck and not
repeated here, likewise left the drain rate essentially unchanged.)

\paragraph{The mechanism: a mesh-locked mode-4 parasitic current, amplified.}
Figures~\ref{fig:mode4-glyphs} and~\ref{fig:mode4-structure} show the velocity
field at $t=0.030\,\mathrm{s}$, $N=64$, for the sourceless control and for
\textbf{R}, from otherwise identical runs. The sourceless field is the familiar
continuum-surface-force artefact on a Cartesian mesh: a four-lobed (mode-4)
spurious current, aligned with the mesh axes and diagonals, with
$\max|\mathbf{v}| = 3.9\times10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m/s}$, and an interface that
remains circular. Switching on \textbf{R} does not change the \emph{structure} of
that field at all --- the same four lobes in the same places --- and multiplies
its amplitude by $260$, to
$\max|\mathbf{v}| = 1.0\times10^{-1}\,\mathrm{m/s}$, while the interface deforms
into a rounded square aligned with the lobes.

That is loop \eqref{eq:capillaryloop} operating as an amplifier rather than as a
transport. The discrete force inherits the four-fold symmetry of the mesh and
supplies a \emph{bounded} mode-4 velocity error; the source reads the strain of
that error and rewrites $\psilev$ in the band; the rewritten band changes
$\kappa = \nabla\cdot(\nabla\psilev/\gradpsi)$ at the cell centres; the changed
curvature changes the force; and the loop closes with a gain greater than one.
The interface becomes a square because the seed has square symmetry, not because
anything in the source prefers squares.

\subsection{Why the time discretization of $\psilev$ is not the driver}
\label{sec:coupled-time}

There is a specific reason to suspect the time discretization on this case, and
it is worth writing out because it is quantitative, because it predicted the
right sign of an effect, and because the measurement then refused it as the
explanation.

With the \texttt{simpleLinearImplicit} linearization the source contributes only
a diagonal coefficient $S_p = f_{nl} = a$. Paired with implicit Euler on
$\partial_t\psilev$, the cell update is
$(\psilev^{n+1} - \psilev^{n})/\Delta t = a\,\psilev^{n+1}$, i.e.
\begin{equation}
  \psilev^{n+1} = G(z)\,\psilev^{n}, \qquad G(z) = \frac{1}{1-z},
  \qquad z \equiv a\,\Delta t .
  \label{eq:amp}
\end{equation}
On the stationary droplet the strain $a$ that the source reads is not a
mean-valued deformation rate: it is the strain of an oscillating parasitic
current, so it changes sign at the parasitic frequency with a near-zero mean.
What matters is therefore not the accuracy of $G$ over one step but how a $+z$
step and a $-z$ step \emph{compose}:
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
    \text{exact:} \quad & e^{+z}e^{-z} = 1, \\
    \text{implicit Euler:} \quad & \frac{1}{1-z}\cdot\frac{1}{1+z}
      = \frac{1}{1-z^2} = 1 + z^2 + O(z^4) \;>\; 1, \\
    \text{Crank--Nicolson:} \quad &
      \frac{1+z/2}{1-z/2}\cdot\frac{1-z/2}{1+z/2} = 1 .
  \end{aligned}
  \label{eq:rectify}
\end{equation}
Implicit Euler therefore \emph{rectifies} a zero-mean oscillating strain into
one-sided growth of $|\psilev|$ at $O(z^2)$ per $\pm$ pair, purely as an artefact
of the linearization; the Crank--Nicolson $(1,1)$ Pad\'{e} factor is symmetric
and its pair product is exactly $1$, so the leading rectification is
$O(z^3)$. The prediction is unambiguous: replacing Euler by Crank--Nicolson on
$\partial_t\psilev$ removes a real, identified amplification term, and it should
help.

It does not. Reading Table~\ref{tab:coupled-production} across its rows,
Crank--Nicolson is marginally better than Euler at $N=32$ ($-0.949$ against
$-1.000$: the droplet survives with $5\%$ of its volume instead of none) and
\emph{worse} at both finer resolutions --- it diverges at $t=0.0456$ against
Euler's $t=0.0584$ at $N=64$, and at $t=0.0304$ against $t=0.0398$ at $N=128$.
The rectification term is real, and removing it is not worth what removing it
costs: Crank--Nicolson is A-stable but not L-stable, its amplification factor
tends to $-1$ rather than to $0$ for stiff modes, and the grid-scale damping it
gives up is doing more work on this problem than the $O(z^2)$ rectification it
removes.

The backward (BDF2) discretization is the discriminating arm, because it is
second order \emph{and} L-stable: it removes the first-order temporal error
without giving up grid-scale damping. It is the best of the three overall, and
decisively so where it matters most. At $N=32$ it retains the most volume,
$-0.837$ against $-0.949$ for Crank--Nicolson and $-1.000$ for Euler. At $N=64$
all three diverge and the ordering is mixed: backward fails at $t=0.0563$,
between Crank--Nicolson's $t=0.0456$ and Euler's $t=0.0584$, reaching $+30.4$
against $+26.2$ and $+27.7$ --- no scheme is better than the others there in any
useful sense. At $N=128$, the finest and most demanding rung, backward is the
only one of the three that does not diverge at all: it completes the full
$13\,335$ steps to $t=0.1$ where Euler failed at $t=0.0398$ and Crank--Nicolson
at $t=0.0304$. It is also not a fix: the $N=128$
backward run that completes reports
$E^{\mathrm{vol}}_{\mathrm{rel}} = -1.000$, which is to say it integrates
stably to the end time and arrives there with no droplet. Note that the pair
argument \eqref{eq:rectify} does not extend to BDF2 as written --- a two-step
operator has no single-step amplification factor that is a rational function of
$z$ alone --- so its advantage here is attributed to L-stability at second order
rather than to a rectification count.

Raising the time order of the \emph{momentum} equation is a separate question,
and it was measured separately: at $N=64$ with the calibrated capillary step
$\Delta t = 2\times10^{-5}\,\mathrm{s}$, backward on momentum peaks at about
$48$ in the reported error metric and Crank--Nicolson with coefficient $0.9$
reaches $10^{102}$.
Momentum therefore stays on Euler in every arm of
Table~\ref{tab:coupled-production}, and only $\partial_t\psilev$ varies, so the
comparison across rows is a single-variable one.

\paragraph{Conclusion of this subsection.} Three time discretizations of the
level set, spanning first-order L-stable, second-order A-stable and second-order
L-stable, change when the run fails and by how much, and none of them changes
\emph{whether} it fails. The time discretization is not the driver of the coupled
failure.

\subsection{The drain is seeded entirely by curvature error}
\label{sec:coupled-headline}

The remaining hypotheses were two, and they are mutually exclusive. Either the
failure is intrinsic to the source under two-way coupling --- the loop
\eqref{eq:capillaryloop} has gain greater than one for any surface-tension force,
because the source reads a computed velocity and no computed velocity is exact
--- or the loop requires a specific seed, namely the curvature error, and would
be harmless without it. The two differ in what would have to be repaired, so the
experiment that separates them is the one worth running.

It can be run with committed code and no new model, because this case has an
exact discrete curvature. The interface is a circle of radius $R$, so
$\kappa = 1/R = 1000\,\mathrm{m^{-1}}$ everywhere on it, and
\texttt{constantCurvatureSurfaceTension} assembles the force flux with $\kappa$
pinned to that value. With $\kappa$ constant the capillary flux
$\sigma\,\kappa\,\mathrm{snGrad}(\alpha)_f\,|\mathbf{S}_f|$ is
$\sigma\kappa$ times a discrete surface-normal gradient of a cell field, i.e.\ an
exact discrete gradient; it lies in the range of the same operator the pressure
Laplacian inverts, so the pressure equation absorbs it and the residual velocity
is zero to round-off. This arm therefore starves loop
\eqref{eq:capillaryloop} of its seed by construction, while changing nothing else
--- the same solver, the same level-set transport, the same source, the same time
step.

\paragraph{The seed is measurably absent.} Three independent quantities, measured
at $N=32$ with the exact-curvature force, confirm that the arm does what it is
supposed to do rather than merely appearing to:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\toprule

\midrule
interface Courant number, max & $9.1\times10^{-11}$ & not measured \\
$\mathrm{rAU}/\mathrm{rAU}_f$ capillary-pressure velocity residual, max
  & $3.0\times10^{-9}\,\mathrm{m/s}$ & not measured \\
non-absorbable capillary flux fraction $\max|R_f|/\max|\phi_g|$
  & $6.5\times10^{-11}$ & $1.7\times10^{-5}$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The third row is the sharpest of the three and needs its definition.
$\phi_g$ is the capillary face flux delivered to the pressure equation and
$R_f = \phi_g - \phi_{p}$ is what remains of it after the pressure projection has
subtracted the flux of the solved pressure field, evaluated on the faces that
carry the force ($|\mathrm{snGrad}(\alpha)_f| > 0$). It is exactly the part of
the capillary flux that the projection \emph{cannot} absorb, and it is what
\texttt{fvc::reconstruct} turns into a spurious velocity. With the exact
curvature that fraction is $6.5\times10^{-11}$ --- round-off --- against
$1.7\times10^{-5}$ for the production delivery, six orders of magnitude larger.
The interface Courant number of $9.1\times10^{-11}$ says the same thing in the
transport variable: the interface does not move.

\paragraph{The result.} Against that force, the strain-cancelling source does not
drain the droplet at all.

surface-tension force whose spurious current is machine zero by construction, the

\rightarrow a \rightarrow \psilev$ has no other seed: remove the curvature error
and the source is harmless.}}
\medskip

This is the single most consequential measurement in the coupled investigation,
because it moves the defect. The prior record established the \emph{mechanism} of
the failure --- a mesh-locked mode-4 parasitic current at
$3.9\times10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m/s}$ with a circular interface in the sourceless run,
amplified $260\times$ to $1.0\times10^{-1}\,\mathrm{m/s}$ with a squared
interface when the source is on --- and left open whether the source or the
coupling was to blame. It is neither: it is the curvature.

\paragraph{Scope limit, which must be stated with the result.} With $\kappa$
pinned to a constant the velocity is of order $10^{-11}\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and the
interface never moves. That is a degenerate configuration, and it must not be
read as a demonstration that \textbf{R} behaves well under two-way coupling in
general. What it proves is a necessary condition: the loop \emph{requires} the
curvature-error seed, and with the seed removed the source is inert rather than
destructive. What it does not prove is that \textbf{R} is well behaved against an
accurate but imperfect force, because no such force was run here. The correct
reading is that the repair belongs on the force side of the loop --- not that any
particular force performs the repair.

That distinction is not academic, and this codebase already contains the
counterexample. The well-balanced conservative surface-tension model of
Abu-Al-Saud, Popinet and Tchelepi \cite{abualsaud2018} is implemented here as
\texttt{integralSurfaceTension}, and on the $N=64$ lab-frame translating
capillary gate it is dynamically \emph{unstable}: the first-step maximum of
$|\mathbf{v} - \mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{trans}}|$ is
$1.003\times10^{-2}\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and the run terminates with a
floating-point exception at $t = 9.33\times10^{-4}\,\mathrm{s}$, against
$1.09\times10^{-8}\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and a run reaching $t=0.05$ for the
exact-curvature control on the same gate. Better static balance can coexist with
larger dynamic feedback gain. Identifying a force that both reduces the curvature
error and keeps the loop gain below one is open work, and nothing here claims it
is easy.

\subsection{The conservative (divergence-form) source, and a retraction}
\label{sec:rdiv}

\texttt{Rdiv} is a rewrite of the same continuum source in divergence form. With
$\mathbf{w} = (\nhat\cdot\mathbf{v})\,\nhat$ the interface-normal part of the
velocity and $\phi_{\mathbf{w}} = \mathrm{flux}(\mathbf{w})$ its face flux, the
matrix it assembles is
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{div}} \;=\;
    \mathrm{div}(\phi_{\mathbf{w}}, \psilev)
  - \mathrm{div}(\phi, \psilev)
  - \mathrm{Sp}\bigl(\nabla\cdot\mathbf{w} - a,\; \psilev\bigr),
  \label{eq:rdivassembly}
\end{equation}
which at the continuum collapses to $\psilev\,(a - \nabla\cdot\mathbf{v})$, i.e.\
to the algebraic source plus the compressibility correction the base equation
already carries. The motivation was structural: the two divergence terms are
conservative, their global sum telescoping to boundary fluxes, which is a
property the algebraic form lacks.

\paragraph{It had never been run kinematically.} This has to be said before any
result is quoted from it. The verification cases
(\texttt{cases/\{2Dvortex,3Dshear,3Ddeformation\}/system/fvSchemes.template})
carried no \texttt{div(phiW,psi)} entry, so \texttt{fvm::div(phiW,psi)} aborted
at the first time step. \texttt{Rdiv} was therefore not merely untested on the
prescribed-velocity ladders --- it was \emph{unrunnable} on them, and every
statement ever made about it came from coupled runs alone.

\paragraph{Run kinematically, it does not converge.} Study
\texttt{sdplsRdivConv2Dvortex} is the 2D reversed vortex at $T=2$, oscillation
on, $\mathrm{CFL}=0.5$, solver \texttt{leiaLevelSetFoam} with a
\emph{prescribed, divergence-free} velocity: no momentum equation, no pressure
equation, no surface-tension force, no curvature, and therefore no capillary
feedback loop of any kind. Table~\ref{tab:rdiv-kinematic} gives all three
metrics, at the instants the protocol of Section~\ref{par:reversal} assigns them.

that should have removed the drain structurally. \emph{That reading is

(Table~\ref{tab:rdiv-kinematic}: band gradient order $-3.800$, shape $-1.306$,

explained by a defect in the discrete operator \eqref{eq:rdivassembly} itself and

direction --- until it converges kinematically. The row \emph{``conservative

on the same ground.}}

The exact-curvature rows must be labelled for what they are, and the label is not
a formality. With the exact-curvature force the velocity is of order
$10^{-11}\,\mathrm{m/s}$, so $\mathbf{w} = (\nhat\cdot\mathbf{v})\nhat \approx
\mathbf{0}$, $\phi_{\mathbf{w}} \approx 0$, and \emph{every term} of
\eqref{eq:rdivassembly} is approximately zero. Those six cases show that the drain
needs the curvature seed --- which is the same conclusion
Section~\ref{sec:coupled-headline} draws, and it is worth having twice --- but
they \emph{cannot} validate the discretization, because the operator is never
exercised. Reporting them as a decisive result for \texttt{Rdiv}, as was done
when they were first obtained, was an error and is corrected here.

The same caveat applies to the exact-curvature arms of
Table~\ref{tab:coupled-exact}: with the velocity at machine zero the strain $a$
is also at machine zero, so the algebraic source is likewise inert there. The
difference between the two --- and it is the whole difference --- is that
\textbf{R} is independently validated on the kinematic ladders, converging the
band gradient error at $+1.112$ on this very study
(Table~\ref{tab:rdiv-kinematic}) and at $+0.74$ and $+0.668$ on the ladders of
Section~\ref{sec:verification}, whereas \texttt{Rdiv} fails there. A null test is
evidence about the \emph{seed}; it is never evidence about the \emph{operator},
and an operator that has passed no other test has nothing to fall back on.

\paragraph{A hypothesis for the defect, stated as a hypothesis.} Two features of
\eqref{eq:rdivassembly} are candidates, and neither has been tested.

\emph{The implicit coefficient has no sign guarantee.} In the Patankar
decomposition $S = S_C + S_P\,\psilev$ the last term of
\eqref{eq:rdivassembly} contributes
\begin{equation}
  S_P \;=\; a - \nabla\cdot\mathbf{w},
  \label{eq:rdivsp}
\end{equation}
and Patankar's positive-coefficient rule \cite{patankar1980} requires
$S_P \le 0$ for the assembled system to retain its M-matrix property: a positive
$S_P$ subtracts from the diagonal and can destroy diagonal dominance. Nothing in
\eqref{eq:rdivsp} bounds its sign --- wherever the interfacial stretching exceeds
the divergence of the normal velocity, $S_P > 0$. This is precisely the failure
the library's \texttt{strictNegativeSpLinearImplicit} member exists to prevent
for the algebraic source, by taking $S_p = \min(f_{nl},0)$ and moving the
positive part to the explicit side; the divergence form has no such clamp,
because its coefficient is assembled from two fields rather than chosen.

\emph{The coefficient contains second derivatives of $\psilev$.} With
$\mathbf{w} = (\nhat\cdot\mathbf{v})\nhat$ and $\nhat =
\nabla\psilev/\gradpsi$, the term $\nabla\cdot\mathbf{w}$ contains
$\nabla\nhat$, i.e.\ second derivatives of the discrete level set. By the
argument of Section~\ref{sec:sourceerror}, a first derivative of the solution
error behaves like $|e|/h$; a second behaves like $|e|/h^2$, so the coefficient
gets \emph{noisier} as $h$ shrinks. That is consistent with a defect that worsens
under refinement, which is what Table~\ref{tab:rdiv-kinematic} measures, and it
is consistent with the algebraic form --- which differentiates $\psilev$ once,
not twice --- converging on the same ladder.

Both are conjectures. Neither is established by anything measured here, and the
observation that a defect worsens under refinement is consistent with many
causes. The experiment that would test the first is a sign-split variant of
\eqref{eq:rdivassembly} that clamps \eqref{eq:rdivsp} the way
\texttt{strictNegativeSpLinearImplicit} clamps the algebraic coefficient, moving
$\max(S_P,0)\,\psilev^n$ to the explicit side and leaving $\min(S_P,0)$ on the
diagonal, run on the same seven-rung ladder. If the divergence appears at the
same rung with the clamp in place, the sign hypothesis is dead and the second
candidate is the one to pursue. That experiment has now been run, and the
remainder of this section reports it.

\paragraph{The sign hypothesis, tested and falsified.} The experiment named in the
previous paragraph exists in the library as the source type
\texttt{RdivStrictSp}. It leaves the two \texttt{fvm::div} flux terms of
\eqref{eq:rdivassembly} exactly as they are and splits only the implicit
coefficient $f \equiv S_P = a - \nabla\cdot\mathbf{w}$ of \eqref{eq:rdivsp} by
sign, assembling
\begin{equation}
  +\,\mathrm{Su}\bigl(\max(f,0)\,\psilev^{n},\;\psilev\bigr)
  \;+\;\mathrm{Sp}\bigl(\min(f,0),\;\psilev\bigr)
  \qquad\text{in place of}\qquad
  +\,\mathrm{Sp}\bigl(f,\;\psilev\bigr).
  \label{eq:rdivstrictsp}
\end{equation}
That is a single-variable change and it is the clamp exactly: the positive part of
the coefficient is moved to the explicit side at the old time level, the remaining
diagonal contribution is non-positive cell by cell, and Patankar's
positive-coefficient rule \cite{patankar1980} is satisfied everywhere by
construction. Study \texttt{sdplsRdivStrictSpConv2Dvortex} runs it on the same
seven-rung reversed 2D vortex as Table~\ref{tab:rdiv-kinematic}, with all three
arms fitted over identical rungs.

The clamp is not inert, and that has to be established before its failure to help
means anything: a repair that changed nothing because it was never triggered would
test nothing. It is triggered heavily. The solver logs $512$ of $1024$ cells with
$f > 0$ at $N=32$ --- half the domain was on the branch that violates the
positive-coefficient rule, and after the split none of it is. The M-matrix
violation is demonstrably removed.

The divergence barely moves. The fitted band gradient order goes from $-3.800$ to
$-3.554$ and the finest-mesh error from $668.9$ to $635$; the shape order goes
from $-1.306$ to $-0.951$ and the volume order from $-1.544$ to $-0.863$.
\texttt{RdivStrictSp} is marginally less bad on every one of the three metrics and
still strongly divergent on every one of them, on a ladder where the algebraic
source \textbf{R} converges at $+1.112$, $+1.883$ and $+2.090$. Half the domain
changed how its coefficient is delivered, and the fitted band gradient order moved
by $0.246$ while remaining strongly negative. \emph{Loss of diagonal dominance is not the mechanism of
the divergence-form failure.} The first of the two hypotheses of the previous
paragraph is dead, killed by the experiment that paragraph named and in the
direction that paragraph said would kill it.

\paragraph{What that eliminates, and what it does not.} The scope of the
falsification must be stated narrowly, because the sign split does one thing and
not another. It \emph{redistributes} the coefficient $f$ between explicit and
implicit delivery; it does not \emph{reduce} the noise in $\nabla\cdot\mathbf{w}$,
which is the same field, computed the same way, before and after the split. What
is eliminated is therefore the diagonal-dominance mechanism specifically --- the
proposition that the assembled system loses its M-matrix property and that this is
what diverges --- and nothing beyond it. Both previously named suspects were
therefore left live by it, and the second is worth restating more sharply than it
was above. (Both have since been measured directly, and both are bounded and
second-order convergent; the probe that was read as convicting the second is
retracted below, and the mechanism that does account for the divergence is a third
thing neither of them named --- Section~\ref{sec:rdiv-mechanism}.)

\emph{(i) The coefficient carries second derivatives of $\psilev$.} With
$\mathbf{w} = (\nhat\cdot\mathbf{v})\nhat$ and $\nhat = \nabla\psilev/\gradpsi$,
the field $\nabla\cdot\mathbf{w}$ is formed as $\mathrm{fvc::div}(\phi_{\mathbf{w}})$
and contains $\nabla\nhat$, that is, second derivatives of the discrete level set.
By the argument of Section~\ref{sec:sourceerror} a first derivative of the
solution error behaves like $|e|/h$ and a second like $|e|/h^2$, so this
coefficient gets noisier as $h$ shrinks --- the correct qualitative signature for
a defect that worsens under refinement, and one the algebraic form avoids because
it differentiates $\psilev$ once and not twice. The sign split leaves this
untouched: it moves $\max(f,0)$ to the explicit side, and $\max$ of a noisy field
is exactly as noisy as the field.

\emph{(ii) The two flux terms nearly cancel, so the operator is a difference of
large numbers.} This is the sharper statement of the two, and it follows from an
identity rather than from a scaling argument. \texttt{Rdiv} and \textbf{R} are
\emph{continuum-identical}: \eqref{eq:rdivassembly} collapses to
$\psilev\,(a - \nabla\cdot\mathbf{v})$, which is the algebraic source plus the
compressibility correction the base equation already carries. The only structural
difference between the two is therefore not what they represent but how they are
assembled --- \texttt{Rdiv} routes part of the operator through the two
\texttt{fvm::div} flux terms, whose discrete $\psilev\,\nabla\cdot\mathbf{w}$
content must cancel against the $\mathrm{Sp}$ term for the assembly to reproduce
the continuum result, and nothing in the discretization guarantees that it does.
Two large, nearly equal contributions whose difference is the small quantity of
interest is the classical setting for catastrophic cancellation, the residual of
such a cancellation need not converge, and the sign split does not address it
because it does not touch the flux terms at all.

\paragraph{The probe, and the retraction of the closeout drawn from it.}
\label{par:rdiv-productrule}
That measurement was made, and the conclusion drawn from it is withdrawn here, at
the place it was made. The probe is sharper than the assembly-difference residual
first proposed: instead of differencing the whole discrete assembly against the
algebraic form --- a quantity that mixes every discretization in the operator and
so localises nothing --- it registers the discrete failure of the one
\emph{identity} the assembly depends on, the product rule. At the continuum
\begin{equation}
  \nabla\cdot(\mathbf{w}\psilev)
  \;-\; \psilev\,\nabla\cdot\mathbf{w}
  \;-\; \mathbf{w}\cdot\nabla\psilev \;=\; 0
  \label{eq:productrule}
\end{equation}
holds identically, and \texttt{Rdiv} relies on it: the
$\psilev\,\nabla\cdot\mathbf{w}$ content carried implicitly inside
$\mathrm{div}(\phi_{\mathbf{w}},\psilev)$ is precisely what the
$-\mathrm{Sp}(\nabla\cdot\mathbf{w},\psilev)$ term exists to cancel, leaving the
convective part. Discretely nothing enforces \eqref{eq:productrule}:
$\mathrm{fvm::div}(\phi_{\mathbf{w}},\psilev)$ interpolates $\psilev$ to faces
with the $\mathrm{div}(\phi_{\mathbf{w}},\psilev)$ scheme, while
$\mathrm{fvc::div}(\phi_{\mathbf{w}})$ is a plain flux divergence, and the two
need not be consistent with each other.

\emph{What was claimed.} Table~\ref{tab:rdiv-productrule} reported that residual
over the 2D vortex ladder and it was read as a closeout: the identity fails, the
failure grows under refinement ($-0.789$ in the volume-weighted $L_2$ norm,
$-1.585$ in $L_\infty$, and $-0.545$ in the residual taken \emph{relative} to the
reference norm), the residual turns upward at $N=64$ where \texttt{Rdiv}'s own
band gradient error turns, and therefore --- the claim --- the divergence form is
built on a discrete identity that its own discretisation violates, increasingly
under refinement. \emph{That conclusion is withdrawn.} It fails twice over, and
both failures are defects of the norm rather than properties of the operator.

\emph{First, the instant is wrong.} The tabulated residuals are evaluated at
$t=T$ of the probe study. By then $\psilev$ is no longer the sampled exact signed
distance: it is a transported field carrying the solution error of every step
taken to reach that instant, and the divergence form is the arm whose $\psilev$ is
worst polluted there, by orders of magnitude (Table~\ref{tab:rdiv-serial}). A probe
that differentiates $\psilev$ three times over --- once in $\nhat$, once in $\nabla\cdot\mathbf{w}$, once in
$\mathrm{div}(\phi_{\mathbf{w}},\psilev)$ --- and is evaluated on a polluted field
measures the pollution and not the operator. The instant at which the operator is
measurable is the \emph{first assembly}, before any transport has occurred, where
$\psilev$ is still the sampled exact field to $5\times10^{-14}$ and the only thing
the residual can contain is the operator's own inconsistency.

\emph{Second, the norm's domain is wrong, in two separate ways.} Repeated at the
first assembly but retaining a \emph{global} $L_\infty$, the residual is flat at
$\approx 0.44$ on every rung of the ladder --- no order at all, in either
direction --- and its argmax sits at $r/h = 0.71$, the cell that holds the
\emph{circle centre}. That is where the exact signed distance has its kink, and
where the exact divergence of its normal,
$\nabla\cdot\nhat = (d-1)/r$ at distance $r$ from the centre of a sphere in $d$
dimensions, is singular as $r \to 0$. A norm whose extremum is pinned to a fixed
geometric singularity of the \emph{exact} solution reports that singularity at every
resolution and says nothing whatever about the scheme; the flatness at $0.44$ is
the signature. The second defect is that the band must be a fixed \emph{physical}
region. A band defined as a multiple of $h$ --- the sign-change band, or any
$n$-layer band --- shrinks with the mesh, so a norm taken over it is taken over a
different set at every rung and has no convergence order to fit at all. The
corrected measurement therefore uses the fixed annulus
$|\psilev_{\text{exact}}| \le 0.05$, which is the same physical set on every rung
and is never a multiple of $h$.

\emph{The corrected measurement.} Measured that way --- at the first assembly, in
the fixed annulus --- every quantity in the probe is second order, and the two
suspects of the previous paragraph are bounded rather than divergent.

Four readings, and they close the two hypotheses of the previous paragraphs in
opposite order to the one they were proposed in.

\emph{The coefficient is not noisy.} Suspect~(i) was that
$\nabla\cdot\mathbf{w}$ contains second derivatives of the discrete level set and
therefore gets noisier as $h$ shrinks, like $|e|/h^2$. Measured against the exact
coefficient it converges at $+1.972$ in $L_2$ and $+1.728$ in $L_\infty$ --- second
order, and second order in the maximum norm as well, which is the norm a noise
argument would break first. The argument of Section~\ref{sec:sourceerror} that
predicted the noise is not wrong; it is inapplicable at the instant the operator
is assembled for the first time, because there $e = 0$ identically and there is no
solution error for the second derivative to amplify. Whether the coefficient
degrades later, on a polluted $\psilev$, is a separate question that the corrected
probe does not answer and that the failure mechanism of
Section~\ref{sec:rdiv-mechanism} makes unnecessary.

\emph{Neither half of the coefficient is the culprit.} Columns 4 and 5 split
column 3 into the divergence operator's own truncation error, obtained by feeding
it an exactly sampled $\mathbf{w}$ ($+1.970$), and the contribution of the discrete
normal, obtained by varying only $\nhat$ ($+2.018$). Both are second order, and
the normal's contribution is the smaller of the two by a factor of about three at
every rung. There is no hidden first-order term inside the coefficient.

\emph{The assembly is not inconsistent either.} Suspect~(ii) was that the
face-interpolated flux divergence and the plain one fail to cancel. The residual
of \eqref{eq:productrule} over the fixed annulus converges at $+2.005$ in $L_2$
and $+1.915$ in $L_\infty$: the identity is satisfied to second order, which is
the order of the discretisation and therefore all that consistency requires. The
reference norm $\|\nabla\cdot(\mathbf{w}\psilev)\|_{L_2}$ over the same annulus is
mesh-independent --- $3.506$, $3.453$, $3.445$, $3.463$ from $N=32$ to $N=256$ ---
which validates both the band and the reference: the residual is falling like
$h^2$ against a quantity that is not moving, so the convergence is not an artefact
of the differenced terms shrinking.

\emph{And the two are not feeding each other.} The one measurement that the
retracted probe could not make is the single-variable one: substituting the exact
smooth $\mathbf{w}$ for the discrete one in the assembly and changing nothing else
moves the product-rule residual by less than $20\%$ at every instant and every
rung (ratio between $0.81$ and $1.09$). The residual is therefore not driven by
the coefficient at all, which settles the question the retracted passage left open
--- whether the second-derivative noise was feeding the inconsistency --- in the
only way that closes it: neither quantity is large enough to matter.

Both previously named suspects are consequently dead. Each is bounded, each is
second-order convergent, and neither can produce an error that grows by three
orders of magnitude over a fourfold refinement. What diverges is not the
coefficient and not the consistency of the assembly, and the retracted probe was
looking in the wrong place because it was reading a polluted field through a norm
pinned to a geometric singularity. The mechanism is elsewhere, and it is
identified in Section~\ref{sec:rdiv-mechanism}.

One caveat on the residual as constructed survives the retraction and is repeated
because it applies to the corrected table as well: the three terms in
\eqref{eq:productrule} are evaluated with three different discrete operators ---
the $\mathrm{div}(\phi_{\mathbf{w}},\psilev)$ scheme, a plain flux divergence, and
the least-squares gradient the source already uses for $\nhat$ --- so the residual
measures the mutual consistency of that triple rather than the error of any one of
them. That is the right quantity for the question asked, because the assembly's
correctness depends on exactly that mutual consistency, but it should not be read
as an accuracy measurement of any individual operator.

\paragraph{Consequence for the method.} The conservative divergence-form source
has now failed a kinematic gate at both dimensionalities. On the 2D reversed
vortex the band gradient order is $-3.800$, the shape order $-1.306$ and the
volume order $-1.544$ (Table~\ref{tab:rdiv-kinematic}); the 3D shear ladder, which
was outstanding when that table was first reported, has since completed and
reproduces the failure on all three metrics --- band gradient order $-3.383$ at
$t=T/2$, shape $-2.571$ and volume $-3.381$ at $t=T$. Two cases, two
dimensionalities, three metrics each, six divergent orders, with every run
completing and no solver failure anywhere. And the one targeted repair that the
method's own failure analysis named has been implemented, run on the full ladder,
and has not rescued it.

The reporting consequence follows and is stronger than the retraction above. That
retraction withdrew \texttt{Rdiv} as \emph{evidence about the coupled loop}, which
left it standing as a source model whose kinematic behaviour was merely
unfavourable. The 3D result and the falsified sign hypothesis together withdraw it
as a \emph{method}: it is not carried as an available option, no result in this
paper rests on it, and no comparison uses it as a baseline. When this was first
written the withdrawal was \emph{pending an explanation}; it no longer is. The
mechanism has since been identified and measured in the assembly
(Section~\ref{sec:rdiv-mechanism}), and the same section establishes that removing
it would recover stability without recovering accuracy, so the withdrawal now rests
on a structural argument rather than on an outstanding question. Recording it as
withdrawn rather than deleting it is deliberate: the operator, the two hypotheses,
the falsification of one of them, the measurement that falsified it, the probe that
was misread and its retraction, and the mechanism that finally accounts for the
divergence are the useful content, and a reader who proposes a divergence-form
source in future should be able to find out what happened to this one.

\subsection{The mechanism: a difference of two upwind convection operators}
\label{sec:rdiv-mechanism}

With both named suspects measured and bounded, the mechanism has to be somewhere
neither probe looked, and it is: not in any coefficient, and not in the
consistency of the assembly, but in the \emph{sign pattern of the matrix} that
\eqref{eq:rdivassembly} produces once \texttt{fvMatrix::operator==} has subtracted
it from the left-hand side.

\paragraph{The assembled matrix is a difference of two convection operators.}
\texttt{fvMatrix::operator==} subtracts the right-hand matrix termwise
(Section~\ref{sec:defects}), so the system actually solved is the base transport
operator minus \eqref{eq:rdivassembly}:
\begin{equation}
  \underbrace{\mathrm{ddt}(\psilev)}_{\text{time}}
  \;+\; 2\,\mathrm{div}(\phi,\psilev)
  \;-\; \mathrm{div}(\phi_{\mathbf{w}},\psilev)
  \;+\; (\text{diagonal terms}) \;=\; 0 .
  \label{eq:rdivmatrix}
\end{equation}
The convection is not one upwind operator with a modified flux. It is a
\emph{difference of two} upwind operators, on two different fluxes, and that
difference is the entire structural distance between \texttt{Rdiv} and the
algebraic source \textbf{R}, which assembles a single $\mathrm{fvm::div}(\phi,
\psilev)$ and carries its source on the diagonal.

\paragraph{Why the difference loses the sign pattern.} For a single
$\mathrm{fvm::div}$ with upwind interpolation the off-diagonal coefficients are
non-positive \emph{whatever the sign of the face flux}: on the internal face $f$
between owner $P$ and neighbour $N$ the upper coefficient is $\min(\phi_f,0)$ and
the lower is $-\max(\phi_f,0)$, and exactly one of the two is nonzero. That is the
property on which the discrete maximum principle of an upwind convection matrix
rests. Subtracting such an operator flips the signs of its off-diagonals, and the
composite of \eqref{eq:rdivmatrix} inherits the flipped ones. On a face where
$\mathrm{sign}(\phi_f) \neq \mathrm{sign}(\phi_{\mathbf{w},f})$ the two operators
upwind from \emph{opposite} cells, nothing overlaps, and the composite
off-diagonal is exactly
\begin{equation}
  2\min(\phi_f,0) - \min(\phi_{\mathbf{w},f},0) \;=\; +\,|\phi_{\mathbf{w},f}|
  \qquad\text{(for } \phi_f > 0 > \phi_{\mathbf{w},f}\text{)},
  \label{eq:posoffdiag}
\end{equation}
and symmetrically on the lower coefficient for the opposite mismatch. Sign
agreement is not sufficient either: with both fluxes positive the lower
coefficient is $-2\max(\phi_f,0) + \max(\phi_{\mathbf{w},f},0) = \phi_{\mathbf{w},f}
- 2\phi_f$, which is positive wherever
$\phi_{\mathbf{w},f} > 2\phi_f$. Both channels are populated in practice, as the
census below shows. The tangential part of $\mathbf{v}$ that
$\mathbf{w} = (\nhat\cdot\mathbf{v})\nhat$ discards is exactly what makes the two
fluxes disagree, and on a vortex that is not a rare event but a bulk property of
the two fields, as the census immediately below measures it to be.

\paragraph{The census, measured in the assembly.} The probe counts the mismatch
and the resulting coefficients inside the assembled matrix rather than inferring
them. On the 2D vortex at $N=64$ --- $4096$ cells, $8064$ internal faces, hence
$16128$ off-diagonal coefficients:

equal to about $0.38\,\mathrm{CFL}$ --- is \emph{mesh-independent}. And no row
loses diagonal dominance outright, which is why every one of these runs completes
and every linear solve converges, exactly as Table~\ref{tab:rdiv-kinematic}
reports.

What is lost is not solvability but the discrete maximum principle. A positive
off-diagonal means the update of a cell is no longer a convex combination of its
neighbours' values, so the scheme can manufacture a new extremum in $\psilev$
where the exact solution has none, and a fresh one at every step. The relative
strength of that mechanism does not fall under refinement --- it is fixed at
$0.38\,\mathrm{CFL}$ --- while the number of steps taken to reach a fixed end time
grows like $1/h$. A per-step gain that does not shrink, applied a number of times
that grows without bound, is an amplification in time; it is not a consistency
error, and it has no convergence order.

\paragraph{Why \textbf{R} is immune, and why \texttt{RdivStrictSp} was bound to
change nothing.} \textbf{R} assembles one $\mathrm{fvm::div}(\phi,\psilev)$ and
adds a diagonal coefficient. Its off-diagonals are $\min(\phi_f,0)$ and
$-\max(\phi_f,0)$ in every row, non-positive by construction, whatever the flow
does; nothing in the source can change them, because the source is not an
operator on $\psilev$'s neighbours. That is the whole of the structural difference
between the two forms, and it now accounts for the whole of the difference in
behaviour.

It also explains, retrospectively and exactly, why \texttt{RdivStrictSp}
\eqref{eq:rdivstrictsp} moved the fitted order from $-3.800$ to $-3.554$ and
nothing more. An M-matrix requires two things: non-positive off-diagonals, and a
diagonal that dominates them. The sign split addresses the \emph{diagonal},
clamping the algebraic coefficient $S_P = a - \nabla\cdot\mathbf{w}$ to be
non-positive, and it does so demonstrably --- half the domain was on the violating
branch at $N=32$ and after the split none of it is. The positive off-diagonals of
\eqref{eq:posoffdiag} are not in $S_P$. They are in the operator pair, which
\texttt{RdivStrictSp} leaves untouched by construction, and which is where the
sign violation that matters lives. The earlier verdict --- \emph{loss of diagonal
dominance is not the mechanism} --- stands as written and is now sharpened: the
diagonal was never the problem, and the experiment that tested it could not have
found the defect it was aimed at.

\paragraph{The 1D corroboration, where the mismatch is zero by construction.} The
uniaxial-stretch gate (\texttt{sdpls1Dstretch}) supplies the control the argument
needs, because on it the mechanism is switched off by the geometry rather than by
a code change. There $\mathbf{v}$ is entirely normal to the interface, so
$\mathbf{w} = (\nhat\cdot\mathbf{v})\nhat = \mathbf{v}$ and
$\phi_{\mathbf{w}} = \phi$ identically: the two flux terms of
\eqref{eq:rdivassembly} are the same operator, and the composite of
\eqref{eq:rdivmatrix} collapses back to a single upwind $\mathrm{fvm::div}$. The
probe confirms it in the assembly rather than on paper --- zero sign-mismatched
faces and zero positive off-diagonal coefficients --- and \texttt{Rdiv} is then
exact on that gate: it reports a band mean $\mathrm{d}\psilev/\mathrm{d}x$ of
$0.367872$ at $t=1$, matching the sourceless arm to the digit, which is the right
answer because in a pure uniaxial stretch the coefficient
$\nabla\cdot\mathbf{w} - a$ of \eqref{eq:rdivsp} vanishes and \texttt{Rdiv}'s
continuum equation \emph{is} the sourceless one. Same operator, same code path,
same source model: with $\phi_{\mathbf{w}} = \phi$ it is exact, and it is only
where the two fluxes differ that it diverges.

\paragraph{The blend sweep, and the property that cannot be recovered.} The
mechanism identifies a repair --- assemble the composite so that its off-diagonals
keep their sign --- and the next measurement establishes that the repair cannot
succeed, for a reason that has nothing to do with stability. The starting point is
that the tangential part of $\mathbf{v}$ does no work on a level set:
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{v}\cdot\nabla\psilev
  \;=\; \mathbf{w}\cdot\nabla\psilev
  \;=\; (\nhat\cdot\mathbf{v})\,\gradpsi ,
  \label{eq:tangentialnowork}
\end{equation}
because $\nabla\psilev$ is parallel to $\nhat$. Every member of the one-parameter
family of convecting fluxes
\begin{equation}
  \phi_\lambda \;=\; \lambda\,\phi \;+\; (1-\lambda)\,\phi_{\mathbf{w}}
  \label{eq:blend}
\end{equation}
is therefore \emph{continuum-equivalent}: they all transport the level set
identically in the exact equation, and any ranking between them is purely a
property of the discretisation. Three members are named. $\lambda = 0$ is
transport on the normal flux alone, which is what the conservative term of
\eqref{eq:rdivassembly} routes the operator through; $\lambda = 2$ is the
$2\mathbf{v}-\mathbf{w}$ composite that \eqref{eq:rdivmatrix} actually assembles;
and $\lambda = 1$ is $\phi$ itself, which is exactly the flux the algebraic source
\textbf{R} convects on. Run with a \emph{single} upwind operator throughout --- so
that the maximum-principle defect is absent by construction and the only variable
is the choice of convecting flux --- on the 2D reversed vortex, $T=2$, serial,
three rungs $N = 32, 64, 128$:

Section~\ref{sec:rdiv-mechanism} is the difference between those two rows, and

\medskip
\noindent\fbox{\parbox{0.97\linewidth}{%
\textbf{The divergence form cannot hold three properties at once.} They are:
\emph{(a)} a single upwind convection operator, hence off-diagonals $\le 0$ in
every row and a discrete maximum principle; \emph{(b)} conservation, which the
form obtains precisely by routing the operator through
$\mathrm{div}(\phi_{\mathbf{w}},\cdot)$ so that the flux telescopes; and
\emph{(c)} the optimal convecting flux, which \eqref{eq:blend} measures to be
$\lambda = 1$, i.e.\ $\mathbf{v}$ itself. Property~(b) forces $\lambda \neq 1$ and
so contradicts~(c); holding~(a) and~(c) together means $\lambda = 1$, at which
point the form \emph{is} the algebraic source \textbf{R} and the conservation is
gone. Repairing the maximum principle --- assembling the composite so that its
off-diagonals keep their sign --- would therefore recover \emph{stability} and
could not recover \emph{accuracy}, because the accuracy deficit at $\lambda \neq
1$ is the price of the conservation the form exists to provide.
\texttt{Rdiv} stays withdrawn, now on a structural argument rather than pending an
explanation.}}
\medskip

That closes the divergence-form line. The mechanism is identified and measured,
the repair its own failure analysis named is falsified, the two suspects that
survived that falsification are measured bounded and second-order
(Table~\ref{tab:rdiv-coefficient}), and the property the form was built to deliver
is shown to be in structural conflict with the property the level set actually
needs from the convecting flux. What is \emph{not} established, and is left open
in Section~\ref{sec:conclusions-open}, is whether any conservative source form can
hold all three at once by some route other than a blended flux; no such form is
proposed here.

\subsection{Two invariants, and the measurement that separates them}
\label{sec:budget}

A conservative source form conserves $\int\psilev\,\mathrm dV$. The quantity the
droplet studies measure is $\mathrm{vol}\{\psilev < 0\}$. These are different
functionals of the same field, and the confusion between them is what motivated
\eqref{eq:rdivassembly} in the first place. The per-step diagnostic
\texttt{psiConservationCSV} now emits both in the same row, together with a
term-by-term budget of the discrete transport equation
\begin{equation}
  \sum_c \bigl(\psilev^{n+1}_c - \psilev^{n}_c\bigr)\,|\Omega_c|
  \;=\; \underbrace{-\Delta t \!\!\sum_{f \in \partial\Omega}\!\! \phi_f\psilev_f}_{\text{flux}}
  \;+\; \underbrace{\Delta t \sum_c (\nabla\cdot\phi)_c\,\psilev_c\,|\Omega_c|}_{\text{the non-flux } \mathrm{Sp} \text{ term}}
  \;+\; \underbrace{\Delta t \sum_c S_c\,|\Omega_c|}_{\text{source}},
  \label{eq:psibudget}
\end{equation}
in which the flux term telescopes over interior and processor faces and survives
only on the non-coupled boundary --- which, on this closed case with
\texttt{noSlip} walls, is identically zero.

Three conclusions follow from Table~\ref{tab:psibudget}, and all three close
questions that had been open.

\emph{There is no flux-consistency bug.} With the source off,
$\int\psilev\,\mathrm dV$ is conserved to machine precision ---
$-3.2\times10^{-12}$ at $N=32$ and $+2.9\times10^{-13}$ at $N=64$, relative ---
so the assembled transport operator conserves what it is supposed to conserve and
the drain cannot be attributed to a defect in the flux assembly.

\emph{The non-flux term costs nothing measurable.} The
$-$\texttt{fvm::Sp}$(\nabla\cdot\phi,\psilev)$ term of the advective
(Hamilton--Jacobi) form does not telescope, and the concern was that it breaks
conservation at a level that matters. It does not: its accumulated contribution
is between $10^{-22}$ and $10^{-26}$ in every row, which is to say the
intermediate PIMPLE fluxes on this case are discretely solenoidal to round-off.
The earlier worry was wrong, and it can be retired.

\emph{The two invariants are decoupled, by four orders of magnitude, and this is
now measured rather than argued.} At $N=32$ the \textbf{R} arm drifts
$\int\psilev\,\mathrm dV$ by only $-2.2\times10^{-4}$ while destroying the
droplet entirely, $E^{\mathrm{vol}}_{\mathrm{rel}} = -1.000$. Conversely
\texttt{Rdiv} conserves $\int\psilev\,\mathrm dV$ \emph{better} than the
algebraic form does --- $-1.3\times10^{-6}$ at $N=32$, as a flux form should ---
while still losing about $1\%$ of the phase volume, and at $N=64$ it drifts
$\int\psilev\,\mathrm dV$ by $-1.1\times10^{-2}$ and diverges. Conservation of
$\int\psilev\,\mathrm dV$ was never evidence about $\mathrm{vol}\{\psilev<0\}$:
$\psilev$ can be conserved globally while its zero contour sweeps through cells.
The design argument that a flux form ``cannot drain'' the droplet is retracted on
this measurement, as it already is in the source documentation, and the
diagnostic now emits both integrals in the same row so that no future claim can
be built on one of them alone.

One column of the budget is deliberately absent. \texttt{BUDGET\_SOURCE} is
\texttt{NaN} for \texttt{Rdiv}, because the decomposition \eqref{eq:psibudget}
assumes an algebraic $S = f_{nl}\,\psilev$ and the divergence form is not one; the
diagnostic flags the row rather than reporting a number it cannot compute. The
$N=64$ \texttt{Rdiv} row's \texttt{BUDGET\_DIVPHI} is likewise not reported here
because that run diverged.

\section{Two models measured in response, and what they do not fix}
\label{sec:remedies}

Two models were implemented and measured in response to the coupled result. Both
are useful; neither addresses the failure of Section~\ref{sec:coupled}, and
saying so precisely is more informative than either promoting or discarding them.

\subsection{Exact-in-time delivery of the source: the exponential linearization}
\label{sec:exp}

The rectification identified in \eqref{eq:rectify} is a property of the rational
one-step factor $G(z) = 1/(1-z)$, not of the continuum source. With the
coefficient frozen at the old time level --- which is what every member of this
hierarchy does, since $f_{nl}$ is evaluated from $\psilev^n$ and $\mathbf{v}$
before the matrix is assembled --- the source is the linear scalar ordinary
differential equation $\mathrm d\psilev/\mathrm dt = a\,\psilev$, whose exact
one-step solution is the multiplicative integrating factor $\psilev^{n+1} =
\psilev^n e^{z}$. The three existing linearizations are rational approximations
of that same factor. The \texttt{exponential} member delivers it directly, by
setting
\begin{equation}
  S_p = 0, \qquad
  S_c = \psilev^n\,\frac{\exp(a\,\Delta t) - 1}{\Delta t},
  \label{eq:expsource}
\end{equation}
so that, paired with implicit Euler on $\partial_t\psilev$, the assembled step
returns $\psilev^{n+1} = \psilev^n \exp(a\Delta t)$ exactly, at any $\Delta t$.
The unit test \texttt{leiaTestSdplsSource} asserts precisely that: the one-step
update matches the closed form to $1.11\times10^{-16}$, with $89$ assertions
passed and $0$ failed. In practice $S_c$ is formed as
$\psilev^n a\,\varphi_1(z)$ with $\varphi_1(z) = (e^z-1)/z$, so the $z\to0$ limit
is exactly $a\,\psilev^n$ in floating point rather than a cancellation of two
nearly equal numbers.

Two consequences are worth separating, because one of them is a real property and
the other is a measurement that came back negative on two of the three metrics.

\paragraph{What it gives.} Exactness in time, and unconditional sign
preservation. $\exp(z)$ is strictly positive and finite for every real $z$,
whereas $1+z$ changes sign at $z=-1$ and $1/(1-z)$ has a pole at $z=1$. A
strictly positive multiplicative factor cannot flip the sign of $\psilev$ in any
cell, so the topology of the discrete zero contour is preserved at any time step
--- $\psilev G = 0 \iff \psilev = 0$ for $G>0$. That is the SDPLS design property
itself, and this member is the only linearization that holds it unconditionally.
It also removes the $\pm z$ rectification identically rather than to leading
order, because $e^{+z}e^{-z}=1$ is an algebraic identity and not a truncation.
The exactness is conditioned on $\partial_t\psilev$ being discretized with Euler;
with Crank--Nicolson the exponential coefficient is still delivered but the pair
no longer cancels exactly. The price is that a multiplicative factor has no exact
representation as a linear diagonal coefficient --- $S_p$ can only produce
$1/(1-S_p\Delta t)$ --- so the whole term goes onto $S_c$ and the member has no
diagonal support: the M-matrix property of the assembled system must come from
the transport operator alone \cite{patankar1980}.

\paragraph{The first measurement of it was two metrics wide, and was re-run for
that reason.} Study \texttt{sdplsExpSource2Dvortex} ran the 2D vortex at $T=1$
with the oscillation \emph{off}, $\mathrm{CFL}=0.5$, over the seven-rung ladder
$N=32\ldots256$, with \textbf{R} delivered by \texttt{simpleLinearImplicit} and by
\texttt{exponential}. It reported band gradient orders of $+1.343$ against
$+1.331$ and volume orders of $+1.925$ against $+2.409$ at $t=T$ and $+3.391$
against $+2.103$ at $t=T/2$, and it reported no shape order at all. The reason is
structural rather than accidental: the shape error is defined against the initial
condition, which is the correct answer only where the flow returns the interface
to it, so a configuration with the oscillation switched off cannot populate that
column at any resolution. The protocol of this paper is three metrics wide, the
missing one was the third, and --- as the re-run shows --- it is the one in which
the two deliveries actually differ. The study was therefore repeated under the
reversed protocol of Section~\ref{par:reversal}:
\texttt{sdplsExpSource2DvortexRev}, $T=2$, oscillation on, $\mathrm{CFL}=0.5$,
the same seven rungs $N = 32\ldots256$ on four MPI ranks, solver
\texttt{leiaLevelSetFoam}, band gradient error read at $t=T/2$ and shape and
volume error at $t=T$. Table~\ref{tab:expsource} is that measurement.

\paragraph{What it does not give: any change in the gradient order.} The gradient
column is flat, $+1.109$ against $+1.112$ --- a difference of $0.003$, which is
inside the spread of a seven-point least-squares fit and is the same size as the
$0.003$ that separates three different gradient schemes for the normal in
Section~\ref{sec:sourceerror}. That is what the error estimate of that section
predicts, and it is worth stating as a confirmation rather than as a
disappointment. The rectification the exponential factor removes is a
\emph{temporal} artefact, of size $O(z^2)$ per $\pm$ pair with $z = a\Delta t$.
The order loss established in Section~\ref{sec:sourceerror} is \emph{spatial}, and
it comes from $(\nabla e)_h = O(h^{p-1})$ --- a term no time discretization
touches. A temporal correction cannot repair a spatial order, and with the two
deliveries differing by an exact integrating factor against a rational
approximation of it, this is the most direct test of that statement available.

\paragraph{What it costs: shape and volume order.} The two remaining columns are
not flat, and they move the same way. The shape order at $t=T$ falls from $+1.883$
to $+1.256$, and the volume order at $t=T$ from $+2.090$ to $+1.718$; the volume
order at $t=T/2$ falls in the same direction and by more, $+3.294$ to $+2.398$.
Three metrics, one direction: the exactness in time is paid for in the accuracy of
the zero contour's position and of the phase volume it encloses. The earlier
two-metric comparison could not see this, because the one column that separates
the deliveries unambiguously is the one it did not have and the two volume
instants it did have moved in opposite directions.

The mechanism is visible in \eqref{eq:expsource} itself and does not require a
further experiment to name. The exponential member sets $S_p = 0$ and puts the
entire source on $S_c$, evaluated from $\psilev^n$ before the matrix is assembled:
the source is delivered \emph{explicitly}. The rational members do not. With
\texttt{simpleLinearImplicit} the coefficient $f_{nl}$ sits on the diagonal of the
same matrix that carries the convection, so within one linear solve the source and
the transport operator act on the \emph{same} $\psilev^{n+1}$ and are coupled;
with the exponential delivery the transport operator is handed a fixed
right-hand-side field and the two act in sequence. Exactness of the source factor
\emph{in isolation} is therefore bought by giving up the implicit coupling between
the source and the transport operator, and it is precisely the metrics that
measure where the zero contour ends up --- shape and volume --- that record the
loss, while the metric that measures the strain balance the source was built to
enforce --- the band gradient --- does not.

\paragraph{Delivering the same factor implicitly: \texttt{exponentialImplicit}.}
\label{par:expimplicit}
The previous paragraph attributes the shape and volume cost to the explicit
delivery rather than to the exponential factor, and that attribution was an
argument from the structure of \eqref{eq:expsource} until a member existed that
carries the factor \emph{and} keeps its diagonal support. One does now, and
constructing it is a two-line derivation. Require the step to reproduce the exact
one-step solution $\psilev^{n+1} = \psilev^n e^{z}$, $z \equiv f_{nl}\Delta t$, but
deliver it through the diagonal, i.e.\ demand
$(\psilev^{n+1} - \psilev^{n})/\Delta t = S_p\,\psilev^{n+1}$ with the
\emph{new} time level on the right. Substituting the required $\psilev^{n+1}$ and
solving for $S_p$ gives $1 - S_p\Delta t = e^{-z}$, hence
\begin{equation}
  S_p \;=\; \frac{1 - e^{-z}}{\Delta t}, \qquad S_c \;=\; 0,
  \qquad z \;=\; f_{nl}\,\Delta t .
  \label{eq:expimplicit}
\end{equation}
The coefficient is \emph{not} $(e^{z}-1)/\Delta t$, and the distinction is not
cosmetic. That is the \emph{explicit} coefficient of \eqref{eq:expsource}, which is
correct on $S_c$ and wrong on $S_p$: used implicitly it gives
$\psilev^{n+1} = \psilev^{n}/(2 - e^{z})$, which has a pole at $z = \ln 2$ and
returns a \emph{negative} $\psilev$ for every $z$ beyond it --- a sign flip of the
level set in the cell, which is the one thing the source is built never to do.
Equation~\eqref{eq:expimplicit} has neither defect: $1 - e^{-z} < 1$ for every
real $z$, so $1 - S_p\Delta t = e^{-z}$ is strictly positive and no pole exists.

Two results, and they point in opposite directions, which is why both are
reported.

\emph{The implicit exponential dominates the explicit one on the interface
metrics, by a factor of $770$ on both.} The interface position error falls from
$-1.60\times10^{-1}$ cell widths to $+2.08\times10^{-4}$, and the relative volume
error from $-3.74\times10^{-3}$ to $+4.85\times10^{-6}$ --- ratios of $769$ and
$771$. The two members deliver the \emph{same} multiplicative factor to the same
precision; the entire difference is that one of them delivers it on the diagonal
and the other on the right-hand side. The 2D shape and volume repair and the 1D
interface repair are the same effect measured two ways.

\emph{And \texttt{simpleLinearImplicit} is exact on this gate, for a structural
reason that neither exponential member can reproduce.} Its band gradient error is
$7.19\times10^{-12}$ --- machine precision, not a small number --- against
$7.5\times10^{-3}$ for both exponential members. The reason is a property of this
source and this exact solution rather than of the integrator. The closed form
$\psilev = x - x_i(t)$ satisfies
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{v}\cdot\nabla\psilev \;=\; a\,\psilev
  \label{eq:steadybalance}
\end{equation}
pointwise: the transport term and the source balance each other exactly and at
every instant, so the exact solution is a \emph{steady balance} rather than a
trajectory that has to be integrated. \texttt{simpleLinearImplicit} takes
$S_p = f_{nl}$, which \emph{is} the continuum coefficient $a$, so the discrete
system enforces \eqref{eq:steadybalance} at time level $n+1$ to machine precision
whatever $\Delta t$ is. Every member that integrates the source ODE more
accurately perturbs that coefficient instead of matching it:
\eqref{eq:expimplicit} expands as
\begin{equation}
  \frac{1 - e^{-z}}{\Delta t} \;=\; a - \frac{a^{2}\Delta t}{2} + O(a^{3}\Delta t^{2}),
  \label{eq:effectivecoeff}
\end{equation}
so the effective coefficient is not $a$, the balance \eqref{eq:steadybalance} is
broken at $O(a^{2}\Delta t)$, and that is the $7.5\times10^{-3}$ that both
exponential members show. \emph{For this source, matching the continuum
coefficient matters more than integrating the source ODE exactly.} The statement
is only visible on a gate with a closed-form steady balance --- on the 2D ladder
the three deliveries agree to $0.03$ in gradient order --- and it is a property of
the SDPLS source specifically, whose whole purpose is to cancel a term rather than
to evolve one.

\paragraph{What the implicit exponential therefore buys: robustness, provably.}
Not accuracy, on either measurement above: on the 2D ladder it matches
\texttt{simpleLinearImplicit} and on the 1D gate it is nine orders of magnitude
behind it in the gradient. What it buys is three guarantees that
\texttt{simpleLinearImplicit} does not carry, each of them a property of
\eqref{eq:expimplicit} rather than a measurement.

\emph{The assembled diagonal cannot change sign.} With
\eqref{eq:expimplicit} the diagonal contribution of the time derivative and the
source together is $1/\Delta t - S_p = e^{-z}/\Delta t$, strictly positive for
every finite $z$ and for either sign of $f_{nl}$.
\texttt{simpleLinearImplicit} carries $1/\Delta t - f_{nl}$ instead, which goes
negative as soon as $f_{nl}\Delta t > 1$; that is the origin of the
$|f_{nl}|\Delta t < 1$ restriction the method as originally stated accepts. The
implicit exponential removes it by construction rather than by clipping the
coefficient, which is what \texttt{strictNegativeSpLinearImplicit} does at the
cost of moving part of the source to the explicit side.

\emph{No iterate is ever multiplied by a factor.} Because $S_c = 0$, the
right-hand side carries no $\psilev$-dependent term at all, so the Picard loop
that relinearizes $f_{nl}$ at every deferred-correction pass cannot converge to the
spurious fixed point
$\psilev^{n}/(2-e^{z})$ that the explicit coefficient used implicitly would
produce. The failure mode is absent rather than avoided.

\emph{The zero contour cannot change topology.} $e^{-z} > 0$ for every real $z$,
so the update is a strictly positive multiplicative factor on $\psilev$ in every
cell, and a strictly positive factor cannot flip a sign. No cell can be carried
across the interface by the source, at any $\Delta t$, on any mesh. This is the
same unconditional guarantee the explicit \texttt{exponential} member holds ---
and it is now available without the shape and volume cost that member pays for it.

\paragraph{The revised verdict, over the whole family.} The exponential member was
first reported here as neutral: identical gradient order, no order gain in either
direction, and value residing in exactness and sign preservation. With the shape
column measured that reading did not survive --- the \emph{explicit} exponential
costs shape and volume order --- and with \eqref{eq:expimplicit} measured the
revision goes one step further, because the cost turns out not to be a property of
the exponential factor at all. Three statements now stand, and they should be read
together.

\emph{The explicit \texttt{exponential} member is superseded.} It is dominated by
\texttt{exponentialImplicit} on every metric measured on both configurations: on
the serial 2D ladder in shape ($+1.360$ against $+1.603$) and volume ($+1.439$
against $+1.653$) at equal gradient order (Table~\ref{tab:expimplicit}), and on
the 1D gate by a factor of $770$ in both interface position and phase volume
(Table~\ref{tab:expimplicit1d}). The unconditional sign preservation that was its
sole retained justification --- $\exp(z) > 0$ for every real $z$, so a strictly
positive multiplicative factor cannot flip the sign of $\psilev$ in any cell and
the topology of the discrete zero contour survives any $\Delta t$ --- is held by
the implicit member as well, through $1 - S_p\Delta t = e^{-z} > 0$. There is no
longer a configuration in which the explicit delivery is the right choice, and the
attribution it was retained under is now confirmed by ablation rather than argued
from structure: what cost the shape and volume order was $S_p = 0$, not $e^{z}$.

\emph{\texttt{simpleLinearImplicit} remains the most accurate delivery where its
restriction holds.} It matches \texttt{exponentialImplicit} to $0.004$, $0.003$ and
$0.015$ in gradient, shape and volume order on the 2D ladder --- indistinguishable
within a three-point fit --- and it is \emph{exact} on the 1D gate,
$7.19\times10^{-12}$ against $7.49\times10^{-3}$, because $S_p = f_{nl}$ reproduces
the continuum coefficient and thereby enforces the steady balance
\eqref{eq:steadybalance} at time level $n+1$ whatever $\Delta t$ is. Its price is
the diagonal $1/\Delta t - f_{nl}$, which changes sign once $f_{nl}\Delta t > 1$.

\emph{\texttt{exponentialImplicit} is the delivery to select where that restriction
cannot be guaranteed.} It buys robustness rather than accuracy: a diagonal
$e^{-z}/\Delta t$ that is strictly positive for every finite $z$, so the
$|f_{nl}|\Delta t < 1$ restriction is removed by construction rather than by
clipping the coefficient; $S_c = 0$, so no iterate is multiplied by a factor and the
spurious Picard fixed point $\psilev^n/(2-e^{z})$ cannot arise; and unconditional
sign preservation. It pays for none of that in the kinematic metrics, which is what
separates it from the member it replaces. Table~\ref{tab:expimplicit} and
Table~\ref{tab:expimplicit1d} are the price list, and the price is zero.

\subsection{Global volume correction by a uniform level-set shift}
\label{sec:volcorr}

The second model is the global volume correction of Song, Long, Cai and Pan
\cite{song2025}, implemented as \texttt{newtonShiftVolumeCorrection}. A single
scalar $\varepsilon$, one number for the whole mesh, is added to the level set,
\begin{equation}
  \psilev \;\leftarrow\; \psilev + \varepsilon,
  \qquad
  \varepsilon \;\text{ the root of }\;
  f(\varepsilon) = \sum_c \alpha_c(\psilev + \varepsilon)\,|\Omega_c| - V_{\text{target}},
  \label{eq:newtonshift}
\end{equation}
found by Newton--Raphson from $\varepsilon_0 = 0$ and safeguarded by bisection.
The reason this model belongs in a paper about the signed-distance property is
that $\varepsilon$ is spatially \emph{constant}, so
$\nabla(\psilev + \varepsilon) = \nabla\psilev$ --- and not only in the
continuum. The discrete Gauss gradient of a cell is
$|\Omega_c|^{-1}\sum_f \mathbf{S}_f\psilev_f$ with $\sum_f \mathbf{S}_f =
\mathbf{0}$ for a closed cell, and the least-squares gradient is a linear
combination of differences $\psilev_N - \psilev_P$; both are invariant under a
uniform shift bit for bit, provided the boundary values shift with the interior,
which \texttt{zeroGradient} does. The correction therefore restores the phase
volume \emph{without spending} the quantity this paper is about: $\gradpsi$ is
untouched, and neither redistancing trigger --- both are functions of $\gradpsi$
alone --- can be provoked by it.

\paragraph{The finding is a crossover.} It helps where the uncorrected volume
error is large and hurts where it is already small.

$-0.161$, $+0.679$ against $+0.707$, $-0.116$ against $-0.107$, $+1.112$ against

not touch the signed-distance property in either direction, which is the claim
the uniform shift makes and the first thing that had to be checked. The
uncorrected \textbf{R} arm of the 2D block reproduces $+1.112$, $+1.883$ and
$+2.090$ --- gradient, shape and volume --- digit for digit against the
exponential study of Section~\ref{sec:exp} (Table~\ref{tab:expsource}) and against
the \textbf{R} arm of the divergence-form study of Section~\ref{sec:rdiv}
(Table~\ref{tab:rdiv-kinematic}), and the sourceless arm likewise reproduces
$-0.116$, $+2.134$ and $+2.733$ against the latter. Three independently configured
studies pin the same case, the same ladder and the same linearization, and agree
on all three metrics; that is the cross-check that they are measuring the same
thing and not three similar things.

Where the uncorrected volume error is large, the correction converts it into
shape accuracy. On 3D shear with the source, whose volume order is a poor $+0.230$,
adding the shift raises the \emph{shape} order from $+0.916$ to $+1.293$ with the
gradient order unchanged. That is the intended trade working in the intended
direction: the volume that was being lost was being lost as a systematic
interface displacement, and restoring it moves the interface back.

Where the uncorrected volume error is already small, the correction is a net
loss. The sourceless 3D shear arm converges in volume at $+3.928$ without help,
and the shift drops it to $+1.050$ while also lowering the shape order from
$+1.866$ to $+1.745$. The sourceless 2D arm says the same thing more sharply
still: $+2.733$ without the shift, and with it a fitted $6.432$ that is not a rate
at all but the signature of an error pinned at the correction's tolerance floor.
A correction that drives an already-converging error down to a fixed floor
destroys its convergence \emph{rate} while improving its absolute value, and on an
arm that was converging at nearly fourth order the rate is the thing worth having.

\paragraph{The 2D block sharpens the crossover, and supplies the piece that was
missing.} With the shape column populated, the 2D vortex is not a second copy of
the 3D result but its mirror image, and the pair is one mechanism rather than two
observations. On the 2D source arm the correction \emph{does} trade shape for
volume: the shape order falls from $+1.883$ to $+1.671$, while the volume order
goes from $+2.090$ to a fitted $0.835$ that is at or near the same tolerance floor
and must not be read as a rate. Shape degrading under the correction is exactly
the behaviour the model was feared to have, and it is exactly what did \emph{not}
happen on 3D shear, where the same correction applied to the same source
\emph{raised} the shape order from $+0.916$ to $+1.293$. The two results are
opposite in sign and they are not in conflict.

They are one story, and the discriminating variable is the size of the uncorrected
volume error rather than the dimensionality of the case. The shift is a single
scalar $\varepsilon$ added to the whole field, so it displaces the zero contour by
$\varepsilon/\gradpsi$ everywhere at once: it can only remove a volume error that
is itself a nearly uniform interface displacement. Where the uncorrected volume
error is large --- 3D shear with the source, volume order $+0.230$ --- that is
what the error is, the shift is the right shape of correction for it, and
restoring the volume moves the interface back, which is why the shape order rises.
Where the uncorrected volume error is already small --- the 2D reversed vortex
with the source, volume order $+2.090$; the sourceless arms of both cases at
$+3.928$ and $+2.733$ --- there is no systematic displacement left to remove, the
root of \eqref{eq:newtonshift} is fitted to what remains, which is residual noise,
and the shift displaces the interface by that noise. The shape order then falls
because the correction is making the interface position worse in order to make one
scalar exact. On 3D shear the sourceless shape order falls $+1.866 \to +1.745$ and
on the 2D vortex $+2.134 \to +2.106$ for the same reason and by less, because
those arms have less volume error for the shift to chase.

The gradient column is untouched at both dimensionalities and in all four pairs of
arms --- $+1.112 \to +1.119$ and $-0.116 \to -0.107$ on the 2D vortex,
$+0.679 \to +0.707$ and $-0.171 \to -0.161$ on 3D shear --- which is the empirical
confirmation of the construction argument above. A spatially constant
$\varepsilon$ leaves $\gradpsi$ unchanged in the discrete operators as well as in
the continuum, and the measurement now says so on four independent pairs of fits
spanning two cases, two dimensionalities and both source settings. Whatever the
correction costs, it does not cost the signed-distance property, which is the one
guarantee this paper needs from it.

\paragraph{The floor artifact, which must not be read as an order.} Four entries in
the record --- the $5.923$, the $6.432$ and the $0.835$ in
Table~\ref{tab:volcorr}, and a $0.016$ seen elsewhere in the same study group ---
are not convergence rates and must never be quoted as such. They are the signature of an error that has been driven to the
tolerance floor of the correction itself, where a least-squares fit through four
or seven points is measuring nothing but noise. The smoke measurement makes the
size of that floor explicit: at $N=32$, $t=0.05$, the relative volume error goes
from $8.5917\times10^{-4}$ to $3.7357\times10^{-16}$ with the
\texttt{simpleLinearImplicit} source and from $2.6103\times10^{-3}$ to
$2.4693\times10^{-13}$ with the exponential source, converging in two Newton
iterations, with $|\varepsilon|$ of order $0.001$ to $0.003$ cell widths. An
error at $10^{-16}$ is at round-off; the correct report is ``no longer
converging, pinned at the correction floor'', and the fitted number should be
suppressed rather than printed.

\paragraph{Documented limits.} Three, and they bound the model's use rather than
qualify it. \emph{First}, a single global $\varepsilon$ conserves the
\emph{total} phase volume and nothing else. \emph{Second}, it is therefore
ill-posed for several disconnected bodies: with bodies $B_1,\dots,B_m$ the honest
problem is $m$ equations $V_i(\varepsilon_i) = V_{i,\text{target}}$ in $m$
unknowns, and one $\varepsilon$ solves their sum --- a run in which one droplet
loses volume while another gains it reports zero total volume error while both
bodies are wrong, and the shift inflates every body when the total is short,
including those already too large. The remedy is a connected-component labelling
of $\{\psilev<0\}$ with a per-body $\varepsilon_i$; it is not implemented here.
\emph{Third}, and most important for this paper: it must not be used on the
coupled droplet of Section~\ref{sec:coupled}. That failure is a mode-4 parasitic
current amplified $260\times$ that squares and destroys the interface; the volume
loss is its symptom, not a bookkeeping error. Applying the correction there would
restore the volume \emph{number} and leave a square droplet of the correct area
--- strictly worse than the visible failure, because a metric table would then
show a converged volume column for a run whose interface has been destroyed. Any
study that uses this model is required to report the shape error alongside the
volume error and the applied shift, for exactly that reason.

\section{Two coupled-patch defects, and the results they suspend}
\label{sec:coupledpatch}

Two further defects have since been found and removed. Both live on
\emph{coupled} (processor) patches, so both are invisible in serial and affect
parallel runs only, and neither could have been caught by any of the single-rank
gates above. They are recorded here because they suspend a class of results rather
than a single number. The full post-mortem, including the audit of every affected
study, is in \texttt{docs/gradU-coupled-patch-contamination.md}; what follows is
what a reader of \emph{this} paper needs in order to know which of its numbers are
provisional.

\subsection{A face-centre value written into a cell-centre slot}
\label{sec:gradUdefect}

\texttt{velocityModel::setVelocity} evaluated the prescribed velocity at the face
centre, $\mathbf{v}(\mathbf{x}_f)$, and wrote it into \emph{every} boundary patch
--- including the coupled ones, where OpenFOAM expects the value at the
\emph{neighbouring cell centre} rather than at the face. The consequence is not a
boundary-condition subtlety, it is an order loss. On a uniform mesh the coupled
patch's face interpolation then returns
$\tfrac12\mathbf{v}(\mathbf{x}_P) + \tfrac12\mathbf{v}(\mathbf{x}_f)$ where it
should return $\mathbf{v}(\mathbf{x}_f)$, an error of $a\,h/4$ for a velocity of
strain rate $a$; divided by the cell volume in the Gauss sum that error becomes an
$O(a)$ --- not $O(h)$ --- error in $\nabla\mathbf{v}$ in \emph{every}
processor-adjacent cell. It does not converge away, and refinement does not dilute
it, because it is proportional to the strain being measured rather than to the
mesh spacing.

$\mathrm{d}\psilev/\mathrm{d}x$ with the source on is $1$ by construction:

\section{Negative results}
Measured and documented here and in the companion deck. Retained:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{beta's target is structurally wrong}, and this is now measured
    rather than argued. Differentiating the transport equation and evaluating at
    $\psilev=0$ gives, for $g=\gradpsi$ and the interfacial normal strain
    $a = \nhat\cdot\nabla\mathbf{u}\cdot\nhat$,
    \[
      \frac{\mathrm{D}g}{\mathrm{D}t} \;=\; g\,(f_{nl} - a),
    \]
    so the strain-cancelling choice $f_{nl}=a$ makes $\mathrm{D}g/\mathrm{D}t=0$
    identically --- whatever error remains is numerical, which is why it
    converges --- while $f_{nl} = \beta - g$ has the fixed point
    $g_\ast = \beta - a$, set by the flow rather than by the mesh. Three
    consequences, all observed on the 2D reversed vortex:
    \begin{enumerate}
      \item The band mean $\gradpsi$ at maximal deformation is
        $1.516, 1.498, 1.478, 1.485, 1.481$ for $N = 32\ldots512$ --- flat to
        about $2\%$ over a sixteenfold refinement, against $1.071 \to 1.002$ for
        the strain-cancelling source. A residual vanishes with $h$; this does
        not.
      \item The measured band strain reaches $\max a = 2.77$ against $\beta=1$,
        so over part of the interface the fixed point $\beta - a$ is
        \emph{negative}. A magnitude cannot reach it, and $g$ is driven toward
        zero instead: the level set flattens hardest exactly where the flow
        stretches hardest. The band minimum, $0.423$ against $0.883$ for
        \texttt{R} at $N=512$, is that effect
        (Figure~\ref{fig:graderr-atlas}).
      \item On this reversible flow the sourceless drift cancels by
        time-reversal symmetry --- band mean $1.0000$ at $t=T$, $N=512$, with
        \texttt{R} at $0.9991$. \texttt{beta} gives $1.2562$ and degrades under
        refinement. It is the only arm that makes the reversal worse than doing
        nothing.
    \end{enumerate}
    None of this is interface displacement: volume and shape errors are at the
    $10^{-4}$ level or below for every arm at fine resolution
    (Figure~\ref{fig:alpha-atlas}).
  \item \textbf{A source term does not repair resolution.} Strain cancellation
    is exact only while the interface is resolved; near $\kappa h \sim 1$ the
    reconstructed normal, and hence $f_{nl}$, is unreliable.
  \item \textbf{The dead \texttt{limit} entry.} \texttt{limit} is read only by
    the tanh profile; \texttt{signedDistance} ignores it, so two studies built
    on it compared identical initial fields.
  \item \textbf{\texttt{sdplsBeta} requires an explicit \texttt{beta}} --- the
    missing-coefficient construction failure; explicit beats a hidden default.
  \item \textbf{Kinematic verification is structurally blind to the coupled
    failure.} Under a prescribed velocity the return path
    $\psilev \rightarrow \mathbf{v}$ of \eqref{eq:capillaryloop} does not exist,
    so no ladder of Section~\ref{sec:verification} --- however many rungs, however
    many arms --- could have detected what Section~\ref{sec:coupled} measures. A
    source term that reads the computed velocity must be verified on both tracks,
    and a method paper that reports only the kinematic one is incomplete by
    construction rather than by omission.
  \item \textbf{A temporal correction cannot repair a spatial order.} The
    \texttt{exponential} linearization delivers the source's one-step factor
    exactly and removes the $O(z^2)$ rectification of \eqref{eq:rectify}
    identically, and it changes the band gradient order by $0.003$: $+1.109$
    against $+1.112$ on the reversed 2D vortex (Table~\ref{tab:expsource}), and
    by $0.028$ on the serial three-rung ladder that carries all three deliveries,
    $+0.936$ against $+0.908$ (Table~\ref{tab:expimplicit}). The order loss of
    Section~\ref{sec:sourceerror} is $(\nabla e)_h = O(h^{p-1})$, which no time
    discretization touches, and this is the most direct test of that statement in
    the paper --- three deliveries of the same source, spanning a rational
    approximation of the one-step factor, the exact factor delivered explicitly
    and the exact factor delivered implicitly, all landing within $0.03$ of each
    other in gradient order.
  \item \textbf{What cost the shape and volume order was the explicit delivery,
    not the exponential factor --- and the ablation now says so.} The explicit
    \texttt{exponential} member sets $S_p = 0$ and delivers the whole source from
    $\psilev^n$, giving up the implicit coupling between source and transport
    operator, and it pays in the two metrics that measure where the zero contour
    ends up: shape $+1.883 \rightarrow +1.256$ and volume $+2.090 \rightarrow
    +1.718$ on the four-rank seven-rung ladder (Table~\ref{tab:expsource}), and
    $+1.600 \rightarrow +1.360$ and $+1.638 \rightarrow +1.439$ on the serial
    three-rung one (Table~\ref{tab:expimplicit}). That attribution was an argument
    from the structure of \eqref{eq:expsource} until a member existed that carries
    the same factor \emph{with} diagonal support. \texttt{exponentialImplicit}
    \eqref{eq:expimplicit}, $S_p = (1-e^{-z})/\Delta t$ with $S_c = 0$, is that
    member, and it repairs the loss completely: $+0.912$, $+1.603$ and $+1.653$
    against \texttt{simpleLinearImplicit}'s $+0.908$, $+1.600$ and $+1.638$ on one
    serial ladder, and a factor of $770$ better than the explicit form in both
    interface position and phase volume on the 1D gate
    (Table~\ref{tab:expimplicit1d}). The coefficient is $(1-e^{-z})/\Delta t$ and
    not $(e^{z}-1)/\Delta t$: the latter is the \emph{explicit} coefficient, and
    used implicitly it gives $\psilev^{n+1} = \psilev^n/(2-e^{z})$, with a pole at
    $z = \ln 2$ and a sign-flipped $\psilev$ beyond it. The explicit member is
    superseded; the implicit one holds the unconditional sign preservation that
    was the explicit member's sole retained justification, through
    $1 - S_p\Delta t = e^{-z} > 0$, and pays nothing for it.
  \item \textbf{For this source, matching the continuum coefficient matters more
    than integrating the source ODE exactly.} On the 1D uniaxial-stretch gate
    \texttt{simpleLinearImplicit} is exact --- band gradient error
    $7.19\times10^{-12}$ --- while both exponential members sit at
    $7.5\times10^{-3}$, nine orders of magnitude behind, despite reproducing the
    one-step factor to machine precision. The reason is structural rather than a
    property of the integrator: the closed-form solution satisfies
    $\mathbf{v}\cdot\nabla\psilev = a\,\psilev$ pointwise
    \eqref{eq:steadybalance}, a steady balance, and $S_p = f_{nl}$ reproduces the
    continuum coefficient $a$ exactly, so the balance is enforced at time level
    $n+1$ to machine precision whatever $\Delta t$ is. A more accurate ODE
    integrator perturbs that coefficient instead of matching it ---
    $(1-e^{-z})/\Delta t = a - a^2\Delta t/2 + O(a^3 \Delta t^2)$ --- and breaks
    the balance at $O(a^2\Delta t)$, which is exactly the $7.5\times10^{-3}$ both
    exponential members show. The effect is invisible on the 2D ladder, where the
    three deliveries agree to $0.03$ in gradient order, and it is a property of a
    source whose purpose is to cancel a term rather than to evolve one.
  \item \textbf{Volume correction is a crossover, not an improvement, and the
    discriminating variable is the size of the uncorrected volume error.}
    Restoring the phase volume with a uniform level-set shift raises the shape
    order where the uncorrected volume error is large ($+0.916 \rightarrow
    +1.293$ on 3D shear with the source, whose uncorrected volume order is a poor
    $+0.230$) and \emph{lowers} it where the uncorrected volume error is already
    small ($+1.883 \rightarrow +1.671$ on the reversed 2D vortex with the source,
    whose uncorrected volume order is $+2.090$). On arms that were converging in
    volume it also destroys the convergence rate itself: $+3.928 \rightarrow
    +1.050$ on the sourceless 3D shear arm, and $+2.733 \rightarrow 6.432$ on the
    sourceless 2D arm, the latter a tolerance floor and not a rate. A single
    scalar $\varepsilon$ displaces the zero contour by $\varepsilon/\gradpsi$
    everywhere at once, so it can only remove a volume error that is itself a
    nearly uniform interface displacement; where no such displacement remains it
    is fitted to residual noise and moves the interface by that noise instead.
    $\gradpsi$ is untouched in all four pairs of arms and at both
    dimensionalities (Section~\ref{sec:volcorr}).
  \item \textbf{Loss of diagonal dominance is not the mechanism of the
    divergence-form failure.} The implicit coefficient \eqref{eq:rdivsp} of
    \texttt{Rdiv} has no sign guarantee, and this was the leading hypothesis for
    its divergence. The sign-split variant \texttt{RdivStrictSp}
    \eqref{eq:rdivstrictsp} removes the violation, and demonstrably so: at $N=32$
    the solver logs $512$ of $1024$ cells with $f = a - \nabla\cdot\mathbf{w} >
    0$, so half the domain was on the destabilising branch and after the split
    none of it is. The divergence barely moves --- band gradient order $-3.554$
    against $-3.800$, ending at $635$ against $668.9$ at $N=256$, shape $-0.951$
    against $-1.306$, volume $-0.863$ against $-1.544$
    (Table~\ref{tab:rdiv-strictsp}). The scope of this is narrow and must be
    stated as such, and it is now sharper than it was when first written. An
    M-matrix requires two things: a dominant diagonal, and non-positive
    off-diagonals. The sign split addresses the \emph{diagonal}, and the diagonal
    was never the problem --- no row of the \texttt{Rdiv} system loses dominance
    outright, which is why every run completes. The sign violation that matters is
    in the \emph{off-diagonals}, which the split leaves untouched by construction
    because they belong to the operator pair and not to $f$
    (Section~\ref{sec:rdiv-mechanism}). The experiment could not have found the
    defect it was aimed at, and the two suspects it left standing have since been
    measured and are bounded and second-order (Table~\ref{tab:rdiv-coefficient}).
  \item \textbf{The divergence-form failure is a maximum-principle violation in a
    difference of two upwind operators, and it cannot be repaired without giving up
    the conservation the form exists to provide.} Because
    \texttt{fvMatrix::operator==} subtracts termwise, \texttt{Rdiv} assembles
    $\mathrm{ddt} + 2\,\mathrm{div}(\phi,\psilev) -
    \mathrm{div}(\phi_{\mathbf{w}},\psilev)$ \eqref{eq:rdivmatrix} --- a
    \emph{difference} of two upwind convection operators rather than one. A single
    upwind $\mathrm{fvm::div}$ has non-positive off-diagonals whatever the sign of
    the face flux; subtracting one flips them, and on a face where
    $\mathrm{sign}(\phi_f) \neq \mathrm{sign}(\phi_{\mathbf{w},f})$ the composite
    off-diagonal is exactly $+|\phi_{\mathbf{w},f}|$ \eqref{eq:posoffdiag}.
    Measured in the assembly at $N=64$: $2924$ of $8064$ internal faces
    sign-mismatched ($36.3\%$, carrying $10.9\%$ of the flux magnitude), $3382$ of
    $16128$ off-diagonal coefficients positive ($21.0\%$), and a largest positive
    off-diagonal row sum of $\approx 0.19$ in units of $|\Omega_c|/\Delta t$ ---
    about $0.38\,\mathrm{CFL}$, and mesh-independent. No row loses diagonal
    dominance, so every run completes; the discrete maximum principle is
    nonetheless gone, and with the per-step gain fixed and the number of steps to a
    fixed end time growing like $1/h$, the amplification grows without bound under
    refinement. The measured error ratios accelerate --- $4.1$, $11.2$, $28.0$ over
    the serial ladder (Table~\ref{tab:rdiv-serial}) --- where a convergence order
    would give a constant ratio, so this is an amplification in time and not a
    consistency error. Two controls close it. On the 1D uniaxial-stretch gate
    $\mathbf{w} = \mathbf{v}$, hence $\phi_{\mathbf{w}} = \phi$, the probe reports
    zero sign-mismatched faces and zero positive off-diagonals, and \texttt{Rdiv}
    is exact. And the blend sweep over $\phi_\lambda = \lambda\phi +
    (1-\lambda)\phi_{\mathbf{w}}$ \eqref{eq:blend}, every member of which is
    continuum-equivalent because the tangential velocity does no work on a level
    set \eqref{eq:tangentialnowork}, is unimodal and peaks at $\lambda = 1$ ---
    i.e.\ at $\phi$, the flux the algebraic source \textbf{R} convects on --- on
    gradient, shape and volume simultaneously (Table~\ref{tab:rdiv-blend}).
    Conservation requires routing the operator through
    $\mathrm{div}(\phi_{\mathbf{w}},\cdot)$ and therefore forces $\lambda \neq
    1$: a single upwind operator, conservation on the normal flux, and the optimal
    convecting flux cannot hold together, and at $\lambda = 1$ the form \emph{is}
    \textbf{R} with the conservation gone.
  \item \textbf{Two coupled-patch defects suspend every parallel kinematic result
    carrying an SDPLS source.} \texttt{velocityModel::setVelocity} wrote a face-centre
    velocity into coupled patches, where the neighbouring cell-centre value is
    expected, giving an $O(a)$ --- not $O(h)$ --- error in
    $\mathrm{fvc::grad}(\mathbf{U})$ in every processor-adjacent cell and hence in
    the strain $a$ the source reads; and \texttt{interfaceExtension::updateFlux}
    wrote the raw flux over processor patches, which are interior faces in serial,
    so the field advecting $\psilev$ differed between serial and parallel by
    construction. Both are fixed; both were parallel-only; neither could be caught
    by a serial gate. Fitted orders from multi-rank ladders are provisional until
    re-measured, and the findings they support stand only where a serial
    measurement reproduces them --- which, for the two that matter here, it does
    (Tables~\ref{tab:rdiv-serial} and~\ref{tab:expimplicit}). Full post-mortem:
    \texttt{docs/gradU-coupled-patch-contamination.md}, and
    Section~\ref{sec:coupledpatch}.
\end{itemize}

    failures are defects of the norm rather than properties of the operator. The
    tabulated residuals are evaluated at $t=T$, by which time the field the probe
    differentiates is no longer the sampled exact signed distance, so they measure a
    polluted $\psilev$ and not the operator. And the same residual measured at the
    first assembly but in a \emph{global} $L_\infty$ is flat at $\approx0.44$ on
    every rung, with its argmax on the cell holding the circle centre, at
    $r/h = 0.71$, where the signed distance has its kink and
    $\nabla\cdot\nhat = (d-1)/r$ is singular: a fixed geometric singularity of the
    exact solution, read at every resolution. Measured at the first assembly, while
    $\psilev$ is still exact to $5\times10^{-14}$, and over the fixed physical
    annulus $|\psilev_{\text{exact}}| \le 0.05$ --- never a multiple of $h$, since a
    norm whose domain moves with the mesh has no convergence order --- the residual
    converges at $+2.005$ in $L_2$ and $+1.915$ in $L_\infty$, the coefficient error
    at $+1.972$, the divergence operator fed an exactly sampled $\mathbf{w}$ at
    $+1.970$ and the discrete normal's own contribution at $+2.018$, against
    mesh-independent reference norms (Table~\ref{tab:rdiv-coefficient}).
    Substituting the exact smooth $\mathbf{w}$ for the discrete one and changing
    nothing else moves the residual by less than $20\%$ at every instant and rung
    (ratio $0.81$ to $1.09$). \emph{Both} previously named suspects --- coefficient
    noise and assembly inconsistency --- are therefore bounded and second-order
    convergent, and neither explains the divergence; the mechanism that does is in
    Section~\ref{sec:rdiv-mechanism}.
  \item ``The two remaining suspects for the divergence-form failure ---
    second derivatives of $\psilev$ inside $\nabla\cdot\mathbf{w}$, and the
    near-cancellation of two large \texttt{fvm::div} terms --- remain live'' ---
    \emph{corrected} by the same measurement. Both are dead. The divergence is not
    in any coefficient and not in the consistency of the assembly; it is in the
    sign pattern of the assembled matrix, which \eqref{eq:rdivmatrix} makes a
    difference of two upwind convection operators.
  \item ``The \texttt{exponential} linearization changes nothing: identical
    gradient order, no order gain in either direction, and its value is exactness
    in time and unconditional sign preservation'' --- \emph{corrected}, at the
    configuration where it was made, and recorded here rather than deleted. That
    verdict was drawn from \texttt{sdplsExpSource2Dvortex}, which ran the 2D
    vortex at $T=1$ with the oscillation off and therefore could not populate the
    shape column at any resolution --- the shape error is defined against the
    initial condition, which is the correct answer only where the flow returns
    the interface to it. The comparison was two metrics wide, and the missing
    metric is the one that separates the two deliveries. Re-run under the
    reversed protocol (\texttt{sdplsExpSource2DvortexRev},
    Table~\ref{tab:expsource}) the gradient half of the claim survives ---
    $+1.109$ against $+1.112$, a difference of $0.003$ --- and the neutrality
    half does not: the shape order falls from $+1.883$ to $+1.256$ and the volume
    order at $t=T$ from $+2.090$ to $+1.718$. The corrected verdict is in
    Section~\ref{sec:exp}: the \emph{explicit} exponential delivery costs shape and
    volume order, because $S_p=0$ gives up the implicit coupling between the source
    and the transport operator. The recommendation that followed that correction ---
    ``select it where its unconditional sign preservation is specifically required''
    --- has since been superseded in its turn, and is recorded here for the same
    reason. \texttt{exponentialImplicit} \eqref{eq:expimplicit} carries the same
    exponential factor on the diagonal, holds the same unconditional sign
    preservation through $1 - S_p\Delta t = e^{-z} > 0$, and does not pay the shape
    and volume order (Table~\ref{tab:expimplicit}). There is no remaining
    configuration in which the explicit member is the right choice.
  \item ``The exact-curvature \texttt{Rdiv} runs are decisive'' ---
    \emph{corrected}: they are a \emph{null test}. With the exact-curvature force
    the velocity is $O(10^{-11})\,\mathrm{m/s}$, so
    $\mathbf{w} = (\nhat\cdot\mathbf{v})\nhat \approx \mathbf{0}$ and every term
    of \eqref{eq:rdivassembly} is approximately zero. Those runs are evidence
    about the seed, not about the operator, and they are labelled as such wherever
    they appear (Table~\ref{tab:rdiv-coupled}).
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusions}
The SDPLS source controls $\gradpsi$ through the transport equation with zero
interface displacement by construction, and --- once the sign, the lifetime bug
and the measuring operator are corrected, and once every arm is run on one
discretization --- the strain-cancelling form converges on a non-reversing flow
where the sourceless baseline diverges, without paying for it in volume
conservation. The two linearizations agree, which is the internal consistency
check the defects had been violating.

The negative results are as load-bearing as the positive one. \texttt{beta} does
not fail through instability or under-resolution: it relaxes accurately to
$\beta - a$, a target set by the flow rather than by the signed-distance
condition, so a constant $\beta$ is correct only where $\beta = 1 + a$ and no
amount of refinement removes the resulting error. And the reversed benchmark
conventionally used to assess such source terms is structurally unable to
discriminate them, because the final-time error of a passively advected level
set cancels by time-reversal symmetry --- it reported the sourceless baseline
as $477\times$ more accurate than the source that, on the same case without the
reversal, is the only arm that converges.

Under two-way coupling the source fails, and the failure has a single seed. On
the stationary droplet, with the production curvature, \textbf{R} drains the
droplet to nothing at $N=32$ and diverges at $N=64$ and $N=128$, while the
identical solver with the source off conserves the phase volume to
$1.2\times10^{-5}$ at $N=128$; the mechanism is a mesh-locked four-lobed
parasitic current that the source amplifies by $260\times$ through the loop
$\psilev \rightarrow \kappa \rightarrow \mathbf{f}_\sigma \rightarrow
\mathbf{v} \rightarrow a \rightarrow \psilev$. Against a surface-tension force
built from the exact curvature $\kappa = 1/R$ of the circle --- for which the
capillary flux is an exact discrete gradient that the pressure equation absorbs,
leaving a non-absorbable flux fraction of $6.5\times10^{-11}$ against
$1.7\times10^{-5}$ for the production delivery --- the same source produces
$0.000$ volume error and $0.000$ shape error at every resolution, on both time
discretizations of $\psilev$, in twelve cases out of twelve. The loop has no
other seed. The hypothesis that the failure is intrinsic to the source under
two-way coupling is therefore dead, and the repair belongs on the force side.
The scope limit belongs in the same breath: with $\kappa$ pinned constant the
velocity is $O(10^{-11})\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and the interface never moves, so this
proves that the loop \emph{requires} the curvature-error seed and not that
\textbf{R} is well behaved against an accurate but imperfect force. That the
distinction is real is shown by the well-balanced model of
\cite{abualsaud2018}, implemented here and measured dynamically unstable on a
translating capillary gate: better static balance can coexist with larger
dynamic feedback gain.

The time discretization is not the driver of that failure, and the divergence-form
source is not a fix for it. Three discretizations of $\partial_t\psilev$ ---
first-order L-stable Euler, second-order A-stable Crank--Nicolson, second-order
L-stable backward --- change when the coupled run fails and by how much, with
backward the best of the three (it retains the most volume at $N=32$ and is the
only one that integrates the finest mesh to the end time at all), and none of
them changes whether it fails; the $O(z^2)$ rectification that implicit Euler applies
to a zero-mean oscillating strain is real, identified and removable, and removing
it is not enough. The conservative rewrite \texttt{Rdiv} is withdrawn as evidence
about the coupled loop entirely: it diverges on a prescribed divergence-free
velocity with no capillary force at all, at band gradient order $-3.800$, shape
$-1.306$ and volume $-1.544$, with every run completing --- converged solutions of
a wrong equation rather than crashes. Its coupled behaviour needs no appeal to
the loop, and it had never been run kinematically because the verification cases
carried no scheme entry for its flux, so it aborted at the first step. It is now
withdrawn as a method as well, on two further measurements. The 3D shear ladder,
outstanding when that retraction was written, has since completed and reproduces
the failure on all three metrics --- band gradient order $-3.383$, shape $-2.571$,
volume $-3.381$ --- so the divergence-form source has failed a kinematic gate at
both dimensionalities. And the one targeted repair its own failure analysis named
has been run and does not rescue it: the implicit coefficient \eqref{eq:rdivsp}
has no sign guarantee, the sign-split variant \texttt{RdivStrictSp} removes that
violation on the half of the domain that carried it --- $512$ cells of $1024$ at
$N=32$ --- and the divergence moves from $-3.800$, $-1.306$ and $-1.544$ only to
$-3.554$, $-0.951$ and $-0.863$. Loss of diagonal dominance is therefore not the
mechanism --- and the replacement mechanism is now established, which is the one
substantive change to this account since it was first written. The two suspects
that the sign split left standing, second-derivative noise in
$\nabla\cdot\mathbf{w}$ and inconsistency between the two \texttt{fvm::div} terms,
have both been measured directly at the first assembly and over a fixed physical
band, and both are bounded and second-order convergent ($+1.972$ for the
coefficient error, $+2.005$ for the product-rule residual, and less than $20\%$
change when the exact $\mathbf{w}$ is substituted for the discrete one); the
earlier probe that was read as convicting the second of them is withdrawn, because
it was evaluated at $t=T$ on a polluted $\psilev$ and through a global $L_\infty$
whose argmax sits on the circle-centre kink, where $\nabla\cdot\nhat = (d-1)/r$ is
singular. What diverges is the sign pattern of the assembled matrix. Because
\texttt{fvMatrix::operator==} subtracts termwise, \texttt{Rdiv} assembles
$\mathrm{ddt} + 2\,\mathrm{div}(\phi,\psilev) -
\mathrm{div}(\phi_{\mathbf{w}},\psilev)$: a \emph{difference} of two upwind
convection operators, whose composite off-diagonal is exactly
$+|\phi_{\mathbf{w},f}|$ on every face where the two fluxes disagree in sign
($36.3\%$ of internal faces at $N=64$, giving $21.0\%$ positive off-diagonal
coefficients at a mesh-independent strength of about $0.38\,\mathrm{CFL}$). No row
loses diagonal dominance, which is why every run completes; the discrete maximum
principle is gone, and the measured error ratios accelerate --- $4.1$, $11.2$,
$28.0$ --- where a convergence order would hold them constant. The algebraic source
\textbf{R} assembles a single \texttt{fvm::div} and cannot do this, which is the
whole of the structural difference between the two forms; on the 1D gate, where
$\phi_{\mathbf{w}} = \phi$ and the mismatch count is zero, \texttt{Rdiv} is exact.
Repairing the sign pattern would nevertheless not rescue the form. Every member of
the blended flux $\phi_\lambda = \lambda\phi + (1-\lambda)\phi_{\mathbf{w}}$ is
continuum-equivalent, because the tangential velocity does no work on a level set,
and the measured optimum over gradient, shape and volume simultaneously is
$\lambda = 1$ --- the flux \textbf{R} already convects on --- with monotone loss in
both directions. Conservation is obtained precisely by routing the operator through
$\mathrm{div}(\phi_{\mathbf{w}},\cdot)$, which forces $\lambda \neq 1$, so a
single upwind operator, conservation on the normal flux and the optimal convecting
flux cannot hold together; fixing the maximum principle would recover stability and
not accuracy. The divergence-form source is accordingly withdrawn on a structural
argument rather than pending an explanation, and is not carried as an available
option. Finally,
the two invariants that the conservative argument had conflated are now measured
apart: with the source off $\int\psilev\,\mathrm dV$ is conserved to machine
precision, while the \textbf{R} arm drifts it by $-2.2\times10^{-4}$ and destroys
the droplet in the same run --- four orders of magnitude between the quantity a
flux form conserves and the quantity the droplet lives in.

Two further results belong in the summary because they change what should be
selected and what should be believed. On the delivery of the source, the cost that
the exact-in-time \texttt{exponential} member pays in shape and volume order is
now known not to be a property of the exponential factor: requiring the exact
one-step factor to be delivered \emph{implicitly} gives
$S_p = (1 - e^{-z})/\Delta t$ with $S_c = 0$ and $z = f_{nl}\Delta t$ --- not
$(e^{z}-1)/\Delta t$, which is the explicit coefficient and, used implicitly, has a
pole at $z = \ln 2$ and returns a negative $\psilev$ beyond it --- and that member
recovers the shape and volume order in full ($+1.603$ and $+1.653$ against
$+1.600$ and $+1.638$ for \texttt{simpleLinearImplicit} on one serial ladder) while
keeping the unconditional sign preservation and removing the
$|f_{nl}|\Delta t < 1$ diagonal restriction by construction. The explicit member is
superseded by it. What the same comparison also shows, on the 1D uniaxial-stretch
gate where the answer is known in closed form, is that
\texttt{simpleLinearImplicit} is \emph{exact} there --- $7.19\times10^{-12}$
against $7.5\times10^{-3}$ for both exponential members --- because the exact
solution is a steady balance $\mathbf{v}\cdot\nabla\psilev = a\,\psilev$ and
$S_p = f_{nl}$ reproduces the continuum coefficient exactly, while any more
accurate ODE integrator perturbs it at $O(a^2\Delta t)$. For this source, matching
the coefficient matters more than integrating the ODE exactly. And on what should
be believed: two coupled-patch defects have been found and fixed --- a face-centre
velocity written into coupled patches, giving an $O(a)$ error in
$\mathrm{fvc::grad}(\mathbf{U})$ in every processor-adjacent cell and hence in the
strain the source reads, and the raw flux written over processor faces in the
interface extension --- so every parallel kinematic result carrying an SDPLS source
predates the fixes and its fitted orders are provisional. Both defects are
parallel-only, nothing measured serially moves, and the two findings that matter most here have
been reproduced serially on clean binaries: the divergence of the divergence-form
source, and the shape and volume cost of the explicit exponential delivery.

What a source term still cannot do is manufacture resolution: the cancellation
is only as good as the normal it is built from.

\paragraph{Open.}
\label{sec:conclusions-open}
Five things, none of them claimed here. \emph{First}, the mechanism of the
divergence-form failure is identified and the line is closed, but the question the
form was raised to answer is not. A conservative source that keeps a single upwind
convection operator, carries its conservation on the normal flux, and convects on
the optimal flux $\lambda = 1$ cannot exist in the family \eqref{eq:blend}, and no
formulation outside that family is proposed here. Whether conservation of
$\int\psilev\,\mathrm dV$ can be obtained by some other route without paying the
accuracy that $\lambda \neq 1$ costs is open, and it is a reformulation rather than
a patch. \emph{Second}, the coupled result names the force as the place to work,
and does not name a force: a model whose curvature error is small \emph{and} whose
dynamic loop gain stays below one has not been identified, and the one obvious
candidate in the literature is measured unstable here. \emph{Third}, the ablation
that the previous version of this paragraph called for --- a member carrying the
exponential factor \emph{with} diagonal support, isolating the explicit delivery
from the exactness as a single variable --- has been built and run, and it confirms
the attribution (Table~\ref{tab:expimplicit}). What is open is the regime in which
the member is worth selecting: the guarantee it adds is a diagonal that cannot
change sign when $f_{nl}\Delta t > 1$, and no ladder reported here runs in that
regime, so the robustness is established by construction and not by measurement.
The exactness of \texttt{simpleLinearImplicit} is likewise established only on the
1D gate, which is the only configuration here whose exact solution is a steady
balance; there is no 2D or 3D analogue of that measurement. \emph{Fourth}, the
non-reversing 3D shear and LeVeque deformation ladders are still outstanding, and
no coupled study reported here measures the band gradient error at all.
\emph{Fifth}, the two coupled-patch defects of Section~\ref{sec:coupledpatch}
leave three things pending. Every multi-rank ladder in this paper is awaiting
re-measurement on the fixed binaries. The serial--parallel gate is improved by six
to seven orders of magnitude but not closed: the residual at $np=8$ is
$2.806\times10^{-3}$ at eight cells per rank, about $10^{-4}$ at sixteen to
thirty-two, and it is the halo path's first-order Taylor surrogate, which is not
yet fixed. And the velocity-extension record's claim that \texttt{closestPoint} is
$2185\times$ worse than plain Euler advection on the band gradient error, measured
at $N=512$ before both fixes, is marked disputed rather than replaced: re-measured
after them the ratio is $0.172$ at $N=128$ and $0.267$ at $N=256$ --- better, not
worse --- while shape and volume remain worse and trend toward the recorded
$3.5\times$ and $14.8\times$. The resolutions differ, so the comparison is not
like-for-like, and the published number stands until an $N=512$ re-measurement
settles it.
