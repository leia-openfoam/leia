Semi-Lagrangian update — no fluxes, no limiters

The level set \(\psi\) is constant along characteristics of \(\partial_t\psi + \mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla\psi = 0\). Trace the characteristic arriving at a cell centre \(\mathbf{x}_c\) one step backward to its departure foot \(\mathbf{x}_d\):

\[ \psi^{\,n+1}(\mathbf{x}_c) \;=\; \psi^{\,n}(\mathbf{x}_d) \]
  • no flux, no divergence scheme, no linear system — one reconstruction per cell
  • exactly two ingredients: locate \(\mathbf{x}_d\), reconstruct \(\psi^n\) there
  • the foot and everything downstream (phase indicator, band, error function objects) are shared verbatim with the quadratic companion line

Second-order departure foot (shared)

\[ \mathbf{x}_d = \mathbf{x}_c - \mathbf{u}^{\,n+1}\Delta t + \tfrac12\left[\frac{\mathbf{u}^{\,n+1}-\mathbf{u}^{\,n}}{\Delta t} + (\mathbf{u}^{\,n+1}\!\cdot\nabla)\,\mathbf{u}^{\,n+1}\right]\Delta t^{2} \]
  • backward Taylor expansion of the arriving characteristic; the bracket approximates the material acceleration \(\mathrm{D}\mathbf{u}/\mathrm{D}t\)
  • dropped terms are \(O(\Delta t^{3})\) per step → the foot is second order in time
  • foot-radius guard: if \(|\mathbf{x}_d-\mathbf{x}_c|\) leaves the neighbour-stencil hull, the operational limit \(\mathrm{CFL}\le 1\) is violated and the run warns

Identical for the linear and the quadratic line — the reconstruction is the ONLY difference between the two methods.

Two ways to reconstruct a linear field — and they behave oppositely

nestedLSQ (the method)

\[ R_c = \psi^{\,n}_c + \mathbf{g}_c\cdot(\mathbf{x}_d-\mathbf{x}_c) \]

\(\mathbf{g}_c\) = least-squares slope over the neighbour stencil \(\mathcal S_c\) (linear-basis restriction of the quadratic fit):

\[ \mathbf{g}_c = \arg\min_{\mathbf g}\!\sum_{i\in\mathcal S_c}\! w_i^2 (\psi_c+\mathbf g\!\cdot\!\mathbf d_i-\psi_i)^2 \]

stencil averaging → STABLE

linearTaylor (the trap)

\[ R_c^{\mathrm T} = \psi^{\,n}_c + (\nabla\psi^{\,n})_c\cdot(\mathbf{x}_d-\mathbf{x}_c) \]

gradient = the arrival cell's single cell gradient, extrapolated across the whole foot displacement — no stencil averaging

single-cell extrapolation → UNSTABLE in advection (next slide)

Both are degree-1 reconstructions of \(\psi^n\); the method line uses reconstruction nestedLSQ;

linearTaylor is unstable in pure advection

Reversed 2D vortex, CFL \(\tfrac12\), integrated to full reversal \(t=T\). The raw single-cell extrapolation overshoots where \(|\nabla\psi|\) is already too steep → steepens it further → positive feedback, worse on finer meshes (more steps):

  • nestedLSQ: band defect converges; linearTaylor: diverges geometrically (to \(10^{21}\))
  • clipToStencilBounds does NOT cure it — the value near \(\psi=0\) stays in range while \(|\nabla\psi|\) explodes in the field around it

nestedLSQ — three properties

  • Accuracy — exact for linear fields → per-step error \(O(h^2)\); over \(T/\Delta t\propto 1/h\) steps the transport error is first-to-second order (2D shape \(\approx\)1.1, volume \(\approx\)1.5, band gradient \(\approx\)2.1) — below UQWLSR's \(\approx\)3, but a genuine monotone convergence
  • Conditionally stable — the stencil-averaged slope does not amplify shear steepening at CFL \(\le\tfrac12\); at CFL 1 it destabilizes on the finest meshes (Part 2). Not CFL-independent like the quadratic line → run CFL \(\le\tfrac12\)
  • Memory-light & local — a \(2\times2\) / \(3\times3\) solve per cell, no cached pseudo-inverse → \(O(1)\) memory, no fine-mesh ceiling; the only parallel comm is the standard \(\psi^n\) halo

Safeguards & phase indicator (shared machinery)

  • Slope limiter (Barth–Jespersen on the increment): measured on the benchmarks — costs accuracy, buys nothing for the linear fit → OFF by default
  • clipToStencilBounds: monotone clamp of the reconstructed value to the stencil \([\min,\max]\) — required on polyhedra (irregular stencils), inactive in smooth regions → order preserved
  • Face-neighbour stencil (stencil face;) for the polyhedral gradient: 12–16 face neighbours already over-determine the fit — compact, better conditioned
  • Phase indicator: Detrixhe–Aslam tetrahedral decomposition — exact wet volume of each tetrahedron from vertex sign patterns, evaluated in the narrow band; identical to the quadratic line

linearTaylor's redemption: the two-phase workhorse

The unstable variant is not discarded — it is the reconstruction of the two-phase consistent-linear pipeline. In coupled flow, cell-centred curvature feeds surface tension back into the flow; a reinitialization-free high-order transport gives that loop zero damping. linearTaylor's dissipation damps it — and the coupled solver's own dissipation + short pre-equilibrium horizon keep linearTaylor in its stable regime:

2D stationary droplet, blow-up time [s]N=64N=128N=256
quadratic pipeline (UQWLSR + reconstructed κ)0.440.1050.035
consistent linear pipeline (linearTaylor + FVM κ)0.800.125> 0.1
  • no contradiction: the coupled result is relative (linear less destabilizing than quadratic in the loop, short horizon); the kinematic instability is absolute (unbounded growth over long reversible integration)
  • linearTaylor outlives quadratic at every N and breaks the finer-blows-first trend

Benchmark & the complete error picture

Reversed single vortex (Rider–Kothe): circle \(r=0.15\) at \((0.5, 0.75)\), sheared, reversed at \(T/2\), exact solution at \(t=T=2\) = the initial circle.
Ladder \(N = 32 \dots 256\) (ratio \(\sqrt2\)), CFL 0.5 and 1.0.

  • shape error: geometric \(L_1\) of \(\alpha-\alpha^{\rm exact}\)
  • volume error: relative signed residual (SL is non-conservative → bounded diagnostic, no fitted order)
  • gradient defect \(\||\nabla\psi|-1\|_{L_2}\): global and in the narrow band
  • half-time variants at \(t=T/2\) (maximum stretching): gradient defect (global + band), volume
  • health: \(\min|\nabla\psi|\) in the band (flattening watchdog)

Every number on the following slides regenerates from data/tables/lsl_convergence.csv + lsl_convergence_orders.csv (single pipeline source, mirrored to the paper).

2D convergence — primary errors ($t=T$)

  • CFL \(\tfrac12\): converges — shape \(\approx\)1.1, volume \(\approx\)1.5, band gradient \(\approx\)2.1; monotone over the whole ladder
  • CFL 1: converges on coarse meshes, then turns UP at N\(\ge\)128 — the method is conditionally stable (unlike the CFL-independent quadratic line) → production runs use CFL \(\le\tfrac12\)

2D convergence — the remaining error measures

  • band gradient defect at \(t=T\): what the numerics actually see near the interface
  • half-time (\(t=T/2\), maximum deformation) gradient defect grows toward \(O(1)\) on all meshes — neither SL line maintains the signed-distance property through maximal stretching (transport accuracy converges regardless)

The volume-fraction field — the method made visible

\(\alpha\) at \(t=0,\,T/2,\,T\) (rows) across the ladder (columns), CFL \(\tfrac12\). Black: \(\psi=0\). Dashed red on the \(t=T\) row: the exact reversed circle. The widened \(\alpha\) transition at \(T/2\) on coarse meshes is the first-order transport smearing; the under-resolved tail is lost irreversibly, and refinement recovers the reversed circle at the measured orders — the field-level picture behind the convergence plots.

3D convergence — the headline result

  • 3D shear-poly converges — shape 1.78, band-gradient 2.10 (full ladder)
  • 3D shear-HEX destabilizes — converges over the stable envelope (shape order 1.18, h≥2e−2), then the |∇ψ| defect runs away (→1e5 by N=128); the figures fit + draw the stable envelope only and mark the limit separately

Same conditional-stability limit as 2D CFL 1 (stable-envelope shape order 0.97), here at CFL \(\tfrac12\): the LeVeque–Liovic shear sustains stronger strain than deformation, steepening ∇ψ until the least-squares slope can't damp it. Flow-dependent: 2D vortex (N=256) + 3D deformation (N=160) stay inside it; strong 3D shear on hex does not, past N=80.

The poly face-neighbour stencil's stronger averaging pushes the shear limit past reach — a lower CFL, the face stencil, or band redistancing is needed to go finer on hex.

  • nestedLSQ: the low-cost, memory-light stable linear transport method (CFL \(\le\tfrac12\))
  • UQWLSR: the kinematic-accuracy champion
  • linearTaylor: never for reinit-free advection — but the two-phase workhorse (Part 1)

Stencil averaging, not the polynomial degree, is what makes an SL reconstruction stable: nestedLSQ (linear + stencil) is stable; linearTaylor (linear, single cell) is not.

When to use which

  • Kinematic studies, interface-accuracy-critical work → reconstruction uncachedQuadraticWeightedLeastSquares; — the accuracy champion
  • Low-cost stable kinematic transport → reconstruction quadraticTaylor; at CFL \(\le\tfrac12\) — memory-light, no ceiling
  • Two-phase Navier–Stokes with explicit surface tension → reconstruction linearTaylor; — its dissipation damps the coupled loop, its instability held in check by the short horizon
  • Every way: the switch is ONE dictionary keyword — foot, phase indicator, band, error metrics, pipeline: shared and identical

Permanence under strong stretching (the \(O(1)\) half-time gradient defect) is an open problem for ALL lines — band-limited signed-distance maintenance is the subject of the redistancing line.

Conclusions

  • The linear line turns on ONE choice: nestedLSQ (least-squares linear over the stencil) is a stable, convergent, memory-light transport method — the naive linearTaylor (single-cell Taylor) is unstable in advection and must not be used for it
  • nestedLSQ converges at first-to-second order in every error measure within its stable envelope — 2D vortex (N=256), 3D deformation (N=160, hex+poly), 3D shear-poly — the complete error battery
  • Conditionally + flow-dependently stable: strong 3D shear on hex bounds it at N=80 (unlike the CFL/flow-independent quadratic line) — go finer with a lower CFL, the face stencil, or band redistancing
  • \(O(1)\) memory, no fine-mesh ceiling, local (gradient halo only), robust on polyhedra with the base-class safeguards
  • The lesson: stencil averaging, not polynomial degree, is what makes a semi-Lagrangian reconstruction stable — and it defers, not removes, the steepening limit
