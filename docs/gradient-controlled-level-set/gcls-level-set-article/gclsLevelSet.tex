\title{Gradient control for semi-Lagrangian level sets on unstructured finite-volume
meshes: source terms, a halo-limited velocity extension, and a verification gate}

\author[tuda]{Tomislav Mari\'{c}}
\author[tuda]{Dieter Bothe}
\address[tuda]{Mathematical Modeling and Analysis, TU Darmstadt, Germany}

\begin{abstract}
A level set that starts as a signed distance does not stay one. The normal strain
rate of the flow changes the gradient magnitude $q=|\nabla\psilev|$ along the
trajectories, and after a strong deformation $q$ differs from one by an amount of
order one near the interface. We study two ways to keep $q$ near one without
redistancing, for the collocated finite-volume method of OpenFOAM on unstructured
meshes, with a quadratic semi-Lagrangian level-set transport that is coupled to the
two-phase Navier--Stokes equations: a source term $\psilev F$ in the level-set
equation, with $F$ from a family of gradient-control laws, and a halo-limited
directional velocity extension that is meant to remove the normal strain at the
interface. For each model we give the continuum form, the discretisation on the mesh,
and the result of a verification gate. The gate has an exact one-dimensional case, a
reversed shear flow, and a stationary, a translating and an oscillating droplet at the
water--air density ratio, each on three resolutions, on four MPI ranks, and with a
serial check of the parallel result. Every one of the six tested combinations fails
the gate. The halo-limited extension alone lowers the observed order of the shape
error in the kinematic shear flow from $3.1$ to $1.4$; the source terms raise the
shape error of the shear flow by a factor of $14$ to $2700$ over the production
method, and the linear laws destroy the stationary droplet at the finest resolution.
We show where the failures come from. The extension with a radius of one cell does
not remove the normal strain at the interface: it relocates the strain into a shell
one to two cells from the interface, inside the reconstruction stencils, which the
one-dimensional arm measures. The source step, discretised with a centred gradient
of $q$, a hard band edge and no time-step guard, is linearly unstable at a rate of
order $\mu$ with no flow in the loop: the linear laws read no velocity, and the
measured growth rates on the stationary droplet are of the order of $\mu$ and far
below the capillary rate of the mesh. We name the experiment that decides each
reading, and the order of the next steps: the discretisation of the source step is
repaired and gated on a zero-flow static test before any law and before any coupled
run. A companion article reports two defects of the parallel implementation of the
production method, found by the serial check, and the late instability of the
translating droplet.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

The level-set method represents an interface $\Gamma$ as the zero set of a function
$\psilev$ and moves it with the material equation
$\partial_t\psilev+\bu\cdot\nabla\psilev=0$ \cite{OsherSethian1988}. The zero set
alone defines the interface, so any $\psilev$ with the right zero set is admissible.
In practice the method starts from a signed distance, $|\nabla\psilev|=1$, because a
signed distance gives a well-scaled normal $\nhat=\nabla\psilev/|\nabla\psilev|$, a
curvature $\kappa=\nabla\cdot\nhat$ that a local polynomial fit can evaluate
accurately, and a transition layer of the phase indicator with a fixed physical width.

The transport equation does not keep this property. Along a trajectory the gradient
magnitude $q=|\nabla\psilev|$ obeys
\begin{equation}
  \Dt q = -a\,q,\qquad a=\nhat\cdot(\nabla\bu)\,\nhat,
  \label{eq:qlaw}
\end{equation}
so a flow with a normal strain rate $a\neq0$ at the interface changes $q$, even for
the exact solution \cite{FrickeEtAl2022,BotheFrickeSoga2024}. In the reversed shear
flow of this paper, $q$ departs from one by an amount of order one near the interface
at the time of maximum deformation (Section~\ref{sec:res-baseline}).

The usual remedy is to redistance: to replace $\psilev$ at intervals by the signed
distance to its own zero set \cite{SussmanSmerekaOsher1994,SussmanFatemi1999}.
Redistancing moves the discrete interface, it is a non-local operation, and on
unstructured meshes under MPI domain decomposition it needs either a global solve or
a wide geometric search. Two alternatives keep the transport local. A source term in
the level-set equation can drive $q$ towards one while it leaves the zero set in
place \cite{FrickeEtAl2022,BotheFrickeSoga2024}. A velocity extension can transport
$\psilev$ with a velocity whose normal strain vanishes on $\Gamma$, so that
\eqref{eq:qlaw} gives $\Dt q=0$ there \cite{AdalsteinssonSethian1999}. Two technical
dossiers \cite{BotheDossierHalo2026,BotheMaricSogaDossier2026} propose a family of
source laws and a halo-limited extension that samples the velocity only inside a
radius of one cell, so that it needs no closest-point search and no halo wider than
one layer of cells.

This paper implements both ideas in the leia library of OpenFOAM
\cite{WellerEtAl1998,LEIA} and tests them. The target is a method for arbitrary
unstructured meshes: every operator is local, uses compact stencils and runs under
MPI domain decomposition. The level set is transported by a quadratic
semi-Lagrangian scheme \cite{StaniforthCote1991} that is coupled to the one-field
two-phase Navier--Stokes equations. We call this coupled method the baseline.

The contributions are the following.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item The continuum form and the finite-volume discretisation of a family of
  gradient-control source laws and of a halo-limited velocity extension for a
  semi-Lagrangian level set on unstructured meshes (Sections~\ref{sec:model}--\ref{sec:fv}).
  \item A verification gate for level-set methods in two-phase flow: five arms, three
  resolutions per arm, the observed orders of an error vector, and a serial check of
  every parallel result (Section~\ref{sec:gate}).
  \item The results of six combinations against the baseline. All six fail
  (Section~\ref{sec:results}). The discussion gives, for each failure, the discrete
  mechanism with its mark (measured, derived, or a hypothesis with the experiment that
  decides it), and the order of the next steps (Section~\ref{sec:discussion}).
  \item Two findings on the baseline itself, reported in full in the companion
  article \cite{maric2026sl}: two defects of its parallel implementation, which the
  serial check found and which we corrected, and the late instability of the
  translating droplet, which the outlet triggers and which has a slower interior growth
  behind it (Sections~\ref{sec:fv-parallel} and~\ref{sec:res-translating}).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Level-set transport and the gradient magnitude}
\label{sec:levelset}

Let $\psilev(\bx,t)$ satisfy
\begin{equation}
  \partial_t\psilev+\bu\cdot\nabla\psilev = 0,\qquad
  \psilev(\bx,0)=\pm\operatorname{dist}(\bx,\Gamma(0)),
  \label{eq:transport}
\end{equation}
negative inside the liquid. The gradient $\mathbf g=\nabla\psilev$ satisfies
$\Dt\mathbf g=-(\nabla\bu)^{\mathsf T}\mathbf g$, and with $q=|\mathbf g|$ and
$\nhat=\mathbf g/q$ this gives \eqref{eq:qlaw}. Only the symmetric part
$\mathbf D=\frac12(\nabla\bu+\nabla\bu^{\mathsf T})$ enters, $a=\nhat\cdot\mathbf D\,\nhat$,
and $|a|\le\sigma=|\mathbf D|_F$. A uniform translation and a rigid rotation have
$\mathbf D=0$ and keep $q$. A shear flow has $a\neq0$ on most of the interface and
changes $q$ everywhere.

Near the interface, $q$ enters a two-phase solver in three places. The phase
indicator of the baseline fits a plane to $\psilev$ and fills each cell with the
closed form of \cite{DetrixheAslam2016}; it does not assume $q=1$. The curvature comes
from the second derivatives of a local quadratic fit and from the offset of the cell
centre to the zero set, $d=\psilev/q$; it is exact for any $q$ in the continuum. The
reconstruction at the departure point, however, fits a quadratic polynomial to the
cell values, and its error grows with the second derivatives of $\psilev$, which grow
where $q$ varies along the normal. The aim of gradient control is therefore modest:
keep $q$ in a band around one, for example $0.9\le q\le1.1$, near the interface, not
enforce $q=1$.

\section{Gradient-control source terms}
\label{sec:model}

\subsection{The modified level-set equation}
\label{sec:model-source}
The source-term route replaces \eqref{eq:transport} by
\begin{equation}
  \partial_t\psilev+\bu\cdot\nabla\psilev = \psilev\,F,
  \label{eq:sourceEq}
\end{equation}
with a scalar coefficient $F(\bx,t)$ [1/s]. Three properties follow directly.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item The zero set is invariant: along a trajectory $\Dt\psilev=\psilev F$, so a
  point with $\psilev=0$ keeps $\psilev=0$, and the interface moves with $\bu$ as
  before.
  \item On the interface the gradient magnitude obeys
  \begin{equation}
    \Dt q = q\,(F-a).
    \label{eq:designlaw}
  \end{equation}
  \item If $F=0$ at $q=1$ and $a=0$, a signed distance is a fixed point.
\end{enumerate}
The source therefore acts on the gradient and not on the interface position, but only
in the continuum. Section~\ref{sec:disc-source} shows how the discrete zero set moves.

\subsection{The law family}
\label{sec:model-laws}
The coefficient has the form
\begin{equation}
  F = w(q)\,a + G(q,\sigma),\qquad
  a=\nhat\cdot\mathbf D\,\nhat,\qquad
  \sigma=|\mathbf D|_F,
  \label{eq:Fdef}
\end{equation}
with a strain weight $w$ and a rate law $G$ with $G(1,\sigma)=0$. The weight
$w=1$ cancels the normal strain exactly in \eqref{eq:designlaw} (the SDPLS source of
\cite{FrickeEtAl2022}); $w=0$ leaves it to $G$. All candidates of this paper use
$w=0$. Table~\ref{tab:laws} lists the laws of the library; with $e=q-1$ and
$\zeta=(q^2-1)/2$, the linear pair has the slope $-\mu$ at $q=1$ for the same $\mu$.
Three laws enter the gate.

\paragraph{Linear laws} \texttt{linearQ}, $G=-\mu(q-1)$, and \texttt{linearZ},
$G=-\mu(q^2-1)/2$. With $w=0$ and a steady strain, \eqref{eq:designlaw} has the fixed
point $q^\ast=1-a/\mu+O(a^2/\mu^2)$: a finite rate leaves an offset of order $a/\mu$,
so $\mu$ must exceed the strain rates of the flow. The gate sets
$\mu=M_\mu/T_\mathrm{ref}$ with $M_\mu=1$ and the time scale $T_\mathrm{ref}$ of each
arm (Table~\ref{tab:candidates}).

\paragraph{Soft wall} \texttt{softWall},
\begin{equation}
  G=-C_\kappa\sqrt{\sigma^2+\varepsilon_D^2}\,
     \tanh\!\left[\gamma\left(\frac{q-1}{\delta_s}\right)^{p}\right],
  \qquad p\ \text{odd}.
  \label{eq:softwall}
\end{equation}
Inside the band $|q-1|<\delta_s$ the wall is almost inactive; at $|q-1|=\delta_s$ it
reaches $\tanh\gamma$ of its full strength. The gate uses $C_\kappa=1.25$,
$\delta_s=0.08$, $p=5$, $\gamma=\operatorname{artanh}0.9$ and $\varepsilon_D=0$, so
$|G|=1.125\,\sigma\ge|a|$ at $|q-1|=\delta_s$: outside the band the wall is at least as
strong as any normal strain, and $q$ cannot leave $[1-\delta_s,1+\delta_s]$ far. The
wall scales with the local strain-rate norm $\sigma$. Where $\sigma=0$ it is off,
whatever the value of $q$.

\section{Velocity extension}
\label{sec:extension}

\subsection{Why an extension controls the gradient}
If the level set is transported with a velocity $\bu_E$ instead of $\bu$, with
$\bu_E=\bu$ on $\Gamma$ and $\nhat\cdot(\nabla\bu_E)\nhat=0$ on $\Gamma$, the interface
moves as before and \eqref{eq:qlaw} gives $\Dt q=0$ on $\Gamma$: a signed distance
keeps $q=1$ at the interface. The classical construction extends $\bu$ from the
interface constant along the normals, $\bu_E(\bx)=\bu(\bx_\Gamma(\bx))$ with the
closest point $\bx_\Gamma$ \cite{AdalsteinssonSethian1999}. It needs a closest-point
search and a band of cells around $\Gamma$. The reference candidate FP0 uses the
closest-point extension of leia, which is a Newton search on the local fits with a
fall-back to a steady extension equation.

\subsection{The halo-limited extension}
\label{sec:model-hl}
The halo-limited extension \cite{BotheDossierHalo2026} samples the velocity at a point
towards the interface that never leaves a radius $R$. With a signed nominal distance
$d$ and the unit direction $\mathbf e$ from the interface towards $\bx$,
\begin{equation}
  S_R(d)=d\left[1+\left(\frac{d}{R}\right)^{2m}\right]^{-1/(2m)},\qquad
  \mathbf Y=\bx-S_R(d)\,\mathbf e,\qquad
  w=\left(\frac{S_R(d)}{d}\right)^{\beta},
  \label{eq:hl}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  \bu_H(\bx)=(1-w)\,\bu(\bx)+w\,\bu(\mathbf Y).
  \label{eq:uH}
\end{equation}
The travel $S_R$ is odd, $S_R(0)=0$, $S_R'(0)=1$ and $|S_R|<R$. On the interface
$\mathbf Y=\bx$ and $w=1$, and the normal derivative of $\mathbf Y$ vanishes, so
$\nhat\cdot(\nabla\bu_H)\nhat=0$ there. Far from the interface $w\to0$ and $\bu_H\to\bu$.
The level-set realisation is $d=\psilev/q$ and $\mathbf e=\nhat$. The gate uses
$R=h$, $m=2$ and $\beta=1$: every sample lies within one cell of its point.

\section{Discretisation in OpenFOAM}
\label{sec:fv}

\subsection{Finite-volume setting}
The unknowns are cell averages, stored at the cell centres $\bx_c$ of a mesh of
arbitrary polyhedra. A face $f$ has the centre $\bx_f$, the area vector
$\mathbf S_f$ that points from its owner to its neighbour cell, and the volumetric flux
$\Phi_f\approx\bu_f\cdot\mathbf S_f$ that the pressure projection makes discretely
divergence-free. Under MPI domain decomposition each rank holds a part of the mesh; a
face between two ranks is a processor face that both ranks hold, each with its own cell
as owner. $h_c$ is the smallest distance from $\bx_c$ to the centre of a face
neighbour, processor neighbours included, so it does not depend on the decomposition.

\subsection{Semi-Lagrangian transport (the baseline)}
\label{sec:fv-sl}
The level set is constant along characteristics, so the update is an assignment at
every cell centre,
\begin{equation}
  \psilev^{n+1}(\bx_c)=\psilev^n(\bx_d),\qquad
  \bx_d=\bx_c-\frac{\Delta t}{2}\left(3\bu^n-\bu^{n-1}\right)
        +\frac{\Delta t^2}{2}\left(\bu^n\cdot\nabla\right)\bu^n,
  \label{eq:sl}
\end{equation}
with the trace velocity $\bu^n$ at the cell centres. The coupled solver advances the
interface before its momentum solve, so the foot point uses the two available time
levels; the Adams--Bashforth centring keeps it second order in time. The trace velocity
is the reconstruction of the pressure-corrected face flux,
\begin{equation}
  \bu_c=\Big(\sum_f\frac{\mathbf S_f\mathbf S_f}{|\mathbf S_f|}\Big)^{-1}
        \sum_f\frac{\mathbf S_f}{|\mathbf S_f|}\,\Phi_f,
  \label{eq:reconstruct}
\end{equation}
the OpenFOAM operator \texttt{fvc::reconstruct}. The value $\psilev^n(\bx_d)$ comes from
a quadratic weighted least-squares fit over the cell--point--cell stencil of the cell
that holds the foot point: with $\mathbf d_i=\bx_i-\bx_c$ and the basis
$\mathbf b(\mathbf d)=(d_a,\tfrac12d_a^2,d_ad_b)_{a<b}$ without a constant term,
\begin{equation}
  R_c(\bx_c+\mathbf d)=\psilev_c+\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf b(\mathbf d),\qquad
  \mathbf a=\arg\min\sum_{i}\frac{\left(R_c(\bx_i)-\psilev_i\right)^2}{|\mathbf d_i|^2}.
  \label{eq:fit}
\end{equation}
The fit gives the gradient $\mathbf g$ and the Hessian $\mathbf H$ of $\psilev$ at
$\bx_c$, from which the curvature is computed (Section~\ref{sec:fv-coupling}).

\subsection{The source step}
\label{sec:fv-source}
After the characteristic step the source integrates $\Dt\psilev=\psilev F$ exactly for
one step with $F$ frozen,
\begin{equation}
  \psilev_c^{n+1}=\psilev_c^{A}\exp\!\left(\Delta t\,F_c\right),
  \label{eq:expUpdate}
\end{equation}
where $\psilev^A$ is the arrival value of \eqref{eq:sl}. $F_c$ comes from
\eqref{eq:Fdef} with $q_c=|\nabla_h\psilev^A|_c$ from a least-squares gradient of the
arrival field and $\mathbf D_c$ from the gradient of the trace velocity. The source acts
only in the band $|\psilev_c|/q_c\le3h_c$ and is exactly zero outside it; $|\Delta
tF_c|$ is clamped at $30$ and every clamp is counted. The update never changes the sign
of a cell value, so no cell changes phase. The Eulerian solvers of leia have the same
law family through their SDPLS source \cite{FrickeEtAl2022}; that line is not part of
this gate.

\subsection{The halo-limited extension on the mesh}
\label{sec:fv-hl}
The extension enters as a correction of the pressure-corrected flux,
\begin{equation}
  \Phi^H_f=\Phi_f+w_f\left[\bu_h(\mathbf Y_f)-\bu_h(\bx_f)\right]\cdot\mathbf S_f,
  \qquad R_f=\min(h_P,h_N),
  \label{eq:hlFlux}
\end{equation}
so that $\Phi^H=\Phi$ where $w=0$ and on every physical boundary. The direction uses
the least-squares gradient $\mathbf g_f$ of $\psilev$ interpolated to the face, with
$d_f=\psilev_f/\sqrt{Q_2}$ and $\mathbf e_f=\mathbf g_f/\sqrt{Q_2}$, where
$Q_2=q^2+\zeta_r(q^2)$ and $\zeta_r(z)=(1-4z)^3/4$ for $z<1/4$ and zero above; so $d=\psilev/q$
and $\mathbf e=\nhat$ hold exactly for $q\ge0.5$. The sampler $\bu_h$ is the mean of
the owner's and the neighbour's local quadratic models of each velocity component, each
a weighted least-squares fit over the cell--point--cell stencil, evaluated at the same
two points $\mathbf Y_f$ and $\bx_f$; the constant part of each model cancels in the
difference. On a processor face each rank evaluates its own cell's model, and the two
corrections are averaged in one orientation, so both sides carry exactly opposite
fluxes. In the semi-Lagrangian solvers the extension enters through the trace velocity:
\eqref{eq:reconstruct} is applied to $\Phi^H$ instead of $\Phi$ (the setting
\texttt{traceFlux extension}).

\subsection{Coupling to the two-phase flow (the baseline)}
\label{sec:fv-coupling}
The flow is the one-field incompressible Navier--Stokes system with
$\rho=\alpha\rho_1+(1-\alpha)\rho_2$ and $\mu=\alpha\mu_1+(1-\alpha)\mu_2$, solved with
the PIMPLE algorithm, three outer correctors, and the level set re-advected from
$\psilev^n$ on every outer corrector with the current velocity iterate. Both time
derivatives, of momentum and of density, use the second-order backward scheme. The
momentum flux is the rhoLENT mass flux \cite{LiuEtAl2023}: an auxiliary density
equation $\partial_t\rho+\nabla\cdot(\rho_f\Phi)=0$ is solved on every outer corrector
with the face density $\rho_f=\alpha_f\rho_1+(1-\alpha_f)\rho_2$ of the upwind cell's
reconstructed plane, bounded to $[\rho_2,\rho_1]$, and $\rho$ is reset from $\alpha$
after the loop. The surface tension is the balanced-force face flux
$\sigma_s\kappa_f(\nabla\alpha)_f\cdot\mathbf S_f$ \cite{Francois2006,Popinet2009} with
the cell curvature of the fit, corrected per cell to the interface by the
parallel-surface inverse $\kappa=(\kappa_d-2Kd)/(1-d\kappa_d+Kd^2)$ with $K=0$ in 2D,
and interpolated linearly to the faces. The time step is fixed at
$\Delta t=0.23\,\Delta t_\sigma$ with the capillary limit
$\Delta t_\sigma=\sqrt{(\rho_1+\rho_2)h^3/(2\pi\sigma_s)}$ \cite{Brackbill1992}. No
redistancing, no filter and no volume correction is applied.

\subsection{Consistency under domain decomposition}
\label{sec:fv-parallel}
The serial check of the gate (Section~\ref{sec:gate-verdict}) found two places where the
baseline handled a processor face as a boundary face of one cell -- the face density of the
mass flux differed between the two sides of a processor face, and the droplet diagnostics
ignored processor faces -- and after both corrections the four-rank run agrees with the
serial run to $3.5\times10^{-7}$ or better in every column, as the companion
article~\cite{maric2026sl} reports with the measurement and the corrected code. Every
parallel result of the baseline that was computed before the corrections carries both
errors; serial runs are unchanged, bit for bit. The same investigation found that the
Eulerian two-phase solver of leia kept the density, the mass flux and the face viscosity
at their initial values; it now shares the mass-flux code of the semi-Lagrangian solver,
and with it the translating droplet travels at the stream speed to $10^{-4}$ instead of at
\SI{42}{\percent} of it \cite{maric2026sl}.

\section{The method gate}
\label{sec:gate}

One 2D study tests every method. It reuses the cases and the workflow of the
production studies; a method enters only as a set of case entries, so a method that
cannot be selected at run time cannot be tested.

\subsection{Arms and resolutions}
\label{sec:gate-arms}

Table~\ref{tab:cases} their parameters. The exact 1D arm transports a plane in a uniaxial
strain with the closed form $q=e^{-\alpha t}$ and runs first; the other arms start only
after it passes. The reversed shear flow returns the interface to its initial position at
$t=T$. The three droplet arms are coupled to the flow with water and air. The number of
cells doubles per rung, and the first rung resolves the droplet radius with ten cells.
Every arm runs on four MPI ranks. The translating arm ends at \SI{0.05}{s}, before the
late instability of Section~\ref{sec:res-translating}.

\subsection{The error vector}
\label{sec:gate-metrics}
Each arm reports a vector of errors, never one number, and every entry is an $L^2$ or an
$L^1$ norm; a maximum norm is not reported, because a single-cell extremum does not
converge on an unstructured mesh.
\begin{itemize}
  \item Shape: for the shear flow the relative $L^1$ difference of $\alpha$ to its
  initial field at $t=T$; for the droplets the $L^2$ norm of the radial distance of the
  zero-set crossings to the exact circle (centred at $\bx_0+\mathbf U_0t$), divided by $R$.
  \item Volume: $|V(t)/V(0)-1|$ at $T$ (and at $T/2$).
  \item Band gradient error: the $L^2$ norm of $q-1$ over the cells of the interface
  band, with the unlimited least-squares gradient. For the reversed shear flow it is read
  at $T/2$, where the deformation is largest; at $T$ the reversal cancels it.
  \item Spurious current: the volume-weighted $L^2$ and $L^1$ norms of $\bu-\mathbf U_0$
  (stationary and translating droplets only: in the oscillating droplet the velocity is
  the physical oscillation).
  \item Pressure jump: the difference of the mean pressure over the pure phases against
  $\sigma_s/R$; curvature: the $L^2$ norm of $\kappa-1/R$ in the band, relative to $1/R$.
  \item Travel: the centroid displacement against $U_0T$.
  \item Oscillation: the period and the damping rate of the mode-2 amplitude. These have
  no exact value in the discretised problem; their convergence is judged by the
  procedure of \cite{Celik2008}.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Orders and verdict}
\label{sec:gate-verdict}
For a quantity with the exact value zero the observed order is the least-squares slope
over the three rungs. A candidate passes if all of the following hold.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Every case of the candidate completes where the baseline completes.
  \item No entry of the vector is more than \SI{10}{\percent} above the baseline at the
  finest rung, and no least-squares order is more than $0.3$ below the baseline's.
  \item The candidate meets its own target: here the band gradient error of the shear
  arm at the finest rung is at most $0.8$ times the baseline's.
  \item The serial checks pass: the coarsest shear rung repeated on one and on eight
  ranks agrees with the four-rank run to $10^{-10}$, and the coarsest translating rung
  repeated on one rank agrees in the error-vector columns to $10^{-5}$. A check whose
  four-rank reference did not complete is not comparable.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{The baseline}
\label{sec:res-baseline}
Table~\ref{tab:baseline} gives the error vector of the baseline.

\paragraph{Shear} The shape error converges at order $3.1$ and the volume error at
order $3.6$. The band gradient error at $T/2$ is $1.6$ at every rung: the flow itself
moves $q$ away from one by an amount of order one (Figure~\ref{fig:shear}b), and the
reversal returns it to $10^{-3}$ at $t=T$. This is the effect that gradient control is
meant to remove.

\paragraph{Stationary droplet} Shape, band gradient, pressure jump and curvature
converge at order $1.9$ to $2.8$. The spurious current is small,
$4\times10^{-7}$\,m/s at $N=200$, but it is larger at finer rungs at the fixed time $T$
(order $-3.4$); the droplet reaches equilibrium more slowly on the finer mesh.

\paragraph{Translating droplet} Shape, volume and travel converge at order $3.1$ to
$3.3$ and the spurious current at order $1.1$. The curvature error does not converge:
it stays between $2.4$ and \SI{3.6}{\percent} of $1/R$ at all three rungs, while the
same estimator converges at order $1.9$ on the stationary droplet. The band gradient
error converges only at order $0.8$.

\paragraph{Oscillating droplet} At $N=100$ and $142$ the band gradient error stays near
$0.1$ over ten oscillation periods. At $N=200$ the gradient drift of the baseline without
redistancing turns unstable within ten periods -- the band gradient error grows
exponentially after $t=\SI{0.05}{s}$, and the period and the damping rate of the finest
rung break the convergence of both quantities \cite{maric2026sl} -- so the oscillating arm
at $N=200$ cannot carry a verdict.

\subsection{Soft wall alone (S1)}
\label{sec:res-s1}
On the stationary droplet S1 agrees with the baseline to four significant digits or
better in every entry. The wall \eqref{eq:softwall} scales with $\sigma$, and the
stationary droplet has $\sigma\approx0$ (its only velocity is the spurious current), so
the source is almost off. Wherever the flow strains the interface, S1
changes the result strongly. In the shear flow it lowers the band gradient error to
$0.71$ of the baseline, which meets its target, and it raises the shape error $410$
times, with an order of $0.06$. The translating droplet diverges at $N=142$. The volume of
the oscillating droplet grows by a factor of $22$. The serial check of the translating
arm fails: one rank and four ranks differ by \SI{76}{\percent} in the spurious current,
against $3.5\times10^{-7}$ for the baseline, so the soft wall amplifies the round-off of
the decomposition. A source that is invariant for the zero set in the continuum
therefore moves the discrete zero set; Section~\ref{sec:disc-source} gives the mechanism.

\subsection{Velocity extensions alone (HL0, FP0)}
\label{sec:res-hl0}
HL0 leaves the stationary droplet almost unchanged: the shape, pressure-jump and
curvature errors change by less than \SI{5}{\percent}, and only the volume error rises,
from $2.2\times10^{-8}$ to $2.0\times10^{-7}$. The extension acts only where the velocity
varies. In the shear flow it keeps the band gradient error at $0.83$ of the
baseline, which misses the target, and it raises the shape error $14$ times at
$N=136$; the order falls from $3.1$ to $1.4$ (Figure~\ref{fig:shear}a). This is the
kinematic arm, with a smooth prescribed velocity and no force, so the loss of order
comes from the transport and not from the coupling. In the coupled arms the extension
destroys the level set: on the oscillating droplet the band gradient error reaches
$2\times10^{7}$ and the volume error $6.7$, and on the translating droplet the shape
error at $N=200$ is $64$ times the baseline's. Its serial checks pass.

The closest-point reference FP0 behaves like HL0 in the shear flow: $34$ times the shape
error and order $1.4$. It is the only candidate whose kinematic serial check fails
(\SI{45}{\percent} between one and four ranks): its closest-point search depends on the
decomposition at the processor boundaries.

\subsection{Linear laws with the extension (HL1q, HL1z)}
\label{sec:res-hl1}
The linear laws do what they are designed for on the gradient and fail on everything
else. HL1q lowers the band gradient error of the shear flow five times, to $0.20$ of the
baseline; its shape error is $173$ times the baseline's and does not converge (order
$0.02$). HL1z has the same slope at $q=1$ but lets the band gradient error grow to
$10.5$, and its shape error is $1.65$. On the stationary droplet both destroy the
interface: the spurious current grows from about $10^{-6}$\,m/s to $0.2$ and
$0.5$\,m/s by $t=\SI{0.035}{s}$ (Figure~\ref{fig:droplets}a), and at $T$ the shape error is $3.8$ and
$3.7$ times $R$ with a volume error of $76$ and \SI{84}{\percent}. These runs complete,
so the completion criterion alone would not detect them; the error vector does.

\SI{0.05}{s}, and the volume of the oscillating droplet grows by a factor of $16$.

\section{Discussion: where the problems are}
\label{sec:discussion}

The gate separates three classes of problems: the discretisation of the source step,
the extension, and the baseline's own limits. For each we state the evidence, the
mechanism it points to, and the experiment that decides it. Each mechanism carries a
mark: \emph{measured} (a number in the archive of \ref{app:data}), \emph{derived} (a
closed form), or \emph{hypothesis} (an experiment is named). The technical report of
the theme folder (\path{gcls-technical-report/gclsTechnicalReport.tex}) gives the
derivations and the full experiment ladder.

\subsection{The source step: a centred gradient makes its own map unstable}
\label{sec:disc-source}
The linear laws read no velocity. The law family is $F=w(q^2)\,a+G(q^2,\sigma)$ with
$w=0$ for every candidate, and \texttt{linearQ} and \texttt{linearZ} have no
$\sigma$, so for HL1q and HL1z the loop is $\psilev\to q\to F\to\psilev$ with no flow in
it. The source step reads $q_c$ from a centred least-squares gradient of the arrival
field. Linearise the update \eqref{eq:expUpdate} about the signed distance,
$\psilev_c=d_c+\delta_c$: $\delta_c\leftarrow\delta_c-\mu\Delta t\,d_c\,(\nhat\cdot
\nabla_h\delta)_c$. For the distance-modulated checkerboard $\delta_c=(-1)^cE\,d_c$ the
central difference gives $(\nhat\cdot\nabla_h\delta)_c=(-1)^{c+1}E$, so
$\delta_c\leftarrow\delta_c(1+\mu\Delta t)$: an exact eigenmode with growth rate
$\mu$, independent of $\Delta t$, $h$ and the flow (in 2D the mode is $(-1)^{i+j}E\,d$;
derived). A one-sided gradient towards the interface damps the same mode at $2\mu d/h$.
A pure checkerboard without the $d$ modulation is invisible to a centred difference,
which is why the band gradient error of the gate cannot see the growing mode. The
stationary droplet exposes the mode because at $\bu\approx0$ the semi-Lagrangian step
is the identity and nothing damps it; at CFL $0.5$ the quadratic fit halves a
checkerboard per step, which hides it in the shear arm.

The histories of the stationary droplet at $N=200$ agree with this reading (all
measured). HL1q's curvature error e-folds at about \SI{290}{s^{-1}} over $10$ to
\SI{20}{ms} (about \SI{460}{s^{-1}} over $2$ to \SI{10}{ms}) and its spurious current at
$320$ to \SI{350}{s^{-1}}, against $\mu=1/T_\mathrm{ref}=\SI{270}{s^{-1}}$ and far below
the capillary rate of the mesh, $\sqrt{\sigma k^3/\rho}\sim\SI{e5}{s^{-1}}$. HL1q and
HL1z agree to three digits until $t=\SI{0.02}{s}$: their linearisations coincide. The
curvature error leads the velocity: at \SI{4.3}{ms} it is $10.6$ times the baseline's
while the spurious current is $1.4$ times, and at that time the band gradient error is
below the baseline's ($3.2\times10^{-4}$ against $4.4\times10^{-4}$), because the smooth
part of the initial $q$ error decays as the law intends while the mode grows unseen.
$\min q$ and $\max q$ leave $[0.99,1.01]$ symmetrically ($0.83$ and $1.17$ at
\SI{20}{ms}, $0.33$ and $2.29$ at \SI{30}{ms}): an oscillatory cell-scale mode, not a
drift. The linearised map predicts a rate of exactly $\mu$; the measured excess is not
explained, and the experiment that decides the reading is the $\mu$ ladder: HL1q on the
stationary droplet at $M_\mu=0.1$ and $10$ must survive \SI{0.1}{s} and die by about
\SI{3.5}{ms}, with a rate proportional to $\mu$. The cheapest discriminator needs no
flow at all: a signed-distance circle with $\bu=0$ and the source on, at
$\mu\in\{27,270,2700\}\,\si{s^{-1}}$; the prediction is a growth of
$\max|\psilev_c-d_c|$ as $e^{\mu t}$ with the pattern $(-1)^{i+j}d$.

Two further effects are of order one in $h$ (derived). A law that reaches its target
multiplies $|\psilev|$ inside the band by $e^{\int F\,dt}=q_\mathrm{far}/q_\mathrm{target}
\approx2$ to $3$ in the shear arm and by one outside, so $|\psilev|$ becomes non-monotone
at the hard band edge $|\psilev|/q=3h$, independent of $h$, and spurious crossings
follow (measured: HL1z reaches a band error of $10.5$ and a volume error of $1.6$ at
$T$). And the soft wall's slope $|dG/dq|$ reaches $40\sigma$ at $|q-1|\approx0.07$ for
the parameters of S1, so $\Delta t\,dG/dq\approx0.5$ per step in the shear arm: a nearly
discontinuous per-cell map, whose signature is the \SI{76}{\percent} serial-check
failure of S1 that HL1q and HL1z do not share.

The exponential update also moves the discrete zero set. With two neighbours at
distance $h$ across the interface, $\psilev_A=-a<0$ and $\psilev_B=b>0$, the linear
crossing lies at the fraction $a/(a+b)$ from $A$, and after one step with $F_A\neq F_B$
it moves by
\begin{equation}
  \delta = h\,\frac{ab}{(a+b)^2}\,\Delta t\,(F_A-F_B)+O(\Delta t^2).
  \label{eq:zeroShift}
\end{equation}
This shift is first order in $h$: for the linear laws $F_A-F_B=-\mu(q_A-q_B)$, and
summed over the shear run it is at most about $0.2h$. The measured shape errors of the
source candidates are of order one in $h$ and flat in $N$ (HL1q: $0.108$, $0.117$,
$0.107$; S1: $0.265$, $0.327$, $0.254$), so \eqref{eq:zeroShift} is a secondary
contribution; the first version of this paper held it responsible, which we correct
here. The repair follows from the mechanisms: a one-sided gradient of $q$ with the
coupled-patch neighbours, a smooth taper instead of the hard band edge, and a
time-step guard $\mu\,3h\,\Delta t/h<1/2$; then $F$ evaluated at the algebraic foot
$\bx_c-d\,\nhat$ and copied along the normal, if the $O(h)$ shift is still measurable.

\subsection{The halo-limited extension relocates the strain}
\label{sec:disc-hl}
The unit gates of the extension pass: for an affine and for a divergence-free quadratic
velocity the face correction equals \eqref{eq:hlFlux} with the exact velocity at the exact
sample point to $2\times10^{-16}$. The first rung above them, the kinematic shear flow,
fails at order $1.4$. The reason is a property of the model, not of one operator
(derived). With the capped travel $S=c\,d$ and the weight $w=c$,
$c=(1+(d/R)^4)^{-1/4}$, the extension transmits the normal strain
$K(d/R)=1-(1-t^4)(1+t^4)^{-3/2}$, $t=d/R$: zero on the interface, $1.00$ at $R$, $1.27$
at $1.5R$ and $1.1$ at $3R$, with $\int_0^D(1-K)\,dd\to R^2/D$. The strain is displaced
by about $1.5h$ into a shell with a \SI{27}{\percent} overshoot, and with $R=h$ the shell
sits inside the band and the reconstruction stencils. The exact one-dimensional arm
measures it: HL0's band mean of $q$ is $0.59$, $0.50$, $0.51$, $0.46$ at $N=32$ to $256$,
against $e^{-1}=0.37$ for the baseline and $0.49$ for the band average of $e^{-K}$. The
pre-registered read-out of HL0 (``zero normal strain on the interface, so the band
error falls'') conflated the interface with the band. The $q$ profile then varies by
order one over one cell, and the quadratic reconstruction at the feet is first order
whatever the trace velocity: the HL0-to-baseline ratio of the shape error grows $4.3$,
$8.4$, $13.7$ over the three shear rungs (measured).

The face-to-cell reconstruction of the corrected flux, which the first version of this
paper held responsible, is one $O(h|\nabla\bu|)$ contribution among others: it is not
necessary, because FP0 has no shell (its extended flux is the interpolated cell field)
and loses the same order, $1.38$, and it is not sufficient, because HL0's excess volume
error appears by $t=\SI{0.02}{s}$, before any kink in $q$ exists (measured). The
experiment that bounds its share traces the kinematic shear arm with the extension's own
cell velocity $\bu_H(\bx_c)$ of \eqref{eq:uH}: the constant should fall and the order
stay below two. The radius ladder that the first version proposed is blocked by
construction: the sampler evaluates the models of the face's own cells, so $R>h$ needs
a sampler that locates the cell containing the sample point with a halo of two to three
layers, which is the halo the design set out to avoid.

The coupled arms fail far more strongly than the kinematic one, for two different
reasons. The translating droplet has no physical gradient to fit across (rigid
translation), and HL0 fails $64$ times there: the sampler reads the cell-scale spurious
current and injects it into the trace flux unprojected, while the baseline traces the
reconstructed projected flux, the smoothest velocity the solver has. HL0 tracks the
baseline to $t\approx\SI{0.02}{s}$, then its curvature error and its velocity grow
together at about $235$ and \SI{210}{s^{-1}} (measured). On the oscillating droplet the
air-side boundary layer, $\sqrt{\nu_\mathrm{air}T}\approx8h$ at $N=200$, gives a normal
strain of about \SI{250}{s^{-1}}, and the sampler fits one quadratic model across the
kink; HL0's band gradient error grows at about \SI{480}{s^{-1}} there (measured), five
times the physical strain, so the fit across the jump is the plausible reading
(hypothesis). A density and viscosity ratio of one cannot separate the two readings,
because it shrinks the spurious current and the kink at once; the experiment that
separates them samples the reconstructed projected flux instead of the cell velocity in
the extension: the translating droplet should improve and the oscillating droplet should
not.

\subsection{The linear laws: what they showed, and the order of the next steps}
HL1q shows the conflict of the linear laws most clearly: it lowers the band gradient error
five times and raises the shape error $173$ times. The gate therefore did not test the
laws; it tested a discretisation of the source step that fails before any law can act,
and an extension that cannot protect the band at $R=h$. HL1z, with the same slope at
$q=1$, adds the term $-\mu q^2/2$, which is unbounded for the large $q$ that the band edge
produces, and grows the gradient error to $10.5$. The next steps follow in a fixed order:
the closed forms of the candidates in the one-dimensional arm, so that its target criterion
is real; the zero-flow static test of the repaired source step at three values of $\mu$,
with and without the band; the dossiers' rigid-motion and homogeneous-strain tests with
$q_0\neq1$; then the stationary droplet at $M_\mu\in\{0.1,1,10\}$ with the prediction
``no growth at a rate of order $\mu$''. No candidate re-enters the coupled gate before the
kinematic ladder passes at the baseline's order.

\subsection{The baseline's own limits}
Three results of the baseline limit what any candidate can show against it
\cite{maric2026sl}.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item The curvature of the moving interface does not converge: \SI{2.4}{} to
  \SI{3.6}{\percent} at all rungs of the translating arm, while the same estimator
  converges on the stationary droplet. This agrees with the earlier finding that the
  curvature estimate is the source of the spurious currents of the translating droplet.
  \item The gradient drift of the oscillating droplet turns unstable at $N=200$ within ten
  periods. This is the drift that gradient control is meant to remove, so it is the case
  on which a working candidate must first show a gain.
  \item The translating droplet grows slowly in the interior and fast near the outlet.
  A gate that runs longer than \SI{0.05}{s} needs a longer box.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusions}

We implemented a family of gradient-control source terms and a halo-limited velocity
extension for a quadratic semi-Lagrangian level set on unstructured finite-volume meshes,
and tested six combinations against the production method in a verification gate with
three resolutions per arm and a serial check of every parallel result. All six fail, and
the gate shows where. The source step, as discretised, is linearly unstable at a rate of
order $\mu$ with no flow in the loop: a centred gradient of $q$ turns the cell-scale mode
$(-1)^{i+j}d$ into an eigenmode of the update, a hard band edge puts an order-one jump into
$|\psilev|$, and the soft wall's slope makes its map nearly discontinuous. The
halo-limited extension with a radius of one cell relocates the normal strain into a shell
one to two cells from the interface instead of removing it, and lowers the transport order
of a smooth kinematic flow from three to one and a half. The gate itself has two blind
spots, which we repair next: the one-dimensional arm has no closed form for a candidate
with a source or an extension, and the centred band gradient metric cannot see the mode.
The laws themselves were not tested, because the discretisation failed first. The next
campaign repairs the source step (a one-sided gradient, a smooth taper, a time-step
guard), gates it on a zero-flow static test and on rigid-motion and homogeneous-strain
tests, and only then runs the stationary droplet at three values of $\mu$; the extension
is abandoned at $R=h$. The technical report of the theme folder carries the assessment
with the mark of every claim and the experiment ladder with its pre-registered
predictions.

\section{Data and reproducibility}
\label{app:data}
Every number of this paper comes from one folder of secondary data,
\archive, named after the version stamp of the leia libraries that produced the gate
results. The folder is regenerated from the raw output by
\path{figures/make_archive.py}; the figures and the tables of this paper are generated
from it by \path{figures/make_result_figures.py} and
\path{workflow/scripts/make_gate_tables.py}. Its \texttt{README.md} lists every file,
its source and the version stamps of the runs.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \texttt{gate/}: the 2D gate of this paper (code version 1150e68, both corrections,
  Lichtenberg, four ranks): the summary, orders, comparison and serial-check files of every
  candidate, the list of cases with their states and stamps, and reduced time histories
  (about 300 rows per case) of every case.
  \item \texttt{prefix/}: the same gate before the corrections of
  Section~\ref{sec:fv-parallel} (code version 79b5a92), with the \SI{0.1}{s} translating
  runs.
  \item \texttt{laptop/}: the translating runs of Section~\ref{sec:res-translating}, the
  serial checks of Section~\ref{sec:fv-parallel} and the Eulerian runs of the mass-flux
  port, whose tables and figures the companion article~\cite{maric2026sl} carries, from
  the preserved raw output \path{runs/gcls-laptop-20260927}.
\end{itemize}
The gate definition is \path{config/gates/methodGate2D.yaml} and the candidates are
\path{config/candidates/*.yaml}. The gate runs with
\begin{center}\scriptsize
\path{make gate GATE=methodGate2D CANDIDATES=baseline+S1+HL0+HL1q+HL1z+HL2+FP0}.
\end{center}
