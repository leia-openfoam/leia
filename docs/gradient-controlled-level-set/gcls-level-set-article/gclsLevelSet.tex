\title{Gradient-controlled level-set transport on unstructured finite-volume
meshes: halo-limited velocity extension and admissible-band source terms}

\author[tuda]{Tomislav Mari\'{c}}

\address[tuda]{Mathematical Modeling and Analysis, TU Darmstadt, Germany}

\begin{abstract}
%% To be written after the 2D and 3D method gates report.
A signed-distance level set gives a well-scaled normal and a fixed physical
width of the interface transition, but the level-set transport equation does
not keep the signed-distance property under a deforming flow. This work keeps
the gradient magnitude $q=|\nabla\psilev|$ inside an admissible band near the
interface instead of enforcing $q=1$, with no redistancing, on unstructured
finite-volume meshes under MPI domain decomposition.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
%% The problem: D_t q = -a q; why redistancing is unwanted (interface
%% displacement, cost, contact lines); the admissible band as the target.
For the material equation $\partial_t\psilev+\bu\cdot\nabla\psilev=0$ the
gradient magnitude obeys, along a trajectory,
\begin{equation}
  \Dt q = -a\,q,\qquad a=\nhat^{\mathsf T}(\nabla\bu)\,\nhat,\qquad
  \nhat=\nabla\psilev/q ,
  \label{eq:qlaw}
\end{equation}
so the normal strain $a$ changes $q$ even for an exact solution
\cite{FrickeEtAl2022,BotheFrickeSoga2024}.

\section{Continuum model}
\label{sec:model}

\subsection{Source calculus}
For $\Dt\psilev=\psilev F$ the zero level set is invariant, and on it
\begin{equation}
  \Dt q = q\,(F-a).
  \label{eq:designlaw}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Halo-limited directional velocity extension}
Let $d$ be a signed nominal distance and $\mathbf e$ the unit direction from
the interface toward $\bx$; the level-set realisation is $d=\psilev/q$,
$\mathbf e=\nhat$. With a radius $R$ and $m\in\mathbb N$,
\begin{equation}
  S_R(d)=d\left[1+\left(\frac{d}{R}\right)^{2m}\right]^{-1/(2m)},\qquad
  Y_R=\bx-S_R(d)\,\mathbf e,\qquad
  w_R=\left(\frac{S_R(d)}{d}\right)^{\beta},
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  \bu_H=(1-w_R)\,\bu(\bx)+w_R\,\bu(Y_R).
\end{equation}
Every sample lies inside the radius $R$, and on the interface
$\nhat^{\mathsf T}(\nabla\bu_H)\nhat=0$.

\subsection{Source-law family}
The source coefficient is
\begin{equation}
  F = w(q)\,a + G(q,\sigma),\qquad
  a=\nhat\cdot\mathbf D\cdot\nhat,\qquad
  \sigma=\lvert\mathbf D\rvert_F,\qquad
  \mathbf D=\tfrac12\left(\nabla\bu+\nabla\bu^{\mathsf T}\right),
  \label{eq:Fdef}
\end{equation}
with $G(1,\sigma)=0$ for every law, so that a signed-distance level set is a
fixed point of \eqref{eq:designlaw}. With $e=q-1$ and $\zeta=(q^2-1)/2$,
Table~\ref{tab:laws} lists the laws. For the same $\mu$ the linear pair has
the slope $-\mu$ at $q=1$ and the cubic pair the slope zero, so a difference
between the q- and the z-form near $q=1$ comes from the discretisation, not
from the coefficients. The weight $w$ is $0$ (\texttt{none}), $1$
(\texttt{full}: the SDPLS source $R$ of \cite{FrickeEtAl2022}) or
$\omega(q)=1-\exp[-\beta((q-1)/\delta_s)^{2m}]$ (\texttt{omega}).

\section{Discretisation on unstructured finite-volume meshes}
\label{sec:fv}
\paragraph{Eulerian line} The level set is transported in material form
with the flux that actually moves it,
\begin{equation}
  \partial_t\psilev+\nabla_h\cdot(\Phi\,\psilev)-\psilev\,\nabla_h\cdot\Phi
  = \psilev F,
  \label{eq:materialFV}
\end{equation}
where $\Phi$ is the extension flux when an extension is selected. $F$ enters
through the linearisations of the SDPLS source \cite{FrickeEtAl2022}.

\paragraph{Semi-Lagrangian line} The characteristic step
$\psilev^A(\bx_c)=\psilev^n(\bx_d)$ traces along
$\mathrm{reconstruct}(\Phi^H)$ when the extension enters the trace
(\texttt{traceFlux extension}), and along $\mathrm{reconstruct}(\Phi)$
otherwise. The source step integrates $\Dt\psilev=\psilev F$ exactly for one
step with $F$ frozen,
\begin{equation}
  \psilev_c^{n+1}=\psilev_c^A\exp(\Delta t\,F_c),
  \label{eq:expUpdate}
\end{equation}
in the band $\lvert\psilev_c\rvert/q_c\le b\,h_c$, with $F=0$ outside it and
$\lvert\Delta t F\rvert$ clamped at $30$ (every clamp is counted). The update
changes no sign, so no cell changes phase. $q_c$ comes from a gradient
scheme that the case selects; $h_c$ is the smallest distance to the centre
of a face neighbour, coupled neighbours included.

\paragraph{Halo-limited flux} The extension enters as a correction of the
pressure-corrected flux,
\begin{equation}
  \Phi^H_f=\Phi_f+w_f\left[\bu_h(Y_f)-\bu_h(\bx_f)\right]\cdot\mathbf S_f,
  \qquad
  R_f=r\min(h_P,h_N),\quad 0<r\le1,
  \label{eq:hlFlux}
\end{equation}
so that $\Phi^H=\Phi$ where $w=0$ and on the physical patches. $\bu_h$ is the
mean of the owner's and the neighbour's local quadratic models (a weighted
least-squares value fit over the cell-point-cell stencil, one velocity
component at a time), each evaluated at the same two points, so that the
constant part of each model cancels. A linear model would reduce the
extension to a weighted SDPLS source at first order. On a coupled face each
side evaluates its own cell's model; the two corrections are summed in the
owner side's orientation and halved, so the two sides carry exactly opposite
values. The direction uses $d=\psilev/\sqrt{Q_2}$, $\mathbf e=\nabla\psilev/
\sqrt{Q_2}$ with $Q_2=q^2+\zeta(q^2)$ and $\zeta(z)=(1-4z)^3/4$ for $z<1/4$,
zero above: $d=\psilev/q$ and $\mathbf e=\nhat$ hold exactly for $q\ge0.5$.

\section{Implementation in leia}
\label{sec:impl}
The method is open to extension and closed to modification: a new law,
extension or strategy is one new class that registers itself in a
runtime-selection table, and one dictionary word selects it. Table
\ref{tab:families} lists the families. The solvers hold composition roots
only; each constructs the families it can select and calls their interface.
Before the new models, four roots were opened in one commit that changed no
result: the source family of the semi-Lagrangian line, the velocity
extension of the Eulerian two-phase solver and of the kinematic
semi-Lagrangian solver, and the trace flux of both semi-Lagrangian solvers.
The bit-identity gate compared every CSV at tolerance zero and every written
field byte for byte against the binaries of the previous commit: 53 cases on
all five solvers, including hexahedral and polyhedral transport ladders at
three resolutions, were identical.

\section{Test cases}
\label{sec:cases}
One 2D study and one 3D study test every method (the method gates). Each
gate has the same arms: an exact one-dimensional check, a reversed shear flow,
and a stationary, a translating and an oscillating droplet.
Figures~\ref{fig:cases-kinematic}--\ref{fig:cases-droplets3D} give the geometry,
the initial condition and the boundary conditions of every arm, and
Table~\ref{tab:cases} gives the parameters and the resolution ladders. In 2D the
number of cells doubles per rung; in 3D the ratio of the cell sizes is at least
1.3 per rung \cite{Celik2008}. The first rung resolves the droplet radius with at
least ten cells. The two droplet fluids are water ($\rho_1=\SI{998.2}{kg/m^3}$,
$\nu_1=\SI{1e-6}{m^2/s}$) and air ($\rho_2=\SI{1.19}{kg/m^3}$,
$\nu_2=\SI{1.53e-5}{m^2/s}$), with $\sigma=\SI{0.07274}{N/m}$ and no gravity.

\section{Verification}
\label{sec:verification}
\paragraph{Unit gates} Each model has a unit gate that exits nonzero on
a failure; the parallel gates run on four ranks with the interface or the
source band across a processor boundary.
\begin{itemize}
  \item The laws (109 checks, no mesh): $G(1)=0$ exactly, the sign of $G$
  opposite to $q-1$, $\partial G/\partial q$ against a central difference,
  the equal q/z slopes, the soft-wall response of the table in \cite{BotheDossierHalo2026}
  table within $5\times10^{-4}$, and fourth-order Runge--Kutta integration
  of $q'=qG$ against the logistic, the $z$-logistic and the implicit cubic
  solutions.
  \item The Eulerian source (189 checks): law \texttt{none} with weight
  \texttt{full} equals the SDPLS source $R$ bit for bit; \texttt{linearQ}
  with $\mu=1$ equals the bounded-gradient source with $\beta=1$ exactly;
  every law applies $F\psilev$ at the exact affine values to $10^{-10}$.
  \item The semi-Lagrangian source (49 checks on one and on four ranks):
  \eqref{eq:expUpdate} to $10^{-12}$ in the band, the cells outside it
  unchanged bit for bit, no sign change, the clamp, and current processor
  values after the step.
  \item The halo-limited extension (18 checks on one and on four ranks):
  $S_R(0)=0$, $S_R'(0)=1$, $\lvert S_R\rvert\le R$ up to $\lvert
  d\rvert=10^6R$, $1-w=(\beta/2m)(d/R)^{2m}$ to leading order; for an affine
  and for the divergence-free quadratic velocity of \cite{BotheDossierHalo2026}, the face
  correction equals \eqref{eq:hlFlux} with the exact velocity at the exact
  sample point to $2\times10^{-16}$; a uniform velocity leaves $\Phi$ and
  $\bu$ unchanged bit for bit; the correction is exactly antisymmetric on
  all 240 coupled faces.
\end{itemize}
